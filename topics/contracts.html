Application by Raquel Gray

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4143Employment & workplaceContracts
The Fair Work Commission determined an unfair termination remedy application by a regulated road transport contractor, Ms Gray, against BESB Couriers after she was replaced on the Toukley delivery run upon returning from leave. The Commission found there was no valid reason for termination and that the Road Transport Industry Termination Code processes were not followed, so the termination was unfair. It ordered that a new services contract be entered into between BESB Couriers and Ms Gray personally.
Key holding

A regulated road transport contractor’s termination is unfair where there is no valid reason for termination and the processes under the Road Transport Industry Termination Code are not followed.

In summary, there was no valid reason for the termination of the services contract, and the processes in the Road Transport Code were not followed, in part because of a breakdown in communication and in part because BESB Couriers is a small business with l imited access to resources or relevant legal or human resources expertise. I am satisfied that the termination of the services contract was unfair.

Mr Josateki Vunimasi & Mr Aminio Cava and Others v Woodward Foods Australia Pty Ltd & Mr Richard Tomasevic

23 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission (Full Bench)[2026] FWCFB 284Employment & workplaceContracts
This was a Full Bench appeal concerning seven PALM scheme workers whose s 365 general protections dismissal applications named Woodward Foods as respondent, although their actual employer/dismisser was Whales. The Deputy President found no valid s 365 application because they did not allege dismissal in contravention of Part 3-1, and the Full Bench refused permission to appeal. The appeal also sought to raise joint employment and host-employer oral promise arguments not argued at first instance.
Key holding

For a valid s 365 application, the person must have been dismissed and must allege that the dismissal contravened Part 3-1; otherwise the Commission’s s 368 powers are not enlivened.

It is abundantly clear that the Deputy President applied the principle outlined in Milford. He properly determined that the Appellants were not entitled to make applications under s 365 and nor were the functions of the Commission under s 368 enlivened because the second criterion was not met.

Kay and Commissioner of Taxation (Taxation and business)

13 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2310Tribunal reviewContracts
Matthew Kay objected to superannuation guarantee charge assessments for failing to make superannuation contributions for Daryl Marschke for 1 July 2019 to 30 June 2023. The Commissioner initially found Mr Marschke was not an employee but later reversed that position on delegation issues and assessed SGC. The Tribunal affirmed the objection decision.
Key holding

A contract is not wholly or principally for a person’s labour if it leaves the contractor free to do the work personally or employ others, even if the contractor in fact performs most of the work.

Even where a person has performed the bulk of the work under the contract, or it was the expectation of the parties that the person would do so, a contract that 'leaves the contractor free to do the work himself or employ other persons to carry it out' is not wholly or principally for the labour of the person.

Application by Raham Shahzad

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3914Employment & workplaceContracts
Mr Shahzad applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after Amazon deactivated him from the Amazon Flex digital labour platform. Amazon objected that he was not protected from unfair deactivation because he had not performed work on the platform on a regular basis for at least six months. The Fair Work Commission dismissed the application.
Key holding

For s 536LD(c), the Commission’s inquiry is limited to whether the person actually performed work and whether that work was performed on a regular basis for at least six months before deactivation.

The relevant inquiry under s 536LD(c) of the Act is limited to, first, whether a person has been performing work and secondly, whether such work was performed on a regular basis for a period of at least 6 months.
