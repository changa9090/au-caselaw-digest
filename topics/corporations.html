Bhardwaj and Commissioner of Taxation (Taxation)

23 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2482Tribunal reviewCorporations
The Tribunal reviewed a self-represented taxpayer’s objection decision concerning deductions for work-related car, travel, clothing, self-education, other expenses, gifts and tax affairs, plus interest on money borrowed to buy shares. It disallowed most deductions and held the interest was capital and not deductible, but allowed $67,500 as the third element of the cost base of the shares. The objection decision was varied accordingly.
Key holding

Interest under a bona fide loan is incurred in the income year it becomes due and payable and may be included in the third element of the cost base even if not paid.

The Tribunal expressed its view that, consistent with the established principle as expressed in Federal Commissioner of Taxation v James Flood Pty Ltd [1953] HCA 65; (1953) 88 CLR 492, the interest became due and payable in the 2020 - 21 income year and, accordingly, the balance of $67,500 was, if shown to be deductable or otherwise allowable in the cost base, deductible or allowable in its entirety in the 2020 - 21 income year.

Merhi and Tax Practitioners Board (Taxation and business)

22 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2456Tribunal reviewVisa integrityCharacter testCorporations
The Tribunal affirmed the Tax Practitioners Board’s decision to terminate Mr Merhi’s tax agent registration and impose a five-year period during which he cannot apply for registration. The decision turned on breaches of the Code of Professional Conduct and a finding that he was no longer a fit and proper person, with the Applicant’s failure to give evidence or contradict ASIC/liquidator material being significant.
Key holding

A registered tax agent bears the onus of establishing, on the balance of probabilities and having regard to the seriousness of the matters raised, that he or she remains a fit and proper person.

The onus of proof in establishing fitness and propriety rests with the tax practitioner. It is up to the tax practitioner to establish, and the Tribunal must be reasonably satisfied, that on the balance of probabilities and having regard to the seriousness of the matters raised that the tax practitioner or relevant individual is a fit and proper person.

Wardingarri Aboriginal Corporation and Registrar of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Corporations (Guidance and Appeals Panel)

8 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2281Tribunal reviewCorporations
Wardingarri Aboriginal Corporation, a registered native title body corporate under the CATSI Act, sought review and a stay of the Registrar’s 22 July 2026 decision concerning its membership eligibility rules and director terms. The Tribunal granted a conditional stay of the membership eligibility amendment but refused to stay the director term amendment. Decision: Wardingarri Aboriginal Corporation and Registrar of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Corporations (Guidance and Appeals Panel) [2026] ARTA 2281 (8 October 2026).
Key holding

Under s 32(2) of the Tribunal Act, the Tribunal may stay the operation or implementation of a reviewable decision if satisfied it is desirable to ensure the effectiveness of the review.

However, on application by a party to a proceeding for review of a reviewable decision, the Tribunal may make an order staying or otherwise affecting the operation or implementation of the decision if the Tribunal considers that it is desirable to do so for the purpose of ensuring the effectiveness of the review.
