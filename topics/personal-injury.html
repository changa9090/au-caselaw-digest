HYDE -v- SOUTH METROPOLITAN HEALTH SERVICE

22 Oct 2026District Court of Western Australia[2026] WADC 102Personal injuryNegligence
Before: MASSEY DCJ HEARD : 19 MAY, 19 & 25 JUNE, 24 AUGUST 2026 DELIVERED : 22 OCTOBER 2
Sarah Hyde applied under s 39 of the Limitation Act 2005 (WA) to extend time to bring a medical negligence claim against South Metropolitan Health Service over alleged over-tightening of a CAM boot in 2019 causing nerve injury. The District Court granted the extension, finding she became aware of the physical cause of her injury only on receipt of Dr Sekel’s expert report dated 19 December 2023, not from earlier hospital records or a lay JobSeeker comment.
Key holding

Under s 39 of the Limitation Act 2005 (WA), awareness of a physical cause and attribution requiring expert medical knowledge ordinarily arises from an expert opinion reasonably capable of acceptance, not mere suspicion or lay opinion.

As McLure P said in AME, awareness of the matters in s 39(4)(a) and s 39(4)(b) of the Act depends on inferences from the primary facts drawn by a medical expert.

Brighton Ceiling Pty Ltd v Pocrnja & Ors

14 Oct 2026New South Wales Court of Appeal[2005] NSWCA 175Personal injury
The NSW Court of Appeal considered whether a workers compensation indemnity claimant could include non-economic loss in notional motor accident damages under s 151Z of the Workers Compensation Act ([2005] NSWCA 175). Because excluding that component would not change the claimant’s recovery, the Court treated the issue as hypothetical and reinstated the arbitrator’s award unchanged. No exact damages amount is stated.
Key holding

A court need not determine whether non-economic loss may be included in notional motor accident damages under s 151Z where excluding that component would not affect the claimant’s recovery.

The Court held the question was hypothetical because even excluding the non-economic loss component would not affect the claimant's recovery, and reinstated the arbitrator's award without modification.

GUPTA v ROBERTSON

13 Oct 2026District Court of South Australia[2026] SADC 130Personal injuryNegligence
The applicant claimed damages for a right-knee injury or aggravation allegedly caused by a low-speed rear-end collision, with negligence admitted. The Court found the collision caused only a minor, temporary exacerbation of a pre-existing 2015 motorcycle injury and dismissed the claim, with the s 124AC set-off reducing compensable loss to nil.
Key holding

Where a plaintiff alleges that a collision aggravated or exacerbated a pre-existing condition, factual causation under s 34(1)(a) requires proof on the balance of probabilities that that particular aggravation or exacerbation would not have occurred but for the defendant’s negligence.

Where the harm alleged is an aggravation or exacerbation of a pre-existing condition, the relevant inquiry under section 34(1)(a) is whether Mr Gupta has proved, on the balance of probabilities, that the particular aggravation or exacerbation would not have occurred but for Ms Robertson's negligence.

Under s 5(1)(a)(iii) of the Motor Accident Insurance Act 1994 (Qld), a vehicle is running out of control when it moves while no person is in a position to steer it or apply the brakes, even if it is not being driven.

However, in my view, a vehicle is running out of control if it is moves when there is no one in a position either to steer the movement or inhibit the movement by applying the brakes. That was the position when this bus moved off the wooden blocks.
