Caller v State of NSW

13 Oct 2026New South Wales Court of Appeal[2026] NSWCA 212Negligence
Before: Leeming JA at [1];Stern JA at [104];Ball JA at [105] Decision: 1. Appeal dismiss
The appellant, a former NSW police officer, suffered PTSD from cumulative exposure to traumatic incidents during service. The primary judge found the State breached its duty by failing to implement its own record-keeping system to monitor cumulative trauma exposure, but held the breach did not cause the PTSD. The Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal, upholding the no-causation finding and rejecting challenges to other alleged breaches.
Key holding

In a negligence claim, the plaintiff must prove not only breach of duty but also that the breach caused the loss claimed.

In order to succeed on the negligence claim brought by the appellant, it was necessary for him to establish that there was a breach of duty owed to him which caused the loss (in this case, his developing PTSD).

Costantino v Hillier (No 2)

12 Oct 2026Supreme Court of New South Wales[2026] NSWSC 1260Negligence
Before: Muston J Decision: (1) The costs of the plaintiff’s notice of motion filed 5 Feb
This is a costs decision following the Court’s earlier determination of limitations issues in a professional negligence claim. The plaintiff failed to obtain an extension of the long-stop limitation period for claims concerning the first four surgeries, while the defendant’s separate question also affected claims concerning the fifth and sixth surgeries. The Court ordered the costs of the plaintiff’s notice of motion and the separate question to be costs in the cause, rather than acceding to the defendant’s proposed 75% costs order.
Key holding

Where parties achieve mixed success on overlapping interlocutory limitation applications, the Court may exercise its costs discretion by ordering costs in the cause rather than apportioning costs issue-by-issue.

In my view, the parties have each achieved some success and some failure in their competing applications which were heard before me on 18 and 19 August 2026. While there is some significant overlap between them, I nevertheless consider the most appropriate way to approach the costs of those applications is to order that they be costs in the cause.

Palmer v State of Queensland

12 Oct 2026Supreme Court of Queensland[2026] QSC 30Negligence
Before: Johnstone J
The plaintiff sued the State of Queensland for psychiatric injury allegedly caused by a rape on Palm Island in about 1959 or 1960, when she was a child. The State applied for summary judgment or strike-out, denying any duty of care and alleging no causal link. The Supreme Court entered judgment for the State and ordered the plaintiff to pay costs.
Key holding

If the material does not suggest a party could plead a legally recognised cause of action, leave to replead should be refused and judgment for the defendant may follow.

Where there is nothing in the material before the court to suggest that a party could plead a cause of action known to law, leave to replead would be refused. Indeed, a logical consequence may be to give judgment for the defendant.

Zvicer v Hunter New England Local Health District

8 Oct 2026Supreme Court of New South Wales[2026] NSWSC 1241Negligence
Before: Muston J Decision: The claims made by each of the plaintiffs are dismissed. Catc
Ms Zvicer sued Hunter New England Local Health District alleging that hospital doctors negligently failed to perform a fasciotomy on her left lower leg on 11 November 2020, causing a below-knee amputation. Her children separately claimed psychiatric injury. The Supreme Court dismissed all claims, rejecting liability.
Key holding

A defendant is not liable in negligence for harm that is the materialisation of an inherent risk, being a risk that could not be avoided by reasonable care and skill.

A person is not liable in negligence for harm suffered by another person as a result of the materialisation of an inherent risk.

Soar Development Group Pty Ltd v Fung

7 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1491Negligence
Soar sought leave to amend its originating application and statement of claim in a Federal Court commercial proceeding, including professional negligence and knowing assistance claims against EFG and Mr Huang. The first to fourth respondents did not oppose, but EFG and Mr Huang opposed certain amendments on delay and prejudice grounds, saying they materially enlarged the case and loss shortly before a combined hearing.
Key holding

The court may grant leave to amend closed pleadings under rr 8.21 and 16.53 in the exercise of a broad discretion informed by the overarching purpose, and may impose conditions including costs and timetable relief to address delay and prejudice.

An order granting leave may be made subject to conditions: r 1.33. The discretion to grant leave is broad, but must be exercised in a manner consistent with the overarching purpose stated in s 37M of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth) (FCA Act), namely to facilitate the just resolution of disputes according to law and as quickly, inexpensively and efficiently as possible.

Frazer v State of Queensland [2026] QSC 82 (22 April 2026)

22 Apr 2026Supreme Court of Queensland[2026] QSC 82Personal injuryNegligence
Ms Frazer, an experienced teacher, sued the State of Queensland after suffering PTSD following an incident where an aggrieved parent attended the school looking to confront her. The State admitted it owed a non-delegable duty of care to Ms Frazer, but disputed breach. The court found no breach and entered judgment for the defendant.
Key holding

A policy authorising physical force to remove an abusive or threatening person does not require force in every circumstance; reasonableness depends on the unfolding situation.

It seems to me that the authorisation in Chief Executive Guideline No 1 of 2012 to take all reasonable steps including physical force to eject an abusive, threatening or disorderly person does not, could not and should not demand in all circumstances that physical force be used to eject an abusive, threatening or disorderly person.
