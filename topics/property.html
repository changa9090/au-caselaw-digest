Yerburgh and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

22 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2291Tribunal reviewVisa nominationProperty
In Yerburgh and Secretary, Department of Social Services [2026] ARTA 2291, the Tribunal reviewed a decision reducing Mrs Yerburgh’s age pension by assessing notional income from a farm treated as an unrealisable asset. The Tribunal set aside the decision and remitted the rate for reassessment from 11 October 2024 on the basis that the relevant income amount was her actual annual ordinary income from the farm, not a commercial lease value.
Key holding

Where an unrealisable farm is already being worked commercially to full capacity, the amount reasonably expected from a purely commercial application under s 1130(5)(b) is the actual income from current operations, and the s 1130(5) notional rate is the lesser of that amount and 2.5% of the farm's value.

However, having heard from Mrs Yerburgh, the Tribunal is satisfied that the farm is currently being operated commercially to its full capacity. As such, the Tribunal finds that what Mrs Yerburgh could reasonably be expected to obtain from a purely commercial application of the farm is the income that is actually being generated from its current operations, not some alternative higher commercial lease figure as adopted by Centrelink.

Fearon and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security)

10 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2294Tribunal reviewProperty
Ms Fearon receives age pension. Centrelink treated $70,500 she effectively gifted to her daughter in December 2022 as a deprived asset. The Tribunal affirmed that decision, finding the gifting rules required the amount to be included in her assets for five years.
Key holding

A disposal of an asset, including money, for no or inadequate consideration must be included as an asset for five years from the date of disposition.

The legislation is clear, where an asset (which includes money) has been disposed of for no or inadequate consideration, it must be included as an asset for five years from the date of disposition.

Ludlam and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

8 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2445Tribunal reviewVisa nominationProperty
The Tribunal reviewed Centrelink’s rejection of Ms Ludlam’s jobseeker payment claim because her combined assets exceeded the applicable limit. Centrelink treated the Cairns house as her principal home and as an asset, applying the lower homeowner assets limit. The Tribunal found Ms Ludlam was not a homeowner and remitted the claim for reassessment under the higher non-homeowner limit.
Key holding

Where a person leaves their owned home and resides in rented accommodation without an s 11A circumstance preserving it as the principal home, the person is not a homeowner under s 11(4) and the non-homeowner assets limit applies, although the former home may still be counted as an asset.

The Cairns property ceased to be Ms Ludlam's principal home when she left Cairns and took up residence in rented accommodation in Canberra. She therefore was not a homeowner as defined in subsection 11(4) of the Act and the higher asset limit applies.
