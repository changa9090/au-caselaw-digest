XMLQ and Minister for Foreign Affairs

19 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2467Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewTorts
XMLQ, a Pakistani national on a Bridging E visa, applied for a Convention Travel Document but was issued a Certificate of Identity instead. On review, the Administrative Review Tribunal held it had no power to determine whether he was a refugee under the Refugee Convention and affirmed the CTD refusal. The decision turns on s 6(1)(b) of the Australian Passports Determination 2015 (Cth).
Key holding

Section 6(1)(b) of the Determination requires an existing recognition as a refugee under the Refugee Convention and does not confer on the Tribunal power to undertake a refugee status determination.

The phrase "is recognised" naturally directs attention to a status that already exists. It describes a condition that must be satisfied before a Convention Travel Document may be issued.

Thiering v Daly

16 Sep 2026Supreme Court of New South Wales[2011] NSWSC 1345Torts
The NSW Supreme Court determined separate questions about whether a catastrophically injured motorcyclist who was a lifetime participant in the Lifetime Care and Support Scheme could still recover Griffiths v Kerkemeyer damages for gratuitous attendant care. The Court held that past gratuitous attendant care damages remained recoverable from the CTP tortfeasor unless the LCS Authority had paid or accepted legal liability, but future care was to be provided for under the LCS Scheme. No final damages award was made.
