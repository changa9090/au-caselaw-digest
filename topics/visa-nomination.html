Lye v Comcare

15 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1517Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationMigration
This was a Federal Court appeal from an Administrative Review Tribunal decision concerning weekly compensation under the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988 (Cth). The Tribunal had equated Mr Lye’s capacity to work full-time usual hours in administrative duties with an ability to earn his normal weekly earnings, reducing compensation to zero. The Court found legal error and, by consent, set aside the decision and remitted the matter for redetermination.
Key holding

Under s 19(2) of the SRC Act, the Tribunal must separately assess the amount per week the employee is able to earn in suitable employment and use that to determine the reduction to NWE; merely equating capacity for normal hours with NWE is legal error.

In its reasons at [40], the Tribunal failed to address the amount per week (if any) that the applicant was able to earn in suitable employment during the relevant periods (i.e., the fourth question). As a result of the above failure, the Tribunal failed properly to address the amount (if any) by which the applicant's NWE should be reduced in order to determine the amount of compensation that he is entitled to receive (i.e., the sixth question).

Mohamedali v Assistant Minister for Citizenship, Customs and Multicultural Affairs

15 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1531Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationCharacter testMigration
The Federal Court allowed a consent judicial review application concerning a decision by the Assistant Minister under s 501BA of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to cancel the applicant’s visa. The Assistant Minister conceded jurisdictional error because he failed to consider an updated psychologist’s report that was constructively before him and relevant to the applicant’s risk of reoffending. The Court made orders quashing the cancellation decision.
Key holding

A s 501BA decision is affected by jurisdictional error if the Minister fails to properly consider material constructively before him that is relevant to the risk the applicant poses to the Australian community and the national interest.

Accordingly, the Assistant Minister accepts that his decision was affected by jurisdictional error as a result of his failure to properly consider the risk the applicant posed to the Australian community.

Smith v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

15 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1520Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
Mr Smith, a New Zealand citizen, had his Class TY Subclass 444 visa cancelled under s 501(3A) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) after criminal convictions and imprisonment. A delegate refused to revoke the cancellation, and the Administrative Review Tribunal affirmed that refusal. On judicial review, the Federal Court found the Tribunal misconstrued Direction 110’s definition of “family violence” and set aside its decision.
Key holding

Conduct falling within an example of family violence in Direction 110 cl 4 is not automatically family violence; it must also have the effect of coercing or controlling a family member or causing that person to be fearful.

For example, behaviour which involves "intentionally damaging or destroying property" will not necessarily constitute family violence. It will only do so if that behaviour had the effect of coercing or controlling a member of the perpetrator's family, or causing the family member or members to be fearful.

South Australian Commission for Catholic Schools Inc and Secretary, Department of Education (Guidance and Appeals Panel)

13 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2304Tribunal reviewVisa nomination
The Tribunal reviewed 2024 Commonwealth recurrent funding determinations for 84 non-government Catholic schools in South Australia. It unanimously held that mid-year reception students who began in term 3 2024 were “primary students” because reception was the “foundation” year of schooling immediately before year 1. A majority then held those students did not have a pattern of regular attendance at school or school generally during 2024, so they failed reg 9B(1)(c) and were ineligible unless the Minister included them under reg 9B(3).
Key holding

Reception undertaken by mid-year reception students is the “foundation” year of schooling immediately before year 1, so those students are primary students in that calendar year.

Giving the phrases to which I have already referred their natural meaning, the logical conclusion is that the 2024 mid-year reception students were in the year of schooling in a course of primary education that was immediately before year 1. It follows that, in 2024, those students were in the foundation year of schooling, were receiving primary education and were therefore primary students at their school.

In Yerburgh and Secretary, Department of Social Services [2026] ARTA 2291, the Tribunal reviewed a decision reducing Mrs Yerburgh’s age pension by assessing notional income from a farm treated as an unrealisable asset. The Tribunal set aside the decision and remitted the rate for reassessment from 11 October 2024 on the basis that the relevant income amount was her actual annual ordinary income from the farm, not a commercial lease value.
Key holding

Where an unrealisable farm is already being worked commercially to full capacity, the amount reasonably expected from a purely commercial application under s 1130(5)(b) is the actual income from current operations, and the s 1130(5) notional rate is the lesser of that amount and 2.5% of the farm's value.
