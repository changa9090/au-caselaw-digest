13 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4414Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Robert McLaughlin
This is a protection visa review concerning an Indonesian citizen of Chinese ethnicity who claimed to fear persecution in Indonesia due to ethnicity, religion, cultural practices, extortion and physical harm. The Tribunal found the applicant’s claims lacked credibility and did not accept that he faced a well-founded fear of persecution or a real risk of significant harm. Key holding

A claimed fear of persecution must not be speculative, and the applicant must provide sufficiently detailed material to enable the Tribunal to establish the relevant facts.

A fear of persecution is not 'well-founded' if it is merely assumed or if it is mere speculation. Although the concept of onus of proof is not appropriate to administrative inquiries and decision-making, the applicant must provide material, in as much detail as is necessary, to enable the Tribunal to establish the relevant facts as claimed.

Aqil (Migration)

13 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3569Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration
Before: Penelope Hunter
Mr Aqil, a Pakistani citizen, had his Subclass 500 (Student) visa cancelled under s 116(1)(b) because he was not enrolled in a full-time registered course between 20 July 2022 and 11 July 2023. The Tribunal affirmed the cancellation, finding the breach of condition 8202(2)(a) went to the core purpose of the student visa and that the discretionary factors did not outweigh cancellation.
Key holding

A student visa may be cancelled under s 116(1)(b) where the holder has breached condition 8202, including the requirement in cl 8202(2)(a) to be enrolled in a full-time registered course, subject to the Tribunal’s discretion.

The issue in the present case is whether the applicant, as the holder of a student visa, has breached condition 8202 of Schedule 8 to the Migration Regulations 1994 (Cth) (the Regulations). If the applicant has breached that condition, under s 116(1) of the Act, the visa may be cancelled.

10 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3918Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Margie Bourke
The Tribunal affirmed refusal of an Aged Parent (Residence) (Class BP) (Subclass 804) visa because the applicant did not satisfy the balance of family test at the time of application. Key holding

A child who holds only a temporary visa at the time of application is not an eligible child for the balance of family test, which is assessed at the time of application.

The daughter Candida Shetty was the holder of a temporary visa at the time of application, and therefore did not meet the definition of eligible child at the time of application for the purpose of the balance of family test. Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
