Mr Kauri Taumaunu v United Personnel Traffic Pty Ltd

24 Dec 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 3940Employment & workplace
Mr Taumaunu applied for an unfair dismissal remedy after being dismissed by United Personnel Traffic Pty Ltd on redundancy grounds. The Full Bench found the dismissal was not a genuine redundancy because United failed to consult as required by its collective agreement, and remitted the matter for determination of merits. Commissioner Durham found the dismissal unfair under s385 and ordered compensation of $4,830.77 gross (one week's remuneration) plus superannuation, reflecting the consultation period that should have occurred.

Mr Omid Shabriz Zamani v QIC LIMITED

24 Dec 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 3938Employment & workplace
Mr Zamani was employed by QIC on a fixed-term contract as a Data Engineer, extended by variation to end on 29 August 2025. After he declined a permanent offer and sought to renegotiate its terms, QIC advertised the role externally, and his employment ended with the expiry of his fixed-term contract. The Commission upheld QIC's jurisdictional objection, finding Mr Zamani was not 'dismissed' under s.386(2)(a) because his employment ended by effluxion of time, and dismissed the application.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
