Mrs Narelle Wild v Hireup Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1584 (1 May 2026)

1 May 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1584Employment & workplace
This is a Fair Work Commission general protections dismissal application under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Mrs Wild lodged her application 4 days after the 21-day limit and sought an extension under s.366(2). The Commission found no exceptional circumstances and dismissed the application.
Key holding

The Commission may extend time for a s.365 general protections dismissal application only if satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist under s.366(2).

Under s.366 of the FW Act, the Commission has the discretion grant an extension of time for the making of a general protections application involving dismissal. The Commission's discretion is only enlivened if it is satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances.

Minister for Immigration, Citizenship, Migrant Services and Multicultural Affairs v KFTJ [2026] FCAFC 52 (30 April 2026)

30 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia (Full Court)[2026] FCAFC 52Migration: court challenge
The Minister personally cancelled KFTJ’s protection visa under s 501BA of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth), after the former AAT had revoked the mandatory cancellation under s 501CA. The primary judge set aside the Minister’s decision as legally unreasonable, and the Full Court dismissed the Minister’s appeal. The Full Court upheld findings that the Minister’s national-interest satisfaction had no rational foundation because he did not explain how cancellation would protect the community or meet community expectations.
Key holding

The Minister’s satisfaction under s 501BA(2)(b) that cancellation is in the national interest must have a rational foundation; reasons containing a logical gap as to how cancellation protects the community or meets community expectations are legally unreasonable.

The primary judge did not err in holding that each of the two main bases for the Minister's satisfaction that cancelling the respondent's visa was in the national interest suffered from what his Honour termed a "logical gap". In each of these ways, the Minister's state of satisfaction rested on reasoning that did not have a rational foundation.

Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v XMBQ (Costs) [2026] FCAFC 55 (30 April 2026)

30 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia (Full Court)[2026] FCAFC 55Migration: court challenge
This costs judgment follows the Minister’s successful appeal in Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v XMBQ [2026] FCAFC 19, in which the Full Court held that s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) is not subject to an implied reasonable-time limit. The primary judge had found otherwise in XMBQ v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs [2025] FCA 553. The Full Court ordered the respondent to pay the Minister’s appeal costs and 50% of the Minister’s first-instance costs.
Key holding

Costs ordinarily follow the event, but the Court may split costs where a party has unsuccessfully pursued a distinct fact-intensive issue.

Although the Court is generally unwilling to split costs orders by reference to particular issues, we consider that in this case the respondent should not have to bear the cost of the Minister having unsuccessfully pursued a fact-intensive issue.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
