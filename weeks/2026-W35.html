Asad v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

27 Aug 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1477Migration: court challengeMigration
Mr Asad sought an extension of time to judicially review the Assistant Minister’s decision under s 501BA of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to set aside the Tribunal’s decision and cancel his resident return visa. As a member of the NZYQ cohort, Mr Asad would remain in the community even if the visa were cancelled. The Federal Court granted an extension only for part of ground 1 but dismissed the application, finding the cancellation decision was not illogical or irrational.
Key holding

In assessing the national interest under s 501BA, it is not illogical or irrational to cancel a visa on protection-of-the-community grounds where the decision-maker distinguishes the permanent risk of continued residence on a substantive visa from the non-permanent, even if indefinite, risk on a temporary or bridging visa.

The former necessarily created a permanent risk, while the latter created a risk that had at least the potential or possibility to be non-permanent, even if of indefinite duration at the time the decision was made.

NAT (Migration)

26 Aug 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2391Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Subclass 500 Student visa because the delegate was not satisfied Mr Patwari was a genuine applicant under cl 500.212. It set aside the refusal and remitted the application, finding his strong recent master’s results, family ties in India and the course’s value outweighed earlier concerns about course changes and slow completion.
Key holding

Under cl 500.212(a), the genuine temporary entrant assessment requires a holistic weighing of Direction No 108 factors rather than checklist compliance, allowing later sustained academic progress and home-country ties to outweigh earlier study concerns.

The Direction indicates that the factors specified should not be used as a checklist but rather, are intended only to guide decision makers when considering the applicant's circumstances as a whole, in reaching a finding about whether the applicant satisfies the genuine temporary entrant criterion.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
