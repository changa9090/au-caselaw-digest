Hares (Migration)

17 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2454Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a delegate’s refusal to grant Subclass 309 (Spouse (Provisional)) visas to Mr Mohamad Bali and family, sponsored by Ms Bassima Hares. The Tribunal had to decide whether the couple was in a genuine and continuing married relationship under s 5F of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) at application and at decision. It affirmed the refusal because, despite accepting a valid marriage, it was not satisfied on the evidence of mutual commitment, a joint household or a genuine continuing relationship.
Key holding

Abundant documentary evidence alone does not establish a genuine spousal relationship; the Tribunal must be positively satisfied of the s 5F(2) elements on all the circumstances.

Documentary evidence in itself, no matter how abundant, does not establish in the Tribunal's view the existence of a genuine spousal relationship.

Gunarathne (Migration)

17 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2449Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a refusal to grant a New Zealand Citizen Family Relationship (Temporary) (Class UP) Subclass 461 visa. The applicant applied while holding only a Bridging A visa and failed to satisfy the Schedule 3 criteria required because he did not hold a substantive visa. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Failure to satisfy either of the cumulative requirements in Schedule 3 criterion 3004(d) or 3004(f) prevents an applicant from meeting criterion 3004 as a whole and therefore cl 461.213(b)(ii).

As I am not satisfied the applicant meets 3004(d) or (f), it follows that the applicant does not meet 3004 as a whole. Accordingly, he does not satisfy cl 461.213(b)(ii) of Schedule 2 to the Regulations and cannot meet the requirements for the grant of the visa.

2306518 (Migration)

17 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2406Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
In 2306518 (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2406, the Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Partner (Temporary) (Class UK) visa (Subclass 820) after the applicant’s relationship with his sponsor ceased. The applicant claimed to have experienced family violence, but the Tribunal was not satisfied and obtained an Independent Expert opinion that he had not. Because the opinion was validly made, the Tribunal treated it as correct and affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Where a non-judicially determined family violence claim is referred to an Independent Expert and the opinion is validly made, the Tribunal must accept that opinion as correct and cannot prefer other reports.

I am required to take the opinion of the Independent Expert as correct, unless that opinion is not validly made. The Tribunal cannot decide to give weight to other reports in the circumstances where the Tribunal has referred the matter to an Independent Expert.

Triolo (Migration)

17 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2358Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a delegate’s refusal to grant a 95-year-old Italian citizen a Visitor (Class FA) visa, Subclass 600. The Tribunal had to decide whether the grant would authorise a stay of more than 12 consecutive months and, if so, whether exceptional circumstances existed under cl 600.215. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding no exceptional circumstances and noting other visitor-visa criteria were also in issue.
Key holding

An intention or desire to remain permanently in Australia, even if reasonable and based on need for care and medical treatment, cannot be an exceptional circumstance for a further temporary visitor visa under cl 600.215.

I also consider that, where a person clearly desires and intends to stay permanently in Australia, (however reasonable and even where it is based on an applicant's need for care and medical treatment as in this case) that desire cannot reasonably be considered to be an exceptional circumstance for the grant of a further temporary visa.

Majak and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

Majak sought review of a delegate’s decision not to revoke the mandatory cancellation of his Class XB Subclass 202 visa under s 501(3A) after serious assault convictions. Key holding

Where the Tribunal is satisfied on the evidence that another reason exists, it should set aside the non-revocation decision and substitute revocation under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii).

Accordingly, it is appropriate for me to set aside the decision under review and substitute decision that the cancellation decision be revoked.

Lee (Migration)

16 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2375Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Mr Lee applied for a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) Subclass 500 visa. The delegate refused the visa because he did not meet the English language requirement. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal because his PTE score of 37 required current ELICOS enrolment, and his prior courses did not establish the timing exemption.
Key holding

Where a Subclass 500 applicant relies on a PTE score between 36 and 41, the English language requirement is not satisfied by past ELICOS study alone; the applicant must be enrolled in the required ELICOS as at the date of assessment.

When an applicant has not achieved a minimum PTE test score of 50 and relies on an ELICOS to satisfy the English language requirements, the applicant must be enrolled in the ELICOS as at the date of assessment.

16 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2321Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewBankruptcy & insolvency
The Applicant, FPXJ, sought an extension of time to bring second review applications in the Administrative Review Tribunal against two AAT first review decisions dated 19 August 2019 and 29 January 2020 concerning disability support pension debts. The Tribunal rejected his procedural and bias arguments and refused the extension. It found the delay exceeded six years, was not fully explained, and that reopening settled matters would prejudice the Respondent and wider public, with no practical utility in the substantive review.
Key holding

Under s 19 of the ART Act, the Tribunal may refuse an extension of time where significant delay, prejudice and lack of practical utility mean it is not reasonable in all the circumstances to extend time.

Given this, and given the prejudice to the Respondent and to the wider public if an extension of time is granted, the Tribunal has decided that it is not reasonable in all of the circumstances to extend time for the Applicant to make second review applications. Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
AustLII
The Australasian Legal Information Institute: a free public database that publishes the full text of Australian court and tribunal decisions. Every case on this page links to its full text there.
