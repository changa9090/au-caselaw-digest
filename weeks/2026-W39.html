Davis and Comcare (Compensation)

25 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2403Tribunal reviewEmployment & workplace
In Davis and Comcare [2026] ARTA 2403, the Tribunal reviewed Comcare’s denial of liability for an AFP officer’s psychological injury. It found compensable PTSD caused by operational trauma between 2017 and 2021, but excluded a later aggravation of that PTSD caused by disciplinary action because it resulted from reasonable administrative action taken reasonably.
Key holding

An aggravation of a compensable mental injury is not compensable where it results from reasonable administrative action taken in a reasonable manner in respect of the employee’s employment.

Comcare is not liable to pay compensation pursuant to section 14 of the SRC Act in respect of the injury identified as aggravation of post-traumatic stress disorder being the aggravation of the existing injury also subject of the claim dated 23 May 2023 but which was the result of reasonable administrative action taken in a reasonable manner in respect of his employment with the AFP.

Manpreet Kaur (Migration)

25 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2386Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewVisa integrityMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa for Ms Kaur, an Indian citizen who has lived in Australia as a student since 2018. Although the Tribunal accepted she was enrolled in a registered course, it was not satisfied she genuinely intended to stay temporarily because her latest lower-level course appeared designed to extend her residence rather than advance her studies. Key holding

Under cl 500.212, all of paras (a), (b) and (c) must be satisfied, so failure to establish cl 500.212(a) alone is sufficient to fail the genuine applicant criterion.

Macrolend Pty Ltd v Deputy Commissioner of Taxation

25 Sep 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1501Migration: court challengeMigration
Macrolend Pty Ltd and Paladin Hydrogen Pty Ltd sought recusal of Derrington J from their applications to set aside Deputy Commissioner of Taxation statutory demands, alleging apprehended bias because his spouse, Sarah C Derrington J, had previously made overlapping findings in related ASIC proceedings. Derrington J dismissed the recusal application, finding no reasonable apprehension of bias. The plaintiffs were ordered to pay the defendant’s costs and the substantive matter was listed for hearing on 2 October 2026.
Key holding

A judge is not required to recuse for apprehended bias merely because their spouse has previously made overlapping findings of fact or credit in related proceedings; there must be a rational logical connection between that relationship or those findings and a possible departure from impartial merits-based decision-making.

Nor would my relationship with Sarah C Derrington J provide such a basis. There is no rational reason to apprehend that, because of that relationship, I might adopt her Honour's findings, depart from them to demonstrate my independence, or resolve the issues otherwise than on their legal and factual merits.

Kulwant KAUR (Migration)

23 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2351Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a refusal of a Visitor (Class FA) Subclass 600 visa on genuine temporary entrant grounds under cl 600.211. The applicant was a 59-year-old widow from India whose children all live in Australia, and the family sought to keep her onshore so she could later apply for a permanent visa. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal because it was not satisfied she genuinely intended only a temporary stay.
Key holding

The Tribunal’s task under cl 600.211 is to assess whether the applicant genuinely intends a temporary stay for the visa’s purpose, not to facilitate permanent residence.

The Tribunal does not see it as its role to facilitate the applicant's intention to remain in Australia permanently regardless of the requirements of the Visitor visa. The Tribunal's role is to simply assess whether the applicant genuinely intends to stay in Australia temporarily for the purpose for which the visa is granted.

Burgess & Burgess (No 2)

23 Sep 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (Division 1 First Instance)[2026] FedCFamC1F 649Migration: court challengeMigration
This was a part-heard family law property proceeding under s 79 of the Family Law Act 1975 (Cth) in which the wife sought to rely on a covert audio recording of the husband. After the High Court’s decision in Potter (A Pseudonym) v The King [2026] HCA 25, Parker J revisited an earlier interlocutory ruling admitting the recording, excluded it, recused herself, and aborted the trial. The husband was granted a costs certificate.
Key holding

In South Australia, s 9(1) of the Surveillance Devices Act 2016 (SA) (and by parity s 12(1)) is a law relating to evidence picked up by s 79(1) of the Judiciary Act and applies in federal family law proceedings, so a recording obtained for a lawful-interest purpose cannot be used unless a s 9(1) exception applies, and s 138 of the Evidence Act does not displace that prohibition.

In my view, subsection 9(1) is therefore a "law relating to evidence," the effect of which is that it is "picked up" by section 79 of the Judiciary Act[17] such that the same limitation applies in the context of a court exercising federal jurisdiction in South Australia, as is the case in the present proceeding. The same reasoning applies with respect to subsection 12(1) of the Surveillance Devices Act.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).

The Australasian Legal Information Institute: a free public database that publishes the full text of Australian court and tribunal decisions. Every case on this page links to its full text there.
