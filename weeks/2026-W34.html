FHFD and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

21 Aug 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2433Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal to revoke the mandatory cancellation of FHFD’s Class TY Subclass 444 Special Category (Temporary) visa after he failed the character test due to corruption and family violence offending. Despite the seriousness of the offending, the Tribunal found a low risk of reoffending and strong ties to Australia, set aside the delegate’s decision and revoked the cancellation.
Key holding

Where mandatory cancellation is Reviewable after a person fails the character test, the Tribunal must decide whether another reason exists to revoke under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii), applying the applicable ministerial direction.

Section 501CA(4)(b)(ii) of the Migration Act requires the Tribunal to consider if there is another reason why the discretion should be exercised to revoke the original decision. In exercising this discretion, the Tribunal must have regard to the Direction (s 499(2A)).

2635010 (Migration)

19 Aug 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2284Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
The Tribunal refused a Bridging E (Class WE) Subclass 050 visa to an Iranian applicant in immigration detention after criminal convictions and prior protection visa refusal. The applicant relied on a pending s 48B ministerial intervention request and asserted he had acceptable arrangements to depart Australia if those avenues failed. The Tribunal affirmed the delegate’s refusal.
Key holding

A Bridging E visa applicant does not satisfy cl 050.212(5B) where they have previously made any s 48B ministerial intervention request, even if the earlier request was finalised as not considered and a later request is treated as non-repeat under the Ministerial Instructions; the Tribunal cannot read the Ministerial Instructions exception into the regulation.

It finds that to conclude for the purposes of cl 050.212(5B) that the applicant's 23 December 2025 request is not a repeat request on the basis of the Ministerial Instructions is to read into cl 050.212(5B) a set of circumstances and provisions that the clause does not contemplate or provide for.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
AustLII
The Australasian Legal Information Institute: a free public database that publishes the full text of Australian court and tribunal decisions. Every case on this page links to its full text there.
