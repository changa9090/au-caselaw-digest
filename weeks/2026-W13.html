Bohdi Vile v WorkPac Mining Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1054 (27 March 2026)

27 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1054Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission arbitrated a dispute under s.306P of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) about whether Mr Vile, a labour-hire carpenter at HVO’s Hunter Valley coal mine, was covered by a regulated labour hire arrangement order. The Commission found he was covered because he would fall within the broad Mine and Process Technician classification in the HVO Agreement. It declined to make the resulting protected rate order retrospective because the parties had not notified agreement to arbitration under s.306Q.
Key holding

Under s.306Q of the FW Act, where the parties have not notified the Commission that they agree to arbitration, an arbitrated protected rate of pay order cannot apply retrospectively and can only take effect from the date it is made or a future commencement date of the regulated labour hire arrangement order.

In contrast, if the parties have not notified the Commission that they agree to the arbitration, an arbitrated protected rate of pay order can only apply from the date the order is made, or the date the relevant regulated labour hire arrangement order will come into force if that is a future date.

A costs assessor appointed under the UCPR is not automatically functus officio simply upon signing a costs certificate; that status arises only at the end of the assessment, when all items have been assessed and the assessor’s function is complete.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
