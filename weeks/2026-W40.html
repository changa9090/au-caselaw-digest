Kintanar (Migration)

2 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2384Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
In Kintanar (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2384, the Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Partner (Temporary) (Class UK)/Subclass 820 visa because the applicant’s non-migrating child, Leonel, failed PIC 4007 due to severe cognitive impairment/autism and an estimated care cost of $1,577,840. The Tribunal found the MOC opinion valid but waived the health criterion under PIC 4007(2)(b), weighing the child’s non-migrating status, the applicant’s and sponsor’s regional NSW contributions, and their capacity to mitigate future costs.
Key holding

Under PIC 4007(2)(b), the Tribunal may waive the health criterion where the benefits and contributions of the applicant and sponsor, the detriment of refusal, and the applicant’s capacity to mitigate future costs outweigh a cost that cannot be realised from the grant of the visa alone.

The Tribunal concludes that the benefits that the visa applicant and her partner bring to the Australian community, the detriment to the visa sponsor and his family if the visa was refused and the applicant and sponsor's capacity to mitigate the potential costs to the Australia community should Leonel apply for a visa in the future to join his mother, outweigh a cost to the Australian community which cannot be realised from the grant of this visa alone.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
