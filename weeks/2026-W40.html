Human Outsource Pty Ltd Trading AS Miles Witt Partnership

2 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWCA 2730Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission approved the Adventist Aged Care Nurses Enterprise Agreement 2026 on application by Human Outsource Pty Ltd T/A Miles Witt Partnership, with Seventh-day Adventist Aged Care (South Queensland) Limited as employer. The Commission accepted undertakings and noted that, because the agreement lacked a compliant delegates’ rights term under s.205A, the modern award term applies instead. The agreement operates from 9 October 2026 and has a nominal expiry date of 30 June 2030.
Key holding

If an enterprise agreement does not contain a delegates’ rights term that is no less favourable than the modern award, the modern award term applies and the agreement’s delegates’ rights term has no effect.

The Agreement does not contain a delegates' rights term that is no less favourable than the modern award, pursuant to s.205A, and on that basis the term in the relevant modern award will apply and the delegates' rights term in the Agreement at clause 44 will have no effect.

Ravenshoe Kindergarten & Playgroup Inc.

2 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWCA 2727Employment & workplace
Ravenshoe Kindergarten & Playgroup Inc applied under s 216CA of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for FWC approval of a variation to the Queensland Kindergarten Early Childhood Education Multi-Employer Enterprise Agreement 2026 to add it and its employees as covered. The variation had majority employee support and was not opposed by the IEUA. Commissioner Simpson approved the variation.
Key holding

The FWC is required to approve a variation of a cooperative workplace agreement to add an employer and employees if satisfied that each s 216CB(1) requirement has been met and that the mandatory refusal provisions and s 216CD do not apply.

As I am satisfied that each of the requirements of s.216CB(1) have been met in relation to the application, having regard to the Form F23IA 'Employer's Declaration' filed in support of application and other material before me, and that ss.216CB(2) and 216CD do not apply with respect to each application, I am required to approve the variation.

Upson and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

1 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2275Migration — court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
In Upson and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2275, a 31-year-old New Zealand citizen who has lived in Australia since infancy had his special category visa mandatorily cancelled under s 501(3A) after being sentenced to 7 years 6 months for serious drug offences. A delegate refused to revoke the cancellation, but the Administrative Review Tribunal set that decision aside and substituted revocation after finding “another reason” under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii).
Key holding

Under Direction no. 110, whether a non-citizen poses an “unacceptable risk” is a distinct evaluative conclusion reached by balancing the likelihood and potential harm of future offending, not merely the likelihood of re-offending.

The concept of 'risk', and whether it is 'unacceptable', under cl 8.1.2(1) of the Direction is not the same thing as the 'likelihood of the non-citizen engaging in further criminal or other serious conduct'.[25]

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits — a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made — the court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
