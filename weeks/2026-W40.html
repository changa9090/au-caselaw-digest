Kintanar (Migration)

2 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2384Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
In Kintanar (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2384, the Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Partner (Temporary) (Class UK)/Subclass 820 visa because the applicant’s non-migrating child, Leonel, failed PIC 4007 due to severe cognitive impairment/autism and an estimated care cost of $1,577,840. The Tribunal found the MOC opinion valid but waived the health criterion under PIC 4007(2)(b), weighing the child’s non-migrating status, the applicant’s and sponsor’s regional NSW contributions, and their capacity to mitigate future costs.
Key holding

Under PIC 4007(2)(b), the Tribunal may waive the health criterion where the benefits and contributions of the applicant and sponsor, the detriment of refusal, and the applicant’s capacity to mitigate future costs outweigh a cost that cannot be realised from the grant of the visa alone.

The Tribunal concludes that the benefits that the visa applicant and her partner bring to the Australian community, the detriment to the visa sponsor and his family if the visa was refused and the applicant and sponsor's capacity to mitigate the potential costs to the Australia community should Leonel apply for a visa in the future to join his mother, outweigh a cost to the Australian community which cannot be realised from the grant of this visa alone.

Ravenshoe Kindergarten & Playgroup Inc.

2 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWCA 2727Employment & workplace
Ravenshoe Kindergarten & Playgroup Inc applied under s 216CA of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for FWC approval of a variation to the Queensland Kindergarten Early Childhood Education Multi-Employer Enterprise Agreement 2026 to add it and its employees as covered. The variation had majority employee support and was not opposed by the IEUA. Commissioner Simpson approved the variation.
Key holding

The FWC is required to approve a variation of a cooperative workplace agreement to add an employer and employees if satisfied that each s 216CB(1) requirement has been met and that the mandatory refusal provisions and s 216CD do not apply.

As I am satisfied that each of the requirements of s.216CB(1) have been met in relation to the application, having regard to the Form F23IA 'Employer's Declaration' filed in support of application and other material before me, and that ss.216CB(2) and 216CD do not apply with respect to each application, I am required to approve the variation.

Upson and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

1 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2275Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
In Upson and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2275, a 31-year-old New Zealand citizen who has lived in Australia since infancy had his special category visa mandatorily cancelled under s 501(3A) after being sentenced to 7 years 6 months for serious drug offences. A delegate refused to revoke the cancellation, but the Administrative Review Tribunal set that decision aside and substituted revocation after finding “another reason” under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii).
Key holding

Under Direction no. 110, whether a non-citizen poses an “unacceptable risk” is a distinct evaluative conclusion reached by balancing the likelihood and potential harm of future offending, not merely the likelihood of re-offending.

The concept of 'risk', and whether it is 'unacceptable', under cl 8.1.2(1) of the Direction is not the same thing as the 'likelihood of the non-citizen engaging in further criminal or other serious conduct'.[25]

Al-Hajj and CEO, National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIS)

30 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2318Tribunal reviewVisa nomination
Mr Al-Hajj sought review of the NDIA’s decision about the statement of participant supports in his NDIS plan, contending for 24/7 1:1 support worker assistance and weekly psychology. The ART considered whether those supports were reasonable and necessary under s 34(1) of the NDIS Act and related rules. It set aside the decisions and remitted the matter with orders for 3 hours/day daily personal activities, 2 hours/day community participation, and 30 hours of psychology.
Key holding

Under s 34(1)(aa) of the NDIS Act, a support must be necessary to address needs arising directly from an impairment for which the participant meets the disability or early intervention requirements.

What follows from the above determination is that for the purposes of s 34(1)(aa), the supports sought on review must be necessary to address needs of Mr Al-Hajj arising directly from his psychosocial impairments.

Austro Enterprises Pty Limited (Migration)

29 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2379Tribunal reviewVisa nominationMigration
Austro Enterprises Pty Limited sought review of a delegate’s refusal to approve a nomination for Mr Erich Martinez as Office Manager under the Core Skills stream of the Subclass 482 visa. The delegate found the position was not genuine under reg 2.72(10) because existing roles allegedly covered the duties. The Tribunal accepted the position was genuine and remitted the nomination for reconsideration.
Key holding

Whether a position is genuine under reg 2.72(10)(a) requires a qualitative, reality-tested comparison between the actual position and the nominated occupation, not merely paper duties.

Regulation 2.72(10)(a) requires that the position associated with the nominated occupation is genuine. A similar requirement was considered in Cargo First Pty Ltd v MIBP [2016] FCA 30, where the Court (at [34]) upheld the Tribunal's approach of qualitatively assessing the position and comparing this with the occupation nominated in order to determine whether it was genuine.

Ms Zhou sought review of the refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) Subclass 500 visa. The delegate refused because it was not satisfied she was a genuine temporary entrant under cl 500.212. By the Tribunal’s decision time she had completed her course and returned home, but because she had no current enrolment, the Tribunal affirmed under cl 500.211.
Key holding

For a Subclass 500 visa, cl 500.211(a) requires current enrolment in a course of study at the time of the Tribunal’s decision, and absence of current enrolment requires affirmation even if the applicant previously studied and completed courses.

However, as she is now not enrolled and cl 500.211 is a time of decision criterion, the Tribunal must affirm the review.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
