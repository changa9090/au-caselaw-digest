2301755 (Refugee)

24 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2464Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewVisa integrityMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a delegate's refusal to grant a protection visa to a gay man from East Timor who claimed he would face societal and familial harm, including ostracism, forced marriage and employment discrimination, if returned. Accepting his claims as credible and finding that state protection would not be effective, the Tribunal was satisfied he is a refugee under s 36(2)(a) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The decision was set aside and remitted for reconsideration.
Key holding

An applicant cannot be required to conceal or modify his sexual orientation to avoid persecution, and the modification exceptions in s 5J(3) apply where conformity would conflict with a characteristic fundamental to his identity or conceal an innate or immutable characteristic.

I find that the applicant could not take reasonable steps to modify his behaviour to avoid a real chance of persecution. Such modifications would fall within the various exceptions in s 5J(3), including conflicting with a characteristic that is fundamental to the applicant's identity or conscience and concealing an innate or immutable characteristic.

2209041 (Refugee)

24 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2443Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a protection visa to a Timorese applicant who claimed economic hardship, witchcraft-related harm and a land dispute. It accepted he could safely live in Dili and found no well-founded fear of persecution or real risk of significant harm. It affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

A claim based on a localised land dispute fails the refugee criterion where the applicant can live safely elsewhere in the country, because the real chance of persecution does not relate to all areas under s 5J(1)(c).

I find that the applicant does not have a well founded fear of persecution because the real chance of persecution arising from the land dispute does not relate to all areas of Timor Leste: s 5J(1)(c).

OLUTAYO ALABA OLORUNDERO (Migration)

24 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2364Tribunal reviewVisa nominationMigration
Olorundero (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2364 concerned review of a delegate’s refusal to approve a Subclass 482 nomination for a Chef (ANZSCO 351311) at The Steaming Cup under s 140GB of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) and reg 2.72 of the Migration Regulations 1994 (Cth). The delegate refused because of the Item 8 limited-service restaurant caveat in LIN 19/048. The Tribunal found the business operated a full-service restaurant and remitted the nomination for reconsideration.
Key holding

A nominated position is not excluded by Item 8 of LIN 19/048 where the preponderance of PAM3 indicative factors establishes that the business is operating a full-service restaurant rather than a limited-service restaurant.

As the Tribunal considers that the applicant meets the preponderance of indicative factors indicating that it is a full-service restaurant as set out in the PAM3, the Tribunal finds that the applicant is operating a full-service restaurant and not a limited-service restaurant.

2104609 (Refugee)

23 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2306Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
In 2104609 (Refugee) [2026] ARTA 2306, the Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal of protection visas to Indian nationals in an interfaith/inter-caste marriage who claimed family and honour-based violence in Punjab. The delegate refused under s 65 because the applicants were not refugees and did not meet complementary protection. The Tribunal set aside the refusal and remitted the matter with a direction that the applicants meet s 36(2)(aa) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth).
Key holding

A protection visa may be granted on complementary protection grounds where the refugee criterion is not met, if removal creates a real risk of significant harm as a necessary and foreseeable consequence.

The issues in this case are whether the applicants have a well-founded fear of being persecuted in India for one or more of the five reasons set out, and, if not, whether there are substantial grounds for believing that, as a necessary and foreseeable consequence of the applicant being removed from Australia to India there is a real risk that they will suffer significant harm.

Yerburgh and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

22 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2291Tribunal reviewVisa nominationProperty
In Yerburgh and Secretary, Department of Social Services [2026] ARTA 2291, the Tribunal reviewed a decision reducing Mrs Yerburgh’s age pension by assessing notional income from a farm treated as an unrealisable asset. The Tribunal set aside the decision and remitted the rate for reassessment from 11 October 2024 on the basis that the relevant income amount was her actual annual ordinary income from the farm, not a commercial lease value.
Key holding

Where an unrealisable farm is already being worked commercially to full capacity, the amount reasonably expected from a purely commercial application under s 1130(5)(b) is the actual income from current operations, and the s 1130(5) notional rate is the lesser of that amount and 2.5% of the farm's value.

However, having heard from Mrs Yerburgh, the Tribunal is satisfied that the farm is currently being operated commercially to its full capacity. As such, the Tribunal finds that what Mrs Yerburgh could reasonably be expected to obtain from a purely commercial application of the farm is the income that is actually being generated from its current operations, not some alternative higher commercial lease figure as adopted by Centrelink.

The Tribunal reviewed the refusal of protection visas for a Bangladeshi man and his minor daughter. Key holding

if, in relation to an application for review of a reviewable protection decision, the applicant raises a claim that was not raised before the primary decision was made, in making a decision on the application, the Tribunal is to draw an inference unfavourable to the credibility of the claim if the Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant does not have a reasonable explanation why the claim was not raised before the primary decision was made (s 367A).

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
AustLII
The Australasian Legal Information Institute: a free public database that publishes the full text of Australian court and tribunal decisions. Every case on this page links to its full text there.
