2215119 (Refugee)

22 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2305Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The ART reviewed a delegate’s refusal of protection visas to a Malaysian family of Indian Malay Roman Catholics. The Tribunal accepted their evidence of racial and religious harassment/discrimination, rejected their loan-shark claims for lack of evidence, and found each applicant met the refugee criterion. It set aside the delegate’s decisions and remitted the matter for reconsideration.
Key holding

Serious, country-wide discrimination or harassment based on race or religion, coupled with unavailable state protection, can amount to persecution under the refugee criterion, and an applicant need not modify their behaviour to avoid it.

no legal protections - discrimination amounts to persecution and serious harm - harm relates to all parts of country, state protection not available and modification of behaviour not required

2105145 (Refugee)

21 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2314Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The ARTA reviewed a delegate’s refusal of Class XA Subclass 866 protection visas to an Indian mother and her two daughters. The Tribunal accepted that the first applicant, a divorced single mother, faced a real chance of serious harm in India as a member of a particular social group of women/single divorced women. It set aside the refusal and remitted the applications for reconsideration.
Key holding

A non-citizen single divorced woman from India may be a refugee where country information shows a real chance of serious harm from the broader community for the essential and significant reason of her profile as a single divorced woman.

For the reasons discussed, the Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant has a real chance of serious harm in the reasonably foreseeable future for the essential and significant reason of her profile as a single, divorced woman, if she returned to India.

2202497 (Refugee)

20 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2463Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed the refusal of a protection visa to a Syrian Alawite widow. It found that changed country conditions after the fall of the al-Assad regime produced a real chance of persecution for reasons of religion and imputed pro-Assad political opinion, with no effective state protection. The Tribunal set aside the refusal and remitted the matter with a direction that the applicant satisfies s 36(2)(a).
Key holding

Even where the risk of harm is multi-faceted and increased by particular vulnerabilities, religion may be an essential and significant reason for the harm, and the harm may be systematic and discriminatory conduct for refugee assessment.

I find there is a real chance of the applicant suffering discrimination, harassment and physical ill-treatment, such as to amount to serious harm. While the reasons for the harm are multi-faceted and the risk and seriousness of the harm are increased by her particular vulnerabilities, I consider that her religion is an essential and significant reason for the harm, and I find that the harm involves systematic and discriminatory conduct.

2404311 (Refugee)

18 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2405Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Administrative Review Tribunal affirmed refusal of a protection visa to an Indonesian citizen of Chinese ethnicity who claimed past racist bullying and feared future discrimination. The Tribunal accepted school bullying and subjective trauma but found adult racist taunts and discrimination were low-level and did not objectively amount to serious or significant harm.
Key holding

For the refugee criterion, a strong subjective fear of harm cannot turn the statutory ‘serious harm’ test into a subjective standard; the feared harm must objectively meet that threshold.

In my view, even a strong subjective fear of harm where the harm does not objectively meet the statutory test for 'serious harm' cannot convert that objective standard into what would become a subjective standard.

Mdallal (Migration)

18 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2382Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationMigration
This was a review of a delegate’s decision to cancel Mr Mdallal’s Subclass 820 (Spouse) visa after he was convicted of contravening an AVO and common assault. Although the Tribunal found the conviction ground for cancellation was made out, it exercised the discretion not to cancel because of his remarriage, his support for two minor children (including an autistic stepdaughter), and the hardship removal would cause. The Tribunal set aside the cancellation and substituted a decision not to cancel.
Key holding

Even where a conviction ground for cancellation under s 116(1)(g) is established, the Tribunal may decline to cancel a temporary spouse visa where the best interests of affected children and associated family hardship outweigh the seriousness of the offending in the particular circumstances.

In the particular circumstances of this case, the Tribunal has decided to give greatest weight to the best interests of the children which weigh against the cancellation. Considering the circumstances as a whole, the Tribunal concludes that the cancellation should be set aside.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
