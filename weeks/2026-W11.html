BHRR v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (No 2) [2026] FCA 250 (13 March 2026)

13 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 250Migration: court challenge
BHRR sought an extension of time under s 477A(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to challenge an AAT decision that it lacked jurisdiction over his visa non-revocation review. The Federal Court found the Minister’s notification letter under s 501G(1)(f)(ii) misstated the review deadline by omitting the effect of s 36(2) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 (Cth), so the AAT did have jurisdiction. The Court granted the extension and held the applicant entitled to final relief.
Key holding

Section 501G(1)(f)(ii) requires notice of the actual time in which review may be made, and where s 36(2) of the Acts Interpretation Act extends the s 500(6B) period because the last day is a weekend or public holiday, a notice stating only nine days is incomplete and inaccurate.

Because s 500(6B) of the Migration Act and s 36(2) of the Acts Interpretation Act do not compete for priority but work together, to state to the applicant that he had nine days after the day on which he was notified of the delegate's decision to lodge an application for review was at least incomplete and was certainly inaccurate if not plainly incorrect.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
