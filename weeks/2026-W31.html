CUCCI (Migration)

31 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2383Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a delegate’s refusal of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa on the basis that Mr Cucci was not a genuine temporary entrant under cl 500.212 of Schedule 2 to the Migration Regulations 1994 (Cth). The Tribunal had considerably more evidence than the delegate and found that the applicant did intend genuinely to stay in Australia temporarily and to comply with visa conditions. It set aside the refusal and remitted the matter for reconsideration.
Key holding

In assessing the genuine temporary entrant criterion, the Tribunal must consider the applicant’s circumstances as a whole and treat the Direction No 108 factors as guides rather than a checklist.

The Direction indicates that the factors specified should not be used as a checklist but rather, are intended only to guide decision makers when considering the applicant's circumstances as a whole, in reaching a finding about whether the applicant satisfies the genuine temporary entrant criterion.

FGS20 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

29 Jul 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1476Migration: court challengeMigration
FGS20 sought a declaration under s 39B of the Judiciary Act 1903 (Cth) that the Secretary failed to bring his ministerial intervention request under ss 46A and 46B of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to the Minister’s attention within a reasonable time. The Federal Court dismissed the application because the applicant had already sent the request directly to the Minister by post and email, so no Secretary duty to duplicate that step was enlivened.
Key holding

Where a ministerial intervention request has already been brought directly to the Minister’s attention by the applicant, any duty on the Secretary to duplicate that notification is not enlivened, so no failure to perform it can be established.

There was no scope for any duty to be imposed on the Secretary to duplicate that information providing exercise. It follows that no such duty was enlivened, and accordingly there could not be any failure to perform such a duty.

Egerton and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

28 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2345Tribunal review
This matter concerned an age pensioner whose rate of payment was reduced after Centrelink treated about half of a terminal illness insurance payout, transferred to his former wife and carer, as a disposal of assets. The Tribunal had to decide whether the care she had provided was “consideration” for the transfer, meaning it should not be assessed under the gifting/disposal rules. The Tribunal held that the transfer was gratuitous and affirmed the decision under review.
Key holding

A transfer to a carer is not for consideration merely because it fairly compensates past care; absent a binding promise, exchange, or legal or equitable obligation to pay, the transfer is gratuitous and assessable under the disposal-of-assets rules.

I accept Mr Egerton's view that the payment was fair compensation for Mrs Egerton's care and labour, but absent any legal or equitable obligation to pay for that care and labour, the payment was gratuitous.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
