Ho (Migration)

3 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2359Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) Subclass 500 visa on genuine temporary entrant grounds. Although the applicant had completed patisserie and hospitality study in Australia, the Tribunal found inconsistencies in his evidence and strong family and financial ties to Australia through his sister’s patisserie business. It was not satisfied he genuinely intended to stay in Australia temporarily and affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

A student visa applicant fails cl 500.212(a) where the Tribunal, on the whole of the evidence and Direction No 108, is not satisfied that the applicant genuinely intends to stay in Australia temporarily.

As the Tribunal is not satisfied that the applicant intends genuinely to stay in Australia temporarily, the applicant does not meet cl 500.212(a). Having concluded that the applicant does not meet cl 500.212(a) it is not necessary to further consider cl 500.212(b) or (c).

Nisha (Migration)

3 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2356Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewVisa integrityMigration
Mrs Batulan Nisha applied for a Partner (Temporary) (Class UK) visa (Subclass 820) in December 2018 on the basis of her marriage to Mr John Mitchell. A delegate refused the visa solely because there was no evidence the sponsor was an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen. On review the applicant produced movement records and citizenship evidence, and the Tribunal, being satisfied the marriage was valid and the relationship genuine and continuing, set aside the refusal and remitted the matter.
Key holding

The absence of joint major assets or liabilities does not tell against the genuineness of a spousal relationship where it is explained by the parties' age and limited income, the reg 1.15A(3) factors being weighed in light of their actual circumstances.

Given their age and limited income, the Tribunal considers the findings that they have no joint major assets or liabilities are not indicative that they are not in a genuine and continuing relationship.

POI Penrith Pty Ltd (Migration)

2 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2369Tribunal reviewVisa nominationMigration
This is a review of a refusal to approve a Subclass 407 training nomination for a Café/Restaurant Manager. The Tribunal found the nominee had completed the only structured training plan and that later material did not show a genuine, current structured training program. It affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

A Subclass 407 nomination cannot succeed where the nominated training program has already been completed, because the decision-maker cannot be satisfied under reg 2.72A(5) that the nominee will participate in that program.

Reg 2.72A(5) requires that 'the Minister is satisfied that the nominee will participate in the nominated program.' As the Abridged Training Plan was completed on 30 April 2026, reg 2.72A(5) cannot be met in relation to this training program.

Dangilan (Migration)

2 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2361Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Administrative Review Tribunal affirmed a refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) Subclass 500 visa because Mr Dangilan did not satisfy the genuine temporary entrant criterion in cl 500.212(a). The Tribunal accepted his career change to hospitality but found his restaurant and teaching plans vague and his further Advanced Diploma extension of limited value. His family and property ties did not outweigh a four-year onshore stay without visits and continuous Australian employment.
Key holding

To satisfy cl 500.212(a), the Tribunal must be affirmatively satisfied on the whole of the circumstances and Direction No 108 that the applicant genuinely intends a temporary stay; otherwise the criterion is not met.

On the basis of the above, the Tribunal is not satisfied that the applicant intends genuinely to stay in Australia temporarily. Accordingly, the applicant does not meet cl 500.212(a).

Teh (Migration)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2362Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This case concerns a Subclass 485 (Temporary Graduate) visa applicant who mistakenly selected the Graduate Work stream but would have met the Post-Study Work stream criteria. The Tribunal held it was bound by Obinwa to assess only the stream selected on the visa application form. The delegate’s refusal was affirmed.
Key holding

An applicant who nominates a particular Subclass 485 stream must satisfy the criteria for that stream, and the Tribunal need not consider an alternative stream.

The consequence is that an applicant for a Class VC visa who nominates a particular stream (as every applicant must, in order to make a valid application) must satisfy the criteria applicable to that stream in order for the visa to be granted. Satisfying criteria that are applicable only to another stream does not assist.

Scanlon (Migration)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2357Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Medical Treatment (Visitor) (Class UB) Subclass 602 visa. The applicant, an Irish citizen in Australia since 2018, claimed he needed treatment for anxiety and a bicep/shoulder injury and intended to stay temporarily. The Tribunal was not satisfied he genuinely intended a temporary stay for medical treatment and affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Clause 602.215 requires a genuine intention to stay temporarily for the specified visa purpose, and an applicant who uses the Medical Treatment visa to maintain ongoing residence does not satisfy it.

Clause 602.215 requires that the applicant genuinely intends to stay temporarily in Australia for the purpose for which the visa is granted.

Ramirez Garcia (Migration)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2353Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed refusals of Temporary Skill Shortage (Class GK) Subclass 482 visas for a primary applicant nominated as a Marketing Specialist and her family. Although the delegate refused on work-experience grounds, the case was ultimately determined by the sponsor’s cessation of trading and the applicant’s move to a different corporate entity. The Tribunal affirmed the refusals but referred the matter for Ministerial intervention.
Key holding

The genuine intention and genuine position requirements in cl 482.212(2) are not met where the applicant is employed by, and intends to remain with, a different corporate entity from the employer sponsor.

Further, given the primary applicant's current employment with another corporate entity that is not the employer sponsor and the clear evidence that she intends to continue to work at this entity, the Tribunal is not satisfied her intention to perform the nominated occupation and the position associated with the nominated occupation are genuine for the purposes of cl 482.212(2) as applicable to this nomination review.

2212562 (Refugee)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2330Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa for a minor Brazilian child, represented by her mother. The Tribunal accepted the family’s explanation for false claims in the original application and found the mother and grandmother credible, but was not satisfied that the claimed risk from Mr R or the general risk of sexual violence in Brazil gave rise to a real chance or real risk of relevant harm. It affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

The “real risk” threshold for complementary protection is the same as the “real chance” test for refugee status.

The Tribunal notes that the threshold for the 'real risk' element in the complementary protection criterion in s 36(2)(aa) is the same as that for the 'real chance' test in the refugee criterion in s 36(2)(a).

2317753 (Refugee)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2323Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The applicant, a Nepalese Christian of Tamang ethnicity, sought review of a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa, claiming past torture and future harm in Nepal due to religion and race. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding he did not face a real chance of serious harm or a real risk of significant harm. Country information indicated Christians in Nepal face low risks, and his past harm claims were not accepted.
Key holding

If an applicant does not face a “real chance” of harm for refugee purposes, the Tribunal will not be satisfied that there is a “real risk” of significant harm for complementary protection.

As I have found that the applicant does not face a 'real chance' of harm, I am not satisfied that there is a real risk that the applicant will suffer harm if he returns to Nepal.

2407106 (Migration)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2270Tribunal reviewVisa integrityVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) visa because a bogus bank document had supported the applicant’s earlier Temporary Activity visa. The Tribunal accepted the document was bogus but found the applicant was unaware of it and neither complicit in nor recklessly indifferent to her coach’s or agent’s conduct. It set aside the refusal and remitted the matter with a direction that PIC 4020 is met for cl 500.217(1).
Key holding

For PIC 4020(1), where a third party supplies a bogus document, an applicant may be taken to have caused it to be given if complicit in, or recklessly indifferent to, that third party’s unlawful or dishonest conduct.

If the applicant is complicit in the provision of false information or 'indifferent' to a third party acting unlawfully or dishonestly, it can be said that the applicant caused it to be given.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
