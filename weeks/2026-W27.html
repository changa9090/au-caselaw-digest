2317753 (Refugee)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2323Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The applicant, a Nepalese Christian of Tamang ethnicity, sought review of a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa, claiming past torture and future harm in Nepal due to religion and race. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding he did not face a real chance of serious harm or a real risk of significant harm. Country information indicated Christians in Nepal face low risks, and his past harm claims were not accepted.
Key holding

If an applicant does not face a “real chance” of harm for refugee purposes, the Tribunal will not be satisfied that there is a “real risk” of significant harm for complementary protection.

As I have found that the applicant does not face a 'real chance' of harm, I am not satisfied that there is a real risk that the applicant will suffer harm if he returns to Nepal.

2407106 (Migration)

1 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2270Tribunal reviewVisa integrityVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) visa because a bogus bank document had supported the applicant’s earlier Temporary Activity visa. The Tribunal accepted the document was bogus but found the applicant was unaware of it and neither complicit in nor recklessly indifferent to her coach’s or agent’s conduct. It set aside the refusal and remitted the matter with a direction that PIC 4020 is met for cl 500.217(1).
Key holding

For PIC 4020(1), where a third party supplies a bogus document, an applicant may be taken to have caused it to be given if complicit in, or recklessly indifferent to, that third party’s unlawful or dishonest conduct.

If the applicant is complicit in the provision of false information or 'indifferent' to a third party acting unlawfully or dishonestly, it can be said that the applicant caused it to be given.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
