Section 91WA(1) of the Act requires the Minister to refuse to grant a protection visa to an applicant who provides, or causes to be provided, a bogus document as evidence of their identity, nationality or citizenship, or if the Minister is satisfied the applicant has destroyed or disposed, or caused the destruction or disposal, of documentary evidence of their identity, nationality or citizenship.

Vani and Minister for Citizenship and Multicultural Affairs (Citizenship)

6 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3180Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Stanley Vani applied for Australian citizenship by descent based on his father’s claimed Aboriginal Australian status, which he said would avoid the effect of s 65(1) of the PNG Constitution. The Tribunal found the Applicant’s father did not satisfy the tripartite test for Aboriginality as at PNG Independence Day, 16 September 1975. The delegate’s refusal of citizenship by descent was affirmed.
Key holding

To establish Aboriginal Australian status, a person must satisfy the tripartite test of biological descent, self-identification, and mutual recognition by elders or others with traditional authority, with mutual recognition requiring authority sourced in pre-European settlement norms.

Membership of the indigenous people depends on biological descent from the indigenous people and on mutual recognition of a particular person's membership by that person and by the elders or other persons enjoying traditional authority among those people.

Before: Jade Murphy

A defect in the Department’s notification letter did not invalidate the notification because the Tribunal found substantial compliance.
Key holding

A notification containing a deemed-receipt error remains valid if it substantially complies with content requirements and causes no substantial prejudice, so the statutory review period runs from deemed receipt.

The Tribunal has considered the content of the refusal notification letter and considers that although there is an error in the notification, the notification is still valid because it substantially complies with the content requirements and the failure to strictly comply does not cause substantial prejudice to the applicant's rights.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
