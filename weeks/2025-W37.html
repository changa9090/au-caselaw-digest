Mr Rage Yassir v Woolworths Group Limited

12 Sep 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2718Employment & workplace
Mr Yassir made an anti-bullying application and a general protections application against Woolworths. After Commissioner Hunt disclosed she owned fewer than 200 Woolworths shares (worth just over $5,000), Mr Yassir applied for her to recuse herself on apprehended bias grounds. Applying the Ebner test, the Commissioner found no logical connection between the shareholding and impartiality given Woolworths has 1.22 billion shares and 372,000 shareholders, and declined to recuse herself.

Mrs Vaishaliben Patel v St John Of God Health Care Inc

11 Sep 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2688Employment & workplace
Mrs Patel resigned from St John of God Health Care and then sought to have the Commission determine a dispute about the calculation of her long service leave entitlements under the enterprise agreement. The Commission found that she had merely made payroll enquiries about her entitlements rather than raising a dispute under the dispute resolution clause while still employed, and that once she resigned she was no longer covered by the agreement. The arbitral powers of the Commission were therefore not enlivened and the application was dismissed.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
