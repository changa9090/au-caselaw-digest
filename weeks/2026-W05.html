Hubzin v The Commissioner for Social Housing; Van Loo v The Commissioner for Social Housing; Irwin v The Commissioner for Social Housing [2026] ACTSC 11 (30 January 2026)

30 Jan 2026Supreme Court of the Australian Capital Territory[2026] ACTSC 11Migration — court challenge
Three long-term ACT public housing tenants (27–42 years' tenure) challenged decisions by the Commissioner for Social Housing to include their dwellings in a redevelopment program and require their relocation. The Commissioner conceded denial of procedural fairness, and the Court granted declaratory relief and set aside the initial decisions, also finding a failure to give proper consideration to the plaintiffs' human rights under the *Human Rights Act 2004* (ACT).

Body Corporate for Westmark Milton CTS v Devine Constructions Pty Ltd [2026] QSC 9 (27 January 2026)

27 Jan 2026Supreme Court of Queensland[2026] QSC 9Negligence
The Supreme Court of Queensland considered whether a body corporate can sue a builder in its own name for defective construction work on common property, relying on s 36(3) of the *Body Corporate and Community Management Act 1997* (Qld) (BCCMA). The builder argued "subrogated" in s 36(3) carried its technical equitable meaning, requiring the body corporate to sue in the original owner's name. Ryan J rejected that argument, declaring the body corporate entitled to proceed in its own name.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits — a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made — the court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
