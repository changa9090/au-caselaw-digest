Hubzin v The Commissioner for Social Housing; Van Loo v The Commissioner for Social Housing; Irwin v The Commissioner for Social Housing [2026] ACTSC 11 (30 January 2026)

30 Jan 2026Supreme Court of the Australian Capital Territory[2026] ACTSC 11Migration: court challenge
Three long-term ACT public housing tenants challenged decisions under the Growing and Renewing Public Housing Program requiring them to transfer from their homes. The Commissioner conceded the initial decisions denied procedural fairness and consented to them being set aside, and the Court also considered human rights claims under the Human Rights Act 2004 (ACT). The Court extended time, granted declaratory relief, and set aside the initial decisions.
Key holding

A decision made under statutory power without the required procedural fairness is invalid and liable to be set aside.

Key holding

Under s 36(3) of the BCCMA, “subrogated” is not used in its technical equitable sense, so a body corporate may enforce the original owner’s contractual rights concerning common property in its own name without joining the original owner.

I am of the view that the word "subrogated", in the wider phrase "subrogated to the rights (if any) of the original owner under the contract", in section 36(3) of the BCCMA does not carry its technical legal meaning. It follows that section 36(3) permits the body corporate to bring a claim in contract in its own name.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
