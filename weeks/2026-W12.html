ABC18 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 274 (20 March 2026)

20 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 274Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa refusal review
ABC18 appealed to the Federal Court from a Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (Div 2) decision dismissing judicial review of an Administrative Appeals Tribunal decision refusing a Protection (Class XA) Subclass 866 visa. The appellant alleged the Tribunal’s adverse credibility findings, based partly on inconsistency between his written and oral protection claims, were illogical, irrational or lacked probative basis. Button J dismissed the appeal with costs.
Key holding

A Tribunal does not act illogically or irrationally by treating a protection applicant’s later oral claims as inconsistent with earlier written claims, and using that inconsistency on credibility, unless it has accepted that the applicant had no knowledge of the earlier written claims.

Unless the Tribunal were to have accepted that the Appellant had no knowledge of the first set of claims being made in his written claims, it was not illogical for the Tribunal to regard the Appellant's accounts as inconsistent and take that into account in assessing the Appellant's oral protection claims.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
