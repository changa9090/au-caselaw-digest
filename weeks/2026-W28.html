Lind and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security)

10 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2296Tribunal review
In Lind and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security) [2026] ARTA 2296, Ms Lind sought review of Centrelink’s suspension of her jobseeker payment while she travelled to [Country 1] to visit her mother after a hip fracture. The Tribunal considered whether her absence was for an approved portability reason, specifically an acute family crisis. The Tribunal affirmed the suspension because her mother was not critically ill or hospitalised at the time Ms Lind departed Australia.
Key holding

Jobseeker payment is portable for an acute family crisis only where the absence is for visiting a family member who is critically ill, meaning their condition is severe or grave or there is a crisis in the condition.

Although the Act does not define what constitutes being critically ill, the case law indicates that "critically" must be given its ordinary meaning and that a person's condition must be severe or grave or that there must be a crisis in the condition - see, for example, Al Umari and Secretary, DFaCS [2003] AATA 431.

Ludlam and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

8 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2445Tribunal reviewVisa nominationProperty
The Tribunal reviewed Centrelink’s rejection of Ms Ludlam’s jobseeker payment claim because her combined assets exceeded the applicable limit. Centrelink treated the Cairns house as her principal home and as an asset, applying the lower homeowner assets limit. The Tribunal found Ms Ludlam was not a homeowner and remitted the claim for reassessment under the higher non-homeowner limit.
Key holding

Where a person leaves their owned home and resides in rented accommodation without an s 11A circumstance preserving it as the principal home, the person is not a homeowner under s 11(4) and the non-homeowner assets limit applies, although the former home may still be counted as an asset.

The Cairns property ceased to be Ms Ludlam's principal home when she left Cairns and took up residence in rented accommodation in Canberra. She therefore was not a homeowner as defined in subsection 11(4) of the Act and the higher asset limit applies.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
