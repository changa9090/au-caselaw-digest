Rushpinder Kaur (Migration)

11 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2367Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
This is a review of a delegate's refusal of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa on the basis that the applicant, a 23-year-old Indian national, was not a genuine temporary entrant. The Tribunal accepted the applicant's evidence as frank and credible, found her family ties in India and the value of her Australian study outweighed her stronger Australian earnings, and remitted the matter. It is a fact-focused GTE decision with no new legal principle.
Key holding

In assessing the genuine temporary entrant criterion, the Direction No. 108 factors are guides only rather than a checklist, and the decision maker must determine on balance whether the applicant satisfies the criterion having regard to all the circumstances.

The Direction indicates that the factors specified should not be used as a checklist but rather, are intended only to guide decision makers when considering the applicant's circumstances as a whole, in reaching a finding about whether the applicant satisfies the genuine temporary entrant criterion.

Lind and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security)

10 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2296Tribunal review
In Lind and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security) [2026] ARTA 2296, Ms Lind sought review of Centrelink’s suspension of her jobseeker payment while she travelled to [Country 1] to visit her mother after a hip fracture. The Tribunal considered whether her absence was for an approved portability reason, specifically an acute family crisis. The Tribunal affirmed the suspension because her mother was not critically ill or hospitalised at the time Ms Lind departed Australia.
Key holding

Jobseeker payment is portable for an acute family crisis only where the absence is for visiting a family member who is critically ill, meaning their condition is severe or grave or there is a crisis in the condition.

Although the Act does not define what constitutes being critically ill, the case law indicates that "critically" must be given its ordinary meaning and that a person's condition must be severe or grave or that there must be a crisis in the condition - see, for example, Al Umari and Secretary, DFaCS [2003] AATA 431.

Fearon and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security)

10 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2294Tribunal reviewProperty
Ms Fearon receives age pension. Centrelink treated $70,500 she effectively gifted to her daughter in December 2022 as a deprived asset. The Tribunal affirmed that decision, finding the gifting rules required the amount to be included in her assets for five years.
Key holding

A disposal of an asset, including money, for no or inadequate consideration must be included as an asset for five years from the date of disposition.

The legislation is clear, where an asset (which includes money) has been disposed of for no or inadequate consideration, it must be included as an asset for five years from the date of disposition.

Scrubb and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

9 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2290Tribunal review
Mrs Scrubb sought review of a Centrelink decision declining additional child care subsidy (ACCS) for her son, [Child A], for 16 June 2025 to 14 September 2025. The Tribunal considered whether [Child A] met the “at risk of serious abuse or neglect” criterion, limited to a claimed risk of neglect. It found the risk was not real and apparent and affirmed the decision.
Key holding

For ACCS child wellbeing, a child is at risk of serious abuse or neglect only if the risk is real and apparent, not merely a hypothetical or far-fetched possibility.

Nonetheless, I accept the sense in the Guide that what 'risk' requires, in context, is a real and apparent risk. The test cannot be made out by pointing to some far-fetched possibility.

Chambwela (Migration)

8 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2388Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Mr Chambwela sought review of a refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) subclass 500 visa. He had completed his Advanced Diploma of Civil Construction and Design and was awaiting graduation, but did not provide proof of current enrolment in a registered course as at the time of decision. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

A subclass 500 applicant must be enrolled in a registered course at the time of decision; having completed the course and awaiting graduation does not satisfy the enrolment requirement.

Awaiting formal graduation is not a basis for a student visa under the Act.

Tambling and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

7 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2295Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationContracts
Ms Tambling was granted Disability Support Pension from 21 August 2023, but Centrelink calculated her rate using an allegedly incorrect gifting/assets assessment. On internal review, the ARO found she had been underpaid $31,904.71, but paid only $25,191.54 due to date-of-effect limits under s 109 of the Social Security (Administration) Act 1999 (Cth). The Tribunal found the remaining arrears were payable and set aside the review decision.
Key holding

Where a person is given notice of a decision and requests review within 13 weeks, a favourable determination on review takes effect from the date of the original decision.

Subsection 109(1) provides that if a person is given notice of a decision, and they request a review within 13 weeks after the date the notice is given, the favourable determination takes effect on the date the original decision took effect.

Morales Rivera (Migration)

6 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2360Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewMigration
This case concerns a Colombian applicant who sought a Medical Treatment (Visitor) (Class UB) Subclass 602 visa for mental health treatment after nearly 14 years in Australia. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal because it was not satisfied she genuinely intended a temporary stay for the visa purpose and considered she would not comply with the mandatory No Work condition.
Key holding

For a Subclass 602 visa, cl 602.215 requires the applicant to genuinely intend to stay temporarily for the visa purpose unless the specific cl 602.212(6) exception applies; use of the visa pathway to maintain ongoing residence will fail that requirement.

Clause 602.215 requires that the applicant genuinely intends to stay temporarily in Australia for the purpose for which the visa is granted.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
