TSXN v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 205 (6 March 2026)

6 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 205Migration: court challenge
The Federal Court judicially reviewed an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Protection (Class XA) (subclass 866) visa under s 501(1) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Court found the Tribunal misunderstood its review task by repeatedly reasoning as if it were deciding whether to revoke a mandatory visa cancellation under s 501CA(4), rather than whether to refuse to grant a visa under s 501(1). The application was allowed, the Tribunal decision quashed, and the matter remitted for reconsideration.
Key holding

On review of a visa refusal under s 501(1), the Tribunal must address the distinct statutory task of deciding whether to refuse to grant the visa, not whether to revoke a mandatory cancellation under s 501CA(4).

A decision made under s 501(1) is distinct to one under s 501CA(4). Whilst s 501(1) is concerned with a refusal by the Minister to grant a visa, s 501CA(4) is concerned with the revocation of the mandatory cancellation of a visa.

6 Mar 2026Supreme Court of New South Wales[2026] NSWSC 130Migration: court challenge
Crowley Australia Pty Ltd challenged an adjudication determination under the Construction Contracts (Security of Payments) Act 2004 (NT) requiring it to refund $6.1 million in bank guarantee proceeds to Latitude 63 LLC. The principal alleged apprehended bias, lack of jurisdiction, procedural unfairness, and sought a stay pending arbitration. Rees J dismissed the Amended Summons with costs and refused the stay.
Key holding

A party's criticisms of an adjudicator in related review proceedings do not, without more, establish apprehended bias in a later adjudication between the same parties.

To the extent that those submissions were intemperate or offensive, that says nothing about the adjudicator's ability to bring a fair, impartial and unprejudiced mind to the resolution of another adjudication determination between the same parties.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
