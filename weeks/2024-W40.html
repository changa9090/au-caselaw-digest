2018339 (Refugee)

6 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4445Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Wayne Pennell
The Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa to a Vietnamese citizen who claimed fear of persecution as a Catholic and as an active member of Viet Tan in Australia. It accepted she had an actual, ongoing and publicly identifiable political profile through Viet Tan and online anti-government activity. The matter was remitted with a direction that she satisfies s 36(2)(a) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth).
Key holding

Where an applicant has an actual, ongoing and publicly identifiable political profile—such as active Viet Tan membership and online anti-government commentary—and country information shows a real chance of persecution on return for political opinion, the applicant has a well-founded fear under s 5J and is a refugee under s 5H.

Having considered all the circumstances as they apply individually and cumulatively to the applicant, the Tribunal finds that there is a real chance she will be persecuted for reasons of her political opinion if she were to return to Vietnam, and her fear of persecution is well-founded as required by section 5J of the Act.

LIN (Migration)

6 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4056Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: T. Quinn
Miss Xinna Lin sought review of a delegate’s refusal of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa because she had not provided English language proficiency evidence. The Tribunal found she was exempt under IMMI 18/015 because she had completed a substantial component of her Diploma of Information Technology in Australia in English while holding a student visa in the two years before applying. It remitted the visa application for reconsideration with a direction that cl 500.213 is met.
Key holding

Under IMMI 18/015, a Subclass 500 visa applicant satisfies the English language requirement in cl 500.213(1) if, in the two years before applying, they successfully completed a substantial component of an AQF Certificate IV or higher course conducted in Australia in English while holding a student visa.

The relevant instrument IMMI 18/015 indicates that an applicant, who in the two years before applying for a Subclass 500 (Student) visa, has successfully completed a substantial component of a course leading to a qualification from the Australian Qualifications Framework at the Certificate IV level or higher that was conducted in Australia and in English, while the applicant was holding a student visa meets the English language requirements of clause 500.213(1).

Before: Adrienne Anderson

Key holding

For a protection visa review where the applicant is not in immigration detention, the application must be lodged within 28 days after deemed notification; if lodged after the prescribed period, the Tribunal lacks jurisdiction.

1913348 (Refugee)

4 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4453Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Bryn Butler
The Tribunal affirmed refusal of a protection visa to a Pakistani citizen who claimed to have converted from Sunni to Shia Islam in Australia and feared harm from family, community and extremists if returned. Although he claimed a 2017 attack and a business dispute with a childhood friend, the Tribunal found the real chance of persecution did not relate to all areas of Pakistan and relocation was reasonable. Decision: 1913348 (Refugee) [2024] AATA 4453, 4 October 2024.
Key holding

For the refugee criterion, a person has a well-founded fear only if the real chance of persecution relates to all areas of the relevant country.

Under s 5J(1), a person has a well-founded fear of persecution if they fear being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion, there is a real chance they would be persecuted for one or more of those reasons, and the real chance of persecution relates to all areas of the relevant country.

Key holding

A protection visa applicant bears the onus of establishing the statutory criteria, and the Tribunal need not accept uncritically mere assertions of feared persecution or significant harm.

The mere fact that a person claims fear of persecution for a particular reason does not establish either the genuineness of the asserted fear or that it is 'well-founded' or that it is for the reason claimed. The primary applicant claimed fear of persecution in Jordan due to opposition to the politics of extremism, but the applicants conceded the criteria were not met and consented to a decision on the papers. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal after considering the claims against country information.
Key holding

In protection visa review, the applicant bears the responsibility to specify all particulars and provide sufficient evidence to establish the claim; the Tribunal is not obliged to specify or establish the claim.

It is the responsibility of the applicant to specify all particulars of the claim to be a person in respect of whom Australia has protection obligations and to provide sufficient evidence to establish the claim. The Tribunal does not have any responsibility or obligation to specify, or assist in specifying any particulars of the claim, or to establish or assist in establishing the claim: s 5AAA.

Mrs Singh, a 72-year-old New Zealand citizen, applied for Australian citizenship by conferral but had been an Australian permanent resident for less than the 12 months required before her application. The Tribunal affirmed the delegate’s refusal because she did not satisfy the residence requirement and no statutory discretion applied.
Key holding

Eligibility under s 21(3)(c) requires satisfaction of the applicable residence or defence service requirement at the time of application, and the Tribunal cannot waive that requirement through a general discretion absent the statutory conditions.

However, these are not factors that assist Mrs Singh in meeting the residence requirements, or that allow them to be waived. Mrs Singh did not satisfy the general residence requirement (see section 22) or the special residence requirement (see section 22A or 22B), or the defence service requirement (see section 23), at the time she applied for citizenship, and so she does not meet the eligibility requirement of subsection 21(3) of the Citizenship Act.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
