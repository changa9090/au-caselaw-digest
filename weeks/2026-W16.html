EFS18 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 461 (17 April 2026)

17 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 461Migration: court challengeMigration
EFS18, an Egyptian national, sought judicial review of an Immigration Assessment Authority decision affirming refusal of a protection visa, which the Federal Circuit and Family Court (Division 2) dismissed. He appealed to the Federal Court, raising grounds about unwarranted assumptions, country information, health issues, procedural fairness and legal unreasonableness. The Federal Court dismissed the appeal with costs.
Key holding

An alleged unwarranted assumption is not a separate ground of review but a rationality challenge, requiring proof that the assumption had no evident or intelligible basis and no reasonable decision-maker could make it.

This is not an independent ground of review but rather a particular instance of rationality review. A party seeking to rely upon an unwarranted assumption to establish jurisdictional error will need to show that the assumption is not one which a reasonable decision maker could make or, perhaps to paraphrase, that it does not have an evident or intelligible basis.

Ms Ashleigh Llewellyn v Sexyland Australia Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1302 (17 April 2026)

17 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1302Employment & workplace

The Commission found the later general protections application was barred by s.725 because the earlier unfair dismissal application had not been withdrawn or failed on specified grounds, and dismissed it under s.587(1)(a).
Key holding

Section 725 prevents an applicant from making a general protections dismissal application while a prior unfair dismissal application in relation to the dismissal remains on foot, and such later application may be dismissed under s.587(1)(a).

As at 2 September 2025 when the general protections application was made, Ms Llewellyn's unfair dismissal application had not been withdrawn, failed for want of jurisdiction, or failed because the Commission found the dismissal to be a genuine redundancy. Ms Llewellyn was, therefore, prevented by section 725 from making a general protections involving dismissal application.

Chinacalle v Minister for Immigration, Citizenship, Migrant Services and Multicultural Affairs [2026] FCA 441 (16 April 2026)

16 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 441Migration: court challenge
Maria Chinacalle held a subclass 485 visa as a secondary visa holder based on her de facto relationship with the primary visa holder, Mr Ortega. After they separated, Mr Ortega’s visa was cancelled under s 116(1)(g), and her visa was cancelled under s 140(1) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). She sought a declaration that her visa was not cancelled because she was no longer a member of his family unit at the time of cancellation; the Federal Court dismissed her application.
Key holding

Section 140(1) automatically cancels a secondary visa that was held because of family-unit membership at the time of grant, regardless of whether that membership still existed when the primary visa was cancelled.

The proper construction of s 140(1) is that it cancels a secondary visa which was held by that other person because of being a member of the family unit of the first person, at the time the secondary person's visa was granted. If that first person's visa is cancelled (on certain bases) then that secondary person's visa is also cancelled.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
