2309210 (Refugee)

15 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2350Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of protection visas for a Filipino mother and her two children after their application was separated from her former partner. It accepted the mother’s history of prolonged gender-based and family violence, ongoing threats, and societal stigma/economic hardship as a single mother, and found she was a refugee. The refusal was set aside and the matter remitted for reconsideration.
Key holding

Unwed single women with children and survivors of domestic violence in the Philippines can constitute a particular social group under s 5L where the shared gender characteristic is innate or distinguishing and is not merely a fear of persecution.

On the evidence before me, I am stated that 'unwed single women with children and survivors of domestic violence' in the Philippines constitute a particular social group within the meaning of 5L of the Act. Their gender is a recognised characteristic which is innate and distinguishes the group from others in the society and the characteristic is not a fear of persecution.

The Tribunal reviewed a protection visa refusal for a South African Afrikaner man who claimed fear of a former worker’s threat, unemployment/BBEE discrimination, violent crime, police inaction, land expropriation and infrastructure failure. Key holding

A refugee claim based on localised crime fails where the real chance of persecution does not relate to all areas of the receiving country and relocation to a safer area is reasonable.

I am not satisfied that the real chance of persecution relates to all areas of South Africa. It would not extend for example to the area covered by 91% of police precincts, a vast area of varied landscape, environments and economic development.

2111508 (Refugee)

This is an ART review of a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa to a Filipino applicant relying on her mental and physical health conditions and claimed inability to access or afford treatment in the Philippines. Key holding

A claimed inability to access or afford mental health care does not establish refugee persecution absent the statutory requirement of systematic and discriminatory conduct, assessed against the evidence and country information.

I also raised with the applicant my concerns that she may not meet the criteria for a protection visa because there is no persecution involving systematic and discriminatory conduct as required by s 5J(4)(c) of the Act.

Key holding

Where a protection applicant raises new claims or evidence at the Tribunal without a reasonable explanation for not having raised them before the primary decision, s 423A requires the Tribunal to draw an adverse credibility inference.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
