Slowey and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social security)

16 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2448Tribunal reviewVisa nominationEmployment & workplace
Mrs Slowey sought review of a Centrelink decision raising a family tax benefit debt of $1,607.51 and an interest charge of $5.02 after her adjusted taxable income included employer-provided accommodation treated as reportable fringe benefits. The Administrative Review Tribunal found the debt was due but waived it in full under s 101, and held the interest charge not payable under s 78E.
Key holding

Where an FTB debt did not arise from knowing non-compliance and there are special circumstances, beyond financial hardship alone, making waiver desirable and more appropriate than write-off, the Tribunal may waive the debt under s 101.

As it is not more appropriate to write off the debt than to waive it, the Tribunal concludes that all elements of the waiver under section 101 of the Family Assistance Administration Act are made out. The Tribunal considers that it is appropriate to waive Mrs Slowey's debt in its entirety.

Rodham and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security)

16 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2346Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationEmployment & workplace
Mr Rodham's jobseeker payment was cancelled after he failed to enter a Job Plan (employment pathway plan) by 22 July 2025, notwithstanding that he had lodged a Centrelink medical certificate on 24 June 2025 certifying incapacity until 24 September 2025. Centrelink did not record the temporary incapacity exemption until 19 July 2025 and conceded it could locate no record of having told him he had to enter a Job Plan within 28 days. The Tribunal set aside the cancellation and substituted a decision that payment remained payable.
Key holding

Where a claimant holds a medical certificate exempting them from mutual obligation requirements for the relevant period, and Centrelink's delay in recording that exemption (combined with a failure to notify the plan requirement) puts compliance beyond the claimant's control, the claimant is not required to satisfy the employment pathway plan requirements and payment remains payable.

The Tribunal is satisfied that these are circumstances which are beyond the person's control which made it unreasonable to expect Mr Rodham to comply with the employment pathway plan requirements in accordance with subsection 40L(2) of the Administration Act, such that a determination is in effect that Mr Rodham was not required to satisfy the employment pathway requirements for the period 24 June 2025 to 24 September 2025.

Kirby and Secretary, Department of Social Services (Social Security)

16 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2292Tribunal reviewVisa nominationEmployment & workplace
The Tribunal reviewed a Centrelink decision raising a $4,109.18 newstart allowance debt against Mr Kirby for 30 November 2018 to 1 April 2019 due to undeclared employment income. The Tribunal set aside the decision and remitted the matter because Centrelink’s earlier income apportionment was not the method required under s 1117D(9) of the Social Security Act 1991. Any recalculated overpayment remains a Commonwealth debt and recovery was not waived.
Key holding

Where pre-7 December 2020 Division 3 work income is reviewed and neither the entitlement period in which it was earned nor the payroll period can be identified, it must be assessed under s 1117D(9) in the entitlement period in which it was received.

This means that the third approach in subsection 1117D(9) must be used so that [Mr Kirby's] wages are to be assessed on each day of the newstart allowance entitlement period in which he received the income.

2532892 (Migration)

15 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2389Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Child (Migrant) (Class AH) Subclass 101 visa for an applicant over 18 who was not in a relationship or full-time work and who completed high school in June 2023 but did not start full-time study until December 2023, after the visa application was lodged on 4 August 2023. The Tribunal accepted the study gap was exceptional and beyond his control but held it had no discretion because binding authority requires actual commencement of full-time study by the time of application. It affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Under cl 101.213(1)(c), a Subclass 101 applicant over 18 must have actually commenced the relevant full-time course of study by the time the visa application is lodged, and the six-month/reasonable-time grace period does not remove that requirement.

The Court held that cl 101.213(1)(c) requires that a visa applicant must have actually commenced a full-time course of study by the time the visa application is lodged and to construe the statutory provision otherwise was to re-write the words 'been undertaking'.

Key holding

For discretionary waiver under s 101, special circumstances require the individual circumstances to be outside the usual or ordinary course; mere inadvertence, financial hardship or administrative delay will not suffice.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).

The Australasian Legal Information Institute: a free public database that publishes the full text of Australian court and tribunal decisions. Every case on this page links to its full text there.
