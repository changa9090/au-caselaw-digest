R v Hall

26 Jun 2026District Court of New South Wales[2026] NSWDC 379Migration: court challengeMigration
Before: Bourke SC DCJ Decision: Convicted and sentenced to a term of imprisonment of 5 y
Mr Daniel Hall was sentenced in the District Court of New South Wales for importing a commercial quantity of cocaine (7,160.78g pure) into Sydney Airport on 19 June 2025, concealed in the lining of a suitcase he collected in London. He pleaded guilty at the earliest opportunity. Bourke SC DCJ found him to be an expendable courier occupying a role above the low range but comfortably below mid-range objective seriousness, and imposed a head sentence of 5 years 3 months with a non-parole period of 2 years 3 months.
Key holding

For Commonwealth offences, the common law requirement that hardship to family be "exceptional" before it significantly mitigates sentence does not apply, because s 16A(2)(p) of the Crimes Act 1914 (Cth) directs the court to take into account the probable effect of the sentence on family or dependants according to its terms.

However, it has been clear in this state since the decision in Totaan [2022] NSWCCA 75, that this is not so in cases involving Commonwealth offences. That is because paragraph (p) of s 16A(2) makes specific reference to the need for the Court to take into account the probable effect on family or dependants.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
