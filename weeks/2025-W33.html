Communications, Electrical, Electronic, Energy, Information, Postal, Plumbing and Allied Services Union of Australia (128V) v Schindler Lifts Australia Pty. Ltd.

14 Aug 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2385Employment & workplace
The ETU applied under s418 for an order stopping a lockout by Schindler Lifts. Schindler had given its notice of employer response action to employees who are ETU workplace delegates rather than to the ETU itself. The Commission found this did not comply with s414(5)(a), so the lockout was not protected industrial action, and ordered that it stop and not occur for 14 days.

Application by United Firefighters' Union of Australia (259V)

14 Aug 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2384Employment & workplace
The UFU sought referral of two questions of law to the Federal Court about the Commission's power to include consultation terms in an intractable bargaining workplace determination. The President refused the referral, holding the questions did not arise in the matter because they rested on a contested factual premise and were partly not pure questions of law. Even if jurisdiction existed, the discretion would not be exercised given the delay it would cause to finalising the determination and delivering wage increases to frontline workers.

Affinity Nursing Recruitment Pty Ltd v Ms Meera Thomas

11 Aug 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2344Employment & workplace
Affinity Nursing sought a stay of a Commissioner's decision rejecting its jurisdictional objection that Ms Thomas, a former casual employee, had not completed the minimum employment period under ss382 and 384 of the Fair Work Act. Vice President Gibian refused the stay, holding that s606(1) only permits staying the operation of the decision itself, which had no ongoing coercive effect, and that the balance of convenience did not favour a stay. The Commissioner retained carriage of the substantive unfair dismissal proceedings.

11 Aug 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2337Employment & workplace
Mr Mohamed sought an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act after Uber terminated his access to its platform for persistently low customer satisfaction ratings. The Commission found Uber complied with each relevant element of the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code, including warning, notice, suspension, opportunity to respond, and a valid reason based on reasonable grounds. Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
