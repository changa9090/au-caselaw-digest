FDB17 v Minister for Immigration, Citizenship and Multicultural Affairs

29 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1612Migration: court challengeMigration
The appellant, a Pakistani protection visa applicant, appealed a Federal Circuit and Family Court judgment dismissing his judicial review application concerning an AAT decision that affirmed refusal of his visa. He argued the Tribunal fell into jurisdictional error by unreasonably declining to telephone four corroborating witnesses (his parents, brother and a friend) who had already provided a joint signed statement. The Federal Court dismissed the appeal, holding the Tribunal's approach had a rational and intelligible foundation.
Key holding

Where a decision-maker is convinced that an applicant's account is fabricated or fundamentally unreliable, it is not irrational or illogical to decline to hear oral corroborating evidence and to reject that corroboration, even absent any independent ground for doubting the corroborators.

It is not necessarily irrational, or illogical, for a finder of fact, who is convinced that a principal witness is fabricating a story, which is considered to be inherently implausible, to reject corroborative evidence, even though there is no separate or independent ground for its rejection, apart from the reasons given for disbelieving the principal witness.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
