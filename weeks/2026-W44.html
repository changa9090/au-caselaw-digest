Estefania Asensio Tato v Lycée Condorcet Trading AS The International French School Of Sydney Ltd

The Fair Work Commission dealt with a s 739 dispute brought by a former secondary Spanish teacher against her former school over alleged underpayments/classification for February and March 2025 work. The Commission upheld the school’s jurisdictional objection because the dispute was not raised until after employment ended, dismissed the application, and noted any underpayment claim could be pursued in court. Key holding

Under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), the Commission lacks jurisdiction to determine an enterprise agreement dispute where the dispute is raised after the employment relationship has ended.

Ms Karen McClear v A Team Tuition Australia Pty Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4174Employment & workplace
Ms McClear resigned after a lengthy suspension pending an unresolved misconduct investigation and brought a s 365 general protections claim alleging she was forced to resign. A Team Tuition objected that she was not dismissed within s 386. The Commission dismissed the application, finding she was not forced to resign because of the employer’s conduct and was therefore not dismissed.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s 386(1)(b) only where an objective assessment of the employer’s conduct shows it made resignation the probable result or left the employee with no effective or real choice but to resign.

In making that assessment, it is appropriate to make an objective analysis of the Respondent's conduct to determine whether it was of such a nature that resignation was the probable result or that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).

The Australasian Legal Information Institute: a free public database that publishes the full text of Australian court and tribunal decisions. Every case on this page links to its full text there.
