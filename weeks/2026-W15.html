GMM18 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 400 (10 April 2026)

10 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 400Migration: court challenge
The appellants, husband and wife citizens of India, appealed to the Federal Court after the Federal Circuit and Family Court dismissed judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming refusal of Protection (Class XA) (Subclass 866) visas. The Tribunal rejected GMM18’s claims of persecution by Shiromani Akali Dal (SAD) in Punjab and found no real chance of serious harm or real risk of significant harm. The Federal Court dismissed the appeal, holding the grounds amounted to disagreement with factual findings and disclosed no jurisdictional error.
Key holding

On judicial review of a migration tribunal decision, the court’s role is limited to identifying jurisdictional error and does not extend to assessing the merits of the applicant’s factual claims.

It was not the role of the Federal Circuit Court, nor is it the role of this Court, to "assess" the appellants' claims. The role of the Court was to review the Tribunal's decision for jurisdictional error and not to engage in merits review of the appellants' claims.

10 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 413Migration: court challenge

Property Exchange Australia Limited v Registrar-General of New South Wales [2026] NSWSC 344 (10 April 2026)

10 Apr 2026Supreme Court of New South Wales[2026] NSWSC 344Migration: court challenge
PEXA sought judicial review of the NSW Registrar-General’s decision to withhold approval for PEXA to pass through a new NECDS Ltd transaction fee to subscribers, pending IPART’s review of ELNO service fees. The Supreme Court dismissed PEXA’s summons, finding no misconstruction of the “may not be unreasonably withheld” requirement and no unreasonable withholding of approval. PEXA was ordered to pay the defendants’ costs.
Key holding

Approval of a cost pass-through under the Operating Requirements is not automatic; the Registrar-General may reasonably withhold approval pending an independent pricing review if that decision is based on evidence and all relevant circumstances.

This provision is not to be construed as a mechanism for an automatic pass through of ELNO operating costs to Subscribers through increased or additional ELNO Service Fees. Rather, it is a matter for the Registrar, acting reasonably, to approve any such increase based on the ELNO's reasoning and evidence, and taking into account all relevant facts and circumstances.

Sheather v V & E Bozzi & Co Transport Pty Ltd (Ruling No 1) [2026] VSC 203 (10 April 2026)

10 Apr 2026Supreme Court of Victoria[2026] VSC 203Negligence
Mike Sheather sued V & E Bozzi & Co Transport Pty Ltd for injuries suffered at Texas Downs, NSW, after the defendant had admitted employment and a Victorian workers compensation claim had been accepted. On the eve of trial the defendant sought leave to withdraw that admission and allege employment by Bozzi Produce, or alternatively to issue a third party notice against Bozzi Produce. Key holding

On a late application to withdraw an admission, the court exercises its discretion by assessing where justice lies, including the extent to which allowing the amendment would cause substantial delay or unfair prejudice to the opposing party.

Particularly in the case of a late amendment such as this, that includes an assessment of whether or the extent to which the amendment if allowed would cause substantial delay or other unfair prejudice to the plaintiff.

DGC24 v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs [2026] FCA 406 (9 April 2026)

9 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 406Migration: court challenge
DGC24 appealed to the Federal Court from a primary judge’s dismissal of his judicial review application concerning an AAT decision to affirm refusal of his protection visa. The appellant had a history of visa cancellation and criminal convictions, and raised numerous grounds challenging the AAT’s credibility findings and procedures. The Federal Court dismissed the appeal with costs, refusing leave for new grounds and finding no appealable error.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
