Fred-Tutavaha v Assistant Minister for Citizenship, Customs and Multicultural Affairs

26 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1586Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicant, a New Zealand citizen who arrived in Australia aged four, sought judicial review of the Assistant Minister's decision under s 501BA of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to set aside an AAT decision revoking the cancellation of his visa. He ran three grounds — unreasonable delay, failure to consider immigration detention as a legal consequence, and legal unreasonableness in giving legal consequences "neutral weight" — with the Court also considering a proposed fourth ground on the national interest assessment.
Key holding

A failure to refer expressly to immigration detention in reasons for a s 501BA cancellation does not permit an inference that this legal consequence was not considered, where detention pending removal is an obvious and well-known statutory consequence and there is no barrier to removal.

It does not follow, however, that the proper inference is that the Assistant Minister failed to appreciate that a legal consequence of his decision was that the Applicant would be taken into immigration detention. The statement of reasons must be read fairly and as a whole, and the fact that a matter is not mentioned expressly does not necessarily mean it was not considered

"Automotive, Food, Metals, Engineering, Printing and Kindred Industries Union" known as the Australian Manufacturing Workers' Union (AMWU) (188V) v Schindler Lifts Australia Pty Ltd

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4166Employment & workplace
The AMWU referred a dispute under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) and clause 10 of the Schindler Lifts Australia Pty Ltd Victorian Enterprise Agreement 2024–2028, asking how the "make up pay for workers comp" entitlement in clause 42 is calculated. The Commission held that "average weekly earnings" in clause 42 is to be worked out by reference to the Victorian workers' compensation concept of pre-injury average weekly earnings under the WIRC Act, and left the arithmetic to the parties.
Key holding

Where an enterprise agreement requires a top-up to 100% of "average weekly earnings" but does not define the term, that expression is to be calculated by reference to the pre-injury average weekly earnings concept in the Victorian workers' compensation legislation, with either s.153(1) or s.153(2) of the WIRC Act available.

As such, I consider that Mr Savari's 'average weekly earnings' for the purposes of clause 42 of the 2024 Agreement in relation to his workers' compensation claim for injury for the injury on 20 March 2025 are able to be calculated by reference to either s.153(1) or s.153(2) of the WIRC Act.

Elizabeth O'Donnell v Fresleven Pty Ltd ATF The Muley Graves Unit Trust T/As Coral Bay Ecotours & Roger Bailye and Others

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4171Employment & workplace
Ms O’Donnell brought a s.365 general protections application against Fresleven Pty Ltd and others, alleging she had been dismissed. The Commission determined a jurisdictional objection that she had not been dismissed. Deputy President O’Keeffe found no dismissal was communicated, upheld the objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A termination of employment is not effective unless the employer communicates it to the employee in clear and unequivocal terms; allusion or insinuation is insufficient.

As I read them the proposition is that an employee is not terminated until they are advised in clear and unequivocal terms that they are terminated. It is not sufficient to allude or insinuate - the employee must be told.

Application by Raquel Gray

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4143Employment & workplaceContracts
The Fair Work Commission determined an unfair termination remedy application by a regulated road transport contractor, Ms Gray, against BESB Couriers after she was replaced on the Toukley delivery run upon returning from leave. The Commission found there was no valid reason for termination and that the Road Transport Industry Termination Code processes were not followed, so the termination was unfair. It ordered that a new services contract be entered into between BESB Couriers and Ms Gray personally.
Key holding

A regulated road transport contractor’s termination is unfair where there is no valid reason for termination and the processes under the Road Transport Industry Termination Code are not followed.

In summary, there was no valid reason for the termination of the services contract, and the processes in the Road Transport Code were not followed, in part because of a breakdown in communication and in part because BESB Couriers is a small business with l imited access to resources or relevant legal or human resources expertise. I am satisfied that the termination of the services contract was unfair.

Mr Marco Maletti v Kitcraft Kitchens Pty Ltd Trading AS Kitchen Craftsmen

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4162Employment & workplace
Marco Maletti applied under s.394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for unfair dismissal after resigning from Kitcraft Kitchens Pty Ltd on 29 January 2026. The employer objected that the Fair Work Commission lacked jurisdiction because Mr Maletti was not dismissed. The FWC upheld the jurisdictional objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

An employee relying on acceptance of repudiation to establish dismissal must elect to treat the repudiation as ending the contract and must not have affirmed it by continuing the employment.

Secondly, that the Applicant had elected to treat the repudiation as bringing the contract to an end. Thirdly and finally, that the Applicant had not affirmed the contract and continued it.

Mr Peter Deane v Hawkesbury Pools Pty Ltd

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4093Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dismissed Mr Deane’s unfair dismissal application against Hawkesbury Pools Pty Ltd ([2026] FWC 4093). The employer established it was a small business and that the summary dismissal for establishing and advertising a competing business was consistent with the Small Business Fair Dismissal Code. Because Code consistency meant the dismissal could not be unfair, the Commission did not determine the merits.
Key holding

For a summary dismissal under the Small Business Fair Dismissal Code, the Commission determines whether the employer held a belief that the employee’s conduct was sufficiently serious to justify immediate dismissal and whether that belief was based on reasonable grounds, not whether the dismissal was valid or warranted.

First, there needs to be a consideration whether, at the time of dismissal, the employer held a belief that the employee's conduct was sufficiently serious to justify immediate dismissal. Secondly it is necessary to consider whether that belief was based on reasonable grounds.

Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd v Ali Hamza

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4164Employment & workplace
Uber sought a stay under s 606 of the Fair Work Act 2009 of a Fair Work Commission order requiring it to reactivate Mr Ali Hamza’s access to its digital platform pending appeal. At first instance the Commissioner found moderate speeding was not a valid reason for deactivation and that Uber had not complied with the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code. Deputy President Easton refused the stay, finding no reasonable prospects on the merits and no balance of convenience favouring it.
Key holding

A stay under s 606 requires an arguable case with reasonable prospects on both permission to appeal and the substantive merits, together with a balance of convenience favouring the stay.

In determining whether to grant a stay application the Commission must be satisfied that there is an arguable case, with some reasonable prospect of success, in respect of both the question of leave to appeal and the substantive merits of the appeal. In addition, the balance of convenience must weigh in favour of the order subject to appeal being stayed.

Daniel Cowell v Brief Access Pty Ltd

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4165Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dealt with an unfair dismissal application by Daniel Cowell against Brief Access Pty Ltd (the named respondent; the extract also refers to Brief Labour Co Pty Ltd). The Commission rejected the employer’s jurisdictional objection that it was a small business entitled to rely on the Small Business Fair Dismissal Code, found the dismissal unfair, declined reinstatement and ordered compensation. The extract does not reproduce the final compensation calculation or full orders.
Key holding

To rely on summary dismissal under the Small Business Fair Dismissal Code, an employer’s belief that conduct is sufficiently serious must be based on reasonable grounds, ordinarily including discussion with the employee and regard to their explanation.

Normally in order to hold a belief on reasonable grounds it will be necessary to have a discussion with the employee about the perceived serious misconduct and pay regard to the explanations and views given by the employee.

Legal words, explained

Medium neutral citation (e.g. [2026] NSWSC 1234)
The standard reference number Australian courts give each decision: year, court code, and sequence number. It identifies the decision even before it appears in a law report.
Court / tribunal
Courts decide disputes between parties and can hear appeals. Tribunals (like the ART) re-examine government decisions on their merits, in a quicker, less formal process.
Judicial review
A court checking whether a government or tribunal decision was legally made. The court looks at legal errors, not whether it agrees with the outcome.
Merits review
A tribunal (usually the Administrative Review Tribunal) re-making a government decision from scratch, considering all the facts again.
Visa cancellation (s 501)
Under section 501 of the Migration Act 1958, a visa can be refused or cancelled on 'character' grounds, often because of a criminal record. Many tribunal cases on this site are appeals against such cancellations.
Set aside / affirmed
When a tribunal or appeal court 'sets aside' a decision, it is cancelled and usually replaced. 'Affirmed' means the original decision stands.
Applicant / respondent
The applicant is the person who brought the case; the respondent is the party responding to it (in tribunal matters, often a government minister or agency).
