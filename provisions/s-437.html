October 2026 (1)

Application by Transport Workers' Union of Australia (179V)

19 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4051Employment & workplace
Deputy President Hampton determined a s.437 protected action ballot order application by the TWU for Cleanaway employees covered by three regional Victorian waste enterprise agreements. The employer objected to scope and ballot questions 5, 9 and 10, including safety-related wording; the Commission rejected the continuing objections and granted the application, with leave to amend some questions. The ballot will be run by Fair Vote Services Pty Ltd and close on 5 November 2026.
Key holding

Under s.443(1) and (3)(d), where a valid s.437 application proposes clear ballot questions, the Commission must make a PABO and has no general discretion to exclude valid questions or amend them merely due to employer objections to wording.

The inference to be drawn from the mandatory nature of s 443(1) is that the order required to be made is one which gives effect to an application validly made under s 437. Thus, in respect of s 443(3)(d), we do not consider that the Commission has a general discretion to determine the questions which will be included in the order, or to simply exclude valid questions, independent of what has been applied for.

Construction, Forestry and Maritime Employees Union v Etex Australia Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 662 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 662Employment & workplace
The CFMEU applied under s.437 of the Fair Work Act 2009 for a protected action ballot order covering employees of Etex Australia Pty Ltd. Etex did not oppose the application generally but objected that eight proposed ballot questions were ambiguous or unclear, including in relation to safety matters. The Commission rejected those objections and issued the order.
Key holding

On a s.437 application, the Commission need not refuse a protected action ballot order merely because ballot questions are ambiguous or lack clarity; the questions need only describe the proposed industrial action so employees are capable of responding, with ambiguity potentially affecting later protection.

Moving now to the construction of s.437 itself, seen in its statutory context, all that the section requires is that the questions should describe the industrial action in such a way that employees are capable of responding to them. If the questions are ambiguous or lack clarity there may be consequences for the bargaining representative and the employees if reliance is placed on the result of the ballot in taking industrial action.
