Rebecca Ladner v Department of Home Affairs

16 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4029Employment & workplace
Ms Rebecca Ladner applied under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair dismissal remedy against the Department of Home Affairs, 336 days outside the 21-day time limit. The Fair Work Commission considered whether to extend time under s 394(2)–(3). It found no exceptional circumstances and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A very lengthy delay weighs strongly against exceptional circumstances, particularly where the applicant had already filed an unfair dismissal application in time but discontinued it to pursue other options.

Having made findings on the factors in s 394(3) and considering them together, I am not satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances. I have given particular weight to the length of the delay, and the fact that Ms Ladner had filed an unfair dismissal application within time, but chose to discontinue it in favour of other options.

Mr Samuel Anderson v Calrossy Anglican School

15 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3962Employment & workplace
Reverend Samuel Anderson applied for an unfair dismissal remedy after his resignation from Calrossy Anglican School took effect on 26 June 2026, but filed on 21 August 2026, 35 days late. Commissioner McKinnon considered whether to extend time under s 394(2) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The Commission refused an extension and dismissed the application.
Key holding

An out-of-time unfair dismissal application can be extended only where the Commission is satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist under s 394(2).

An extension of time to apply to the Commission for an unfair dismissal remedy can only be allowed in exceptional circumstances.

Mr Joshua Barbagallo v Bentrol Pty Ltd

8 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3853Employment & workplace
Mr Barbagallo applied for an unfair dismissal remedy under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) on 10 July 2026. The Commission had to determine when his dismissal took effect and, if the application was late, whether exceptional circumstances warranted an extension. The Commission found the dismissal took effect on 10 June 2026, the application was 9 days late, and no exceptional circumstances were established, so the application was dismissed.
Key holding

Under s 394(2) and s 394(3), if an unfair dismissal application is made more than 21 days after dismissal takes effect and the Commission is not satisfied there are exceptional circumstances, it cannot allow further time and must dismiss the application.

As the application was made more than 21 days after the dismissal took effect and I am not satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances, I cannot allow Mr Barbagallo further time. I dismiss his application for an unfair dismissal remedy.

Alexander Burton v SSAA Pty Ltd (St Marys Indoor Shooting Centre) [2026] FWC 560 (23 February 2026)

23 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 560Employment & workplace
Alexander Burton applied for an unfair dismissal remedy against SSAA Pty Ltd after being removed from casual rostering. The Commission found Mr Burton was dismissed on 21 October 2025 when SSAA first clearly told him his employment had ceased. Although the application was 21 days late, Commissioner McKinnon granted an extension of time because exceptional circumstances existed.
Key holding

Exceptional circumstances under s.394(2) may be established by a combination of factors which, viewed together, produce a situation out of the ordinary, unusual, special or uncommon.

The ordinary and natural meaning of "exceptional circumstances" includes a combination of factors which, when viewed together, may reasonably be seen as producing a situation which is out of the ordinary course, unusual, special or uncommon.
