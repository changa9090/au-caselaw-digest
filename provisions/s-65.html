Amanpreet Singh Bhullar v Transit Systems (Victoria) Pty Ltd

14 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3029Employment & workplace
Mr Bhullar, a Melbourne bus driver employed by Transit Systems (Victoria) Pty Ltd, applied under s.65B of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after his employer refused a "flexible work" request that was in substance a request to be transferred to Torrens Transit Pty Ltd in Adelaide, an associated Kelsian Group company. The Commission held that a s.65 request can only concern a change in working arrangements with the employee's own employer, found the two companies were separate legal employers, and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A request for flexible working arrangements under s.65 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) is confined to changes in working arrangements with the applicant's own employer, and a request to move to a separate (even associated) corporate employer is outside the Commission's jurisdiction under s.65B.

That much is clear from the various references to the "employer", and it is evident, given that context and purpose of the provision, that this must be the relevant applicant's employer.

July 2026 (1)

2104609 (Refugee)

23 Jul 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2306Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
In 2104609 (Refugee) [2026] ARTA 2306, the Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal of protection visas to Indian nationals in an interfaith/inter-caste marriage who claimed family and honour-based violence in Punjab. The delegate refused under s 65 because the applicants were not refugees and did not meet complementary protection. The Tribunal set aside the refusal and remitted the matter with a direction that the applicants meet s 36(2)(aa) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth).
Key holding

A protection visa may be granted on complementary protection grounds where the refugee criterion is not met, if removal creates a real risk of significant harm as a necessary and foreseeable consequence.

The issues in this case are whether the applicants have a well-founded fear of being persecuted in India for one or more of the five reasons set out, and, if not, whether there are substantial grounds for believing that, as a necessary and foreseeable consequence of the applicant being removed from Australia to India there is a real risk that they will suffer significant harm.

May 2026 (1)

2543646 (Refugee)

13 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2412Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
In 2543646 (Refugee) [2026] ARTA 2412, the Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s refusal under s 65 of protection visas for an Indian Sikh man, his wife and child. The main applicant claimed fear based on support for Khalistan and association with [Mr A], who had a dispute with political opponents. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding no real chance of persecution or real risk of significant harm.
Key holding

For complementary protection, a real risk of significant harm is assessed using the same standard as a real chance of persecution.

A 'real risk' and 'real chance' involve the same standard.

March 2026 (1)

Kellie Fitzpatrick v University of New South Wales [2026] FWC 677 (2 March 2026)

2 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 677Employment & workplace
Ms Kellie Fitzpatrick referred a dispute to the Fair Work Commission under s.65B(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after UNSW did not accept her request to change her existing hybrid part-time arrangement to work exclusively from home for three days each week. The Commission found the request was not made “because of” her eligible parent circumstance and dismissed the application. UNSW therefore had no responsive obligations under s.65A.
Key holding

Under s.65, a request for flexible working arrangements is validly made only if there is a causal connection between the employee’s eligible circumstance and the requested change.

For these reasons, I find no causal connection between the circumstance of Ms Fitzpatrick as a parent of a young child and her request to work full time from home, whether for a finite or indefinite period, to support a move interstate. The request made on 3 September 2025 was not validly made under s.65 of the Act.

October 2025 (1)

Karlene Chandler v Westpac Banking Corporation

20 Oct 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 3115Employment & workplace
Ms Chandler requested a flexible working arrangement under s.65 of the Fair Work Act to work remotely from home to care for her six-year-old children. Westpac refused on reasonable business grounds, and the Commission heard the dispute under s.65B. The Commission rejected Westpac's argument that any order would be inconsistent with clause 19 of the Westpac Agreement, and proposed to order that Westpac grant the flexible work request.
