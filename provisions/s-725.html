Mrs Carolyn Monks v The Hobart Clinic Association Limited Trading AS The Hobart Clinic

30 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission (Full Bench)[2026] FWCFB 288Employment & workplace
Mrs Monks lodged a s 365 general protections application after already making an unfair dismissal application that remained on foot. The Full Bench refused permission to appeal from the Deputy President’s decision dismissing the s 365 application as contrary to s 725 of the Fair Work Act 2009.
Key holding

A s 365 general protections application is barred by s 725 where an earlier unfair dismissal application in relation to the same dismissal has not been withdrawn and has not failed for want of jurisdiction or genuine redundancy.

Even within the confines of an application for permission to appeal, which is concerned to identify arguable error and not to determine the merits of the appeal, it is clear that Mrs Monks' general protections claim was barred by s 725.

Ms Vikki Turner v Ithaca Crystals Pty Ltd

21 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4080Employment & workplace
Ms Turner lodged an unfair dismissal application under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Her former employer asserted she was not protected because it was a small business and she had not completed the minimum employment period. Ms Turner sought to amend to a general protections claim under s 586 (with an alternative extension of time under s 366(2)); the Commission held the application was in substance a general protections claim filed on the wrong form and allowed the amendment.
Key holding

Although s 586 cannot be used merely to substitute a general protections cause of action for an unfair dismissal claim so as to avoid ss 725/729, the Commission may waive the irregularity and permit amendment where the initiating application was in substance a general protections claim made on the wrong approved form.

Though there is no power to amend an application to substitute a general protections cause of action for an unfair dismissal one, that does not mean that the form in which the application is made is determinative. If a party simply uses the wrong form to make one type of claim or the other, that is an irregularity that can be corrected or waived pursuant to s 586 of the Fair Work Act: Hambridge v Spotless Facilities Services Pty Ltd

Ricky Connors v Ablewood Pty Ltd

20 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4074Employment & workplace
Mr Connors filed both an unfair dismissal application and a general protections application involving dismissal about the same dismissal. The Commission held that the second application was barred by s 725 and dismissed it under s 587(1)(a). This is a short procedural decision about the strict “one application” rule.
Key holding

A general protections dismissal application cannot be made while an earlier unfair dismissal application about the same dismissal remains on foot and has not been withdrawn or failed for want of jurisdiction.

When the general protections application involving dismissal was made on 19 September 2026, Mr Connors's unfair dismissal application had not been withdrawn or failed for want of jurisdiction. Mr Connors was, therefore, prevented by section 725 from making a general protections application involving dismissal.

Dorothy Lazenkas v Mondelez Australia Pty Ltd

16 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission (Full Bench)[2026] FWCFB 280Employment & workplace
Ms Lazenkas made an unfair dismissal application, then later filed a general protections application in which she stated she withdrew the earlier unfair dismissal claim. The Full Bench considered whether s 725 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) barred the general protections application because the earlier application had not been withdrawn before the second application was made. By majority, the appeal was dismissed, with Vice President Gibian dissenting.
Key holding

Under the Fair Work Act, if an unfair dismissal application has been made, it must have been withdrawn before a subsequent s 365 general protections application is made; simultaneous withdrawal in the later application does not disengage s 725.

We do not consider that the language and structure of ss 725 and 729(1)(b)(i) can accommodate the simultaneous making of a second prescribed application and a withdrawal of a first. The withdrawal must precede the second application.

Ms Mary Muzorewa v Eldercare Pty Ltd

13 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3623Employment & workplace
Ms Muzorewa lodged a s.365 general protections dismissal application against Eldercare 297 days after the 21-day limit. The Commission considered whether to extend time under s.366(2) but found no exceptional circumstances, largely because her reliance on ANMF/administrative processes did not reasonably explain the delay. The application was dismissed; the s.725 statute-bar and settlement-release objections were noted but not finally determined.
Key holding

The Commission may extend time for a s.365 general protections dismissal application under s.366(2) only if satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist, taking into account the statutory factors.

Section 366(2) of the Act provides the Commission with discretion to extend the time for lodgement beyond the 21 -day period where it is satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist to warrant that action. In considering whether exceptional circumstances exist for this purpose, I am required to take into account the considerations outlined in paragraphs (a) to (e) of s.366(2) of the Act.

Ms Ashleigh Llewellyn v Sexyland Australia Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1302 (17 April 2026)

17 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1302Employment & workplace
Ms Llewellyn made an unfair dismissal application on 27 August 2025 and then a general protections dismissal application on 2 September 2025. The Commission found the later general protections application was barred by s.725 because the earlier unfair dismissal application had not been withdrawn or failed on specified grounds, and dismissed it under s.587(1)(a).
Key holding

Section 725 prevents an applicant from making a general protections dismissal application while a prior unfair dismissal application in relation to the dismissal remains on foot, and such later application may be dismissed under s.587(1)(a).

As at 2 September 2025 when the general protections application was made, Ms Llewellyn's unfair dismissal application had not been withdrawn, failed for want of jurisdiction, or failed because the Commission found the dismissal to be a genuine redundancy. Ms Llewellyn was, therefore, prevented by section 725 from making a general protections involving dismissal application.

Agustina Rodriguez v Elena Stevens; Michael George Stevens [2026] FWC 550 (20 February 2026)

20 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 550Employment & workplace
Ms Rodriguez filed a general protections application four days out of time after the Commission advised she withdraw earlier unfair dismissal and general protections applications because s.725 barred two applications about the same dismissal. The Fair Work Commission accepted the circumstances were exceptional and granted an extension of time. The substantive general protections dispute can now proceed.
Key holding

The Commission may grant an extension of time for a late general protections application where the delay is caused by the need to withdraw overlapping applications under s.725, because those circumstances can be exceptional.

The Commission may exercise its discretion to allow a further period for a general protections application to be made if it is satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances. I consider the circumstances set out above to be exceptional circumstances that warrant an extension of time.
