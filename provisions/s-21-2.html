Kola v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

22 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1550Migration: court challengeMigration
The Federal Court dismissed Mr Kola’s challenge to an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming refusal of citizenship by conferral under s 21(2)(h) of the Australian Citizenship Act 2007 (Cth). The Court upheld the Tribunal’s finding that his criminal history outweighed remorse, rehabilitation evidence and a low risk of reoffending.
Key holding

A low risk of future offending does not by itself establish that a person is presently of good character under s 21(2)(h).

An assessment that a person presents a low risk of future offending is not equivalent to a conclusion that the person is presently of good character within the meaning of s 21(2)(h).

Huseni and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Citizenship)

13 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2420Tribunal reviewVisa nominationCharacter testMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of Australian citizenship by conferral on good character grounds under s 21(2)(h) of the Australian Citizenship Act 2007 (Cth). The Minister alleged the applicant failed to declare alternative names and provided undeclared migration assistance through his company, Immi Solution. The Tribunal found no dishonest intent and no proven personal provision of immigration assistance, and was satisfied the applicant was of good character.
Key holding

For s 21(2)(h), good character requires a holistic assessment of enduring moral qualities and is not negated by every acknowledged mistake or oversight, particularly where there is no fraud or dishonesty.

His actions, when considered on an individual or cumulative basis, are regrettable but do not reach the threshold of being demonstrative of someone who is presently not of good character.

October 2024 (1)

Kamara and Minister for Immigration, Citizenship and Multicultural Affairs (Citizenship)

8 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3560Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
The Applicant sought review of a delegate’s decision refusing his citizenship by conferral application because the delegate was not satisfied he was of good character under s 21(2)(h) of the Australian Citizenship Act 2007 (Cth). The Tribunal found the Applicant was of good character despite historical offending, a 2016 drink-driving offence, traffic infringements and disclosure issues. It remitted the matter with a direction that he satisfies s 21(2)(h).
Key holding

The good character criterion in s 21(2)(h) requires a broad moral assessment against the moral norms of the Australian community, rather than a purely conviction-based test.

The character test is ultimately a moral test, and morality can only be tested within the confines of moral norms in society.

XMNF and Minister for Immigration, Citizenship and Multicultural Affairs (Citizenship)

11 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3248Tribunal reviewCharacter testMigration
XMNF, a 51-year-old Afghan citizen, sought review of a refusal of Australian citizenship by conferral. The refusal was based on the good character requirement after he was found guilty of three counts of unlawful assault in a family violence context and failed to disclose the offending in his citizenship application. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding it was not satisfied XMNF was of good character at the time of its decision.
Key holding

In assessing good character under s 21(2)(h), the Tribunal may consider judicial findings of guilt without recorded convictions and the underlying conduct, absent a preclusion such as that in Lesianawai or Thornton.

The Tribunal sees no reason to exclude consideration of the judicial findings against the Applicant, irrespective of whether convictions were recorded.
