EZS21 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

12 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1986Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The applicant sought an extension of time under s 477(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to seek judicial review of a Tribunal decision that it lacked jurisdiction because his review application was late. The Court found the delegate’s decision was validly renotified by email on 30 May 2020 under s 66 and s 494C, so the 28-day review period expired on 26 June 2020.
Key holding

An extension of time under s 477(2) is not in the interests of the administration of justice where the proposed judicial review grounds disclose no arguable case of jurisdictional error on the part of the Tribunal.

The lack of any arguable case of jurisdictional error on the part of the Tribunal, judged at an impressionistic level, is such that it is not in the interests of the administration of justice for the Court to grant the applicant an extension of time in which to seek judicial review of the Tribunal's decision.

EZK19 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

12 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1901Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was an application for an extension of time under s 477(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to seek judicial review of a 2017 Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a protection visa. The Court refused the extension because the applicant delayed for 914 days, gave no adequate evidenced explanation, and the proposed judicial review grounds did not disclose an arguable jurisdictional error.
Key holding

An extension of time under s 477(2) may be refused where there is substantial delay, no adequately evidenced explanation for it, and no reasonably arguable ground of review.

The combination of a substantial delay, the absence of an adequate explanation for that delay supported by credible evidence, and the lack of a reasonably arguable ground of review weighs decisively against an extension of time.

Parvez v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

9 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1997Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
Parvez sought an extension of time to seek judicial review of an Administrative Appeals Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Subclass 500 student visa because he was not enrolled in a registered course at the time of decision. The Federal Circuit and Family Court refused the extension, finding no reasonably arguable jurisdictional error in the Tribunal’s approach to cl 500.211, adjournment, or procedural fairness. Costs were awarded to the Minister.
Key holding

The Court may refuse an extension under s 477(2) where the proposed judicial review grounds lack merit, even if the delay is relatively short and there is no prejudice to the Minister.

Taking all of the circumstances into account, and giving particular weight to the lack of merit in the proposed judicial review application, I am not satisfied that an extension of time is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice within the meaning of s 477(2) of the Act.

BKB23 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

9 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1980Migration: court challengeMigration
This case concerned an application for an extension of time under s 477(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to seek judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a protection visa. The Court refused the extension because the delay was years out of time, the explanation was unsatisfactory, and the proposed review grounds had no arguable merit.
Key holding

An extension of time under s 477(2) should be refused where the proposed judicial review grounds have no arguable prospect of success, even if the delay is otherwise satisfactorily explained.

It follows that the applicant has no arguable case for setting aside the Tribunal's decision. On this basis, I would have declined to have extended time in the circumstances of this matter even if I had considered that the delay had been satisfactorily explained.

Maamari v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

An extension of time under s 477(2) may be refused despite a short delay and no prejudice where there is no reasonable explanation for the delay and the proposed judicial review ground lacks sufficient prospects.

Taking into account the length of the delay, the lack of a reasonable excuse for the delay and the limited prospects of success of ground of review, I am satisfied that an extension of time in this case is not in the interests of the administration of justice.

ANF26 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

2 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1949Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicant, a Malaysian citizen who claimed protection because he is gay, sought judicial review after the Administrative Review Tribunal affirmed refusal of his protection visa. He filed 17 days outside the 35-day period and needed an extension under s 477(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Court refused the extension because the proposed judicial review application lacked sufficient merit, notwithstanding the relatively short delay and absence of specific prejudice.
Key holding

On a s 477(2) extension application, an underlying judicial review application lacking merit can decisively weigh against an extension even where the delay is short and no specific prejudice is shown.

When I weigh the considerations, I am unable to be satisfied it is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice to extend the time for making the application for review. The absence of merit in the underlying application weighs decisively against granting an extension of time.

BQT23 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

30 Sep 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1909Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
BQT23 sought judicial review of an Administrative Appeals Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a protection visa, filing 2,785 days out of time. The Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia refused an extension of time under s 477 of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) and dismissed the application with costs.
Key holding

Under s 477(2)(b), an extension of time is granted only if the Court is satisfied that it is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice.

The second limb of s 477(2)(b) of the Act allows the Court to grant an extension of time for the filing of an application if the Court is satisfied that it is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice to make such an order.
