October 2024 (1)

2410235 (Migration)

7 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4272Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Michael Ison
The Tribunal affirmed cancellation of an Albanian national’s Subclass 010 (Bridging A) visa under s 116(1)(g) and reg 2.43(1)(oa) because he had convictions for cannabis cultivation in 2018 and 2021. Although cancellation would cause family and psychological hardship, the Tribunal gave determinative weight to adverse credibility findings about his explanations for the offending and the absence of extenuating circumstances. He continued to hold a Bridging E visa permitting work, so cancellation did not require departure.
Key holding

Where a prescribed cancellation ground is established but cancellation is not mandatory, the Tribunal must still determine whether the visa should be cancelled in the exercise of its discretion.

For these reasons, the Tribunal is satisfied that the ground for cancellation in s 116(1)(g) and reg 2.43(1)(oa) exists. As that ground does not require mandatory cancellation under s 116(3), the Tribunal must proceed to consider whether the visa should be cancelled.

September 2024 (1)

Alvindo (Migration)

9 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3378Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration
Before: David McCulloch
Alvindo concerns review of a delegate’s decision to cancel Mr Alvindo’s Subclass 500 (Student) visa after he was convicted of knowingly possessing child abuse material and two optical surveillance device offences. The Tribunal found the conviction ground was made out but affirmed cancellation after weighing the serious privacy offences against his study record, remorse and hardship. It is a discretionary migration cancellation decision.
Key holding

A conviction for an offence against a Commonwealth, State or Territory law is a prescribed ground for cancellation under s 116(1)(g), and where s 116(3) does not mandate cancellation the Tribunal must exercise the cancellation discretion.

For these reasons, the Tribunal is satisfied that the ground for cancellation in s 116(1)(g) exists. As that ground does not require mandatory cancellation under s 116(3), the Tribunal must proceed to consider whether the visa should be cancelled.

August 2024 (2)

Terrones Tafur (Migration)

31 Aug 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3967Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration
Before: Christine Kannis
The Tribunal reviewed cancellation of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa because the first applicant was not enrolled in a full-time registered course from 20 January 2022 to 5 January 2023. It affirmed cancellation, finding breach of condition 8202(2)(a) and no sufficient discretionary reasons against cancellation. It also held it had no jurisdiction over the secondary applicants, whose visas were automatically cancelled under s 140(1).
Key holding

Once a breach of condition 8202 grounds cancellation under s 116(1)(b) and cancellation is not mandatory under s 116(3), the Tribunal must proceed to exercise the discretion whether to cancel.

For these reasons, the Tribunal is satisfied that the ground for cancellation in s 116(1)(b) exists. As that ground does not require mandatory cancellation under s 116(3), the Tribunal must proceed to consider whether to exercise its discretion to cancel the visa.

Movono (Migration)

31 Aug 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3916Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration
Before: Christine Kannis
Miss Make Liku Movono held a Subclass 500 (Student) visa. A delegate cancelled it because she was not enrolled in a full-time registered course from 9 August 2021, breaching condition 8202(2)(a). The Tribunal reviewed the cancellation and affirmed it, finding significant non-enrolment and no extenuating or compassionate circumstances.
Key holding

A breach of student visa condition 8202(2)(a) by failing to be enrolled in a full-time registered course establishes the discretionary cancellation ground under s 116(1)(b), which is not mandatory under s 116(3) and therefore requires consideration of the discretion to cancel.

For these reasons, the Tribunal is satisfied that the ground for cancellation in s 116(1)(b) exists. As that ground does not require mandatory cancellation under s 116(3), the Tribunal must proceed to consider whether to exercise its discretion to cancel the visa.
