EnerMech advised it had no objection. The Fair Work Commission granted a further 30-day extension, operating from 18 March 2026.
Key holding

Under s.459(3), the FWC may accept a late application to extend the 30-day protected action authorisation period, but may grant only one extension of up to 30 days, for a maximum of 60 days from the ballot result declaration.

The Commission may accept and consider an application made under s.459(3) at that time. The Full Federal Court in Energy Australia Yallourn Pty Ltd v Construction, Forestry, Mining and Energy Union [1] confirmed that this was permissible; [2] however the Commission may only provide a single extension of the original 30 day period for a further period of up to 30 days.

v Crown Equipment Pty Limited [2026] FWC 704 (3 March 2026)

3 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 704Employment & workplace
The AWU applied under s.459(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to extend by 30 days the period for protected industrial action authorised by a protected action ballot declared on 27 January 2026. Crown Equipment objected to the application because the lodgement document wrongly stated it was made within the initial 30-day period. The Commission amended the application and granted the extension.
Key holding

The Commission may accept a s.459(3) extension application after the initial 30-day period expires, but can grant only one extension of up to 30 days, so protected action cannot be authorised beyond 60 days from declaration and gap-period action may be unprotected.

The Full Federal Court in Energy Australia Yallourn Pty Ltd v Construction, Forestry, Mining and Energy Union [1] confirmed that this was permissible; [2] however, the Commission may only provide a single extension of the original 30 day period for a further period of up to 30 days. [3] That is, a total of 60 days from the declaration of the ballot results.
