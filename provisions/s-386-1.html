Ms Karen McClear v A Team Tuition Australia Pty Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4174Employment & workplace
Ms McClear resigned after a lengthy suspension pending an unresolved misconduct investigation and brought a s 365 general protections claim alleging she was forced to resign. A Team Tuition objected that she was not dismissed within s 386. The Commission dismissed the application, finding she was not forced to resign because of the employer’s conduct and was therefore not dismissed.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s 386(1)(b) only where an objective assessment of the employer’s conduct shows it made resignation the probable result or left the employee with no effective or real choice but to resign.

In making that assessment, it is appropriate to make an objective analysis of the Respondent's conduct to determine whether it was of such a nature that resignation was the probable result or that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

Mr Johnathon Burr v The Trustee for Cornerstone Labour Hire Unit Trust

22 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4121Employment & workplace
Mr Burr’s host assignment with CEVA Logistics ended and he brought an unfair dismissal application against his labour-hire employer, Cornerstone. The Commission had to decide whether Cornerstone dismissed him or whether only his host placement ended; because Cornerstone kept him on its books and sought alternative work, the application was dismissed.
Key holding

Where a labour-hire employee’s host placement ends but the labour-hire employer remains ready and actively attempts to redeploy the employee, the employment has not been terminated on the employer’s initiative and there is no dismissal under s 386(1).

Under s 386(1), a person is considered dismissed if their employment with the employer has been terminated on the employer's initiative. It follows that if the employment continues or if the employer is actively seeking to find alternative work for the employee, there has been no dismissal.

Yuanyuan (Denise) Wang v Hk Calibration Technologies Pty Ltd

Mark Shorrock made a s.365 general protections application against Cinc Switchboards Pty Ltd, alleging constructive dismissal. The Commission determined a jurisdictional objection that he had resigned voluntarily and was not dismissed. It upheld the objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s.386(1)(b) only where the employer’s conduct or course of conduct was intended to bring about the resignation or had the probable result that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign, with employer conduct being essential.

To satisfy the test for constructive dismissal requires a substantive and demonstratable course of conduct aimed at bringing about the end of the employment. This action is evident in the actions of the Employer, and the intended result of the conduct to bring about the Applicant's resignation; or that that would be the probable result.

Kibreab Teklu v Liferaft System Australia [2026] FWC 668 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 668Employment & workplace
Mr Teklu claimed he was unfairly dismissed after leaving Liferaft System Australia on 8 July 2025 and not returning to work. LSA disputed the dismissal, variously saying he was dismissed for misconduct, resigned, or was not dismissed. The Fair Work Commission found he was not dismissed at LSA’s initiative, upheld the jurisdictional objection, and dismissed the application.
Key holding

Before an unfair dismissal claim can proceed, the employee must establish a dismissal under s.386(1); where the evidence shows voluntary departure rather than employer-initiated termination or forced resignation, there is no dismissal.

I consider the evidence does not support a finding that Mr Teklu was dismissed on the initiative of the employer within the meaning of s.386 of the Act.

26 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 636Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission considered a jurisdictional objection to a s.365 general protections application brought by a former HR manager who alleged she was constructively dismissed. The Commission upheld the objection, finding the Applicant had not been dismissed within s.386 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The decision turned on whether the Employer’s conduct forced her resignation.
Key holding

For a s.386(1)(b) forced-resignation dismissal, the employer’s conduct must be intended to bring employment to an end, or be of such a nature that termination was the probable result and the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

A resignation that is "forced" by conduct or a course of conduct on the part of the employer will be a dismissal within the second limb of the definition in s.386(1)(b). The test to be applied here is whether the employer engaged in the conduct with the intention of bringing the employment to an end or whether termination of the employment was the probably result of the employer's conduct such that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.
