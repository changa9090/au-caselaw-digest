Mr Johnathon Burr v The Trustee for Cornerstone Labour Hire Unit Trust

22 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4121Employment & workplace
Mr Burr’s host assignment with CEVA Logistics ended and he brought an unfair dismissal application against his labour-hire employer, Cornerstone. The Commission had to decide whether Cornerstone dismissed him or whether only his host placement ended; because Cornerstone kept him on its books and sought alternative work, the application was dismissed.
Key holding

Where a labour-hire employee’s host placement ends but the labour-hire employer remains ready and actively attempts to redeploy the employee, the employment has not been terminated on the employer’s initiative and there is no dismissal under s 386(1).

Under s 386(1), a person is considered dismissed if their employment with the employer has been terminated on the employer's initiative. It follows that if the employment continues or if the employer is actively seeking to find alternative work for the employee, there has been no dismissal.

Yuanyuan (Denise) Wang v Hk Calibration Technologies Pty Ltd

21 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3891Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission determined a s.365 application by Ms Yuanyuan (Denise) Wang against HK Calibration Technologies Pty Ltd. It upheld the employer’s jurisdictional objection, finding Ms Wang resigned but was not “dismissed” under s.386(1)(b) of the Fair Work Act 2009 because the employer’s conduct was not intended to end employment and did not leave her with no effective or real choice but to resign. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

For a resignation to be a dismissal under s.386(1)(b), the employer’s conduct must be intended to bring employment to an end or make resignation the probable result, leaving the employee no effective or real choice but to resign.

The test to be applied here is whether the employer engaged in the conduct with the intention of bringing the employment to an end or whether termination of the employment was the probably result of the employer's conduct such that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

Mr Mark Shorrock v Cinc Switchboards Pty Ltd

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3911Employment & workplace
Mark Shorrock made a s.365 general protections application against Cinc Switchboards Pty Ltd, alleging constructive dismissal. The Commission determined a jurisdictional objection that he had resigned voluntarily and was not dismissed. It upheld the objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s.386(1)(b) only where the employer’s conduct or course of conduct was intended to bring about the resignation or had the probable result that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign, with employer conduct being essential.

To satisfy the test for constructive dismissal requires a substantive and demonstratable course of conduct aimed at bringing about the end of the employment. This action is evident in the actions of the Employer, and the intended result of the conduct to bring about the Applicant's resignation; or that that would be the probable result.

Ms Alex Culhane v Psychsense Therapeutic Services Pty Ltd & Chantelle Chapman

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3893Employment & workplace
Ms Culhane applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), alleging dismissal by Psychsense Therapeutic Service Pty Ltd and Ms Chapman. The Commission had to determine a jurisdictional objection that she had not been dismissed because she resigned. Deputy President Clancy found there was no dismissal and dismissed the application.
Key holding

For a s.365 application at the material time, the Commission lacks jurisdiction unless the applicant was actually dismissed under s.386(1), meaning termination on the employer’s initiative or a resignation forced by employer conduct.

I have concluded that in this case there was no dismissal within the meaning of s.386 of the Act because I am satisfied there was no termination of the Applicant's employment on the Respondent's initiative and nor have I been persuaded that the Applicant was forced to resign because of conduct or a course of conduct engaged in by the Respondent .

Jessie Perrin v Yarra City Council [2026] FWC 1133 (1 April 2026)

1 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1133Employment & workplace
Jessie Perrin, a casual aqua instructor, applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) alleging Yarra City Council dismissed her by removing her from her regular Friday shift after she sought payment for set-up time. The Council objected that there was no dismissal and she remained a casual employee. The Commission found she had been dismissed at the Council’s initiative and dismissed the jurisdictional objection.
Key holding

Removing a casual employee from their regular continuing roster and replacing them can constitute termination at the employer’s initiative under s.386(1)(a), even without an express dismissal.

Objectively viewed, in circumstances where Ms Perrin had worked a regular Friday shift since early 2022, in being removed from these shifts and replaced on the roster with another member of staff, it is reasonably apparent the Respondent did not intend to offer her any future work and that her employment was being brought to an end.

Mr Timothy Stephen Ayres v Reed Mining Services Pty Ltd (in liquidation) [2026] FWC 826 (13 March 2026)

13 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 826Employment & workplace
Mr Ayres applied under s.394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), alleging unfair dismissal by Reed Mining Services Pty Ltd (in liquidation) after unpaid wages and a claimed forced resignation in September 2025. The Commission found his employment had ended on or about 29 January 2025 on the employer’s initiative, making the application out of time. Key holding

A persistent and unjustified failure by an employer to pay salary may repudiate the employment contract and can constitute termination on the employer’s initiative under s 386(1)(a).

Ms Izabela Dembowska v Abergeldie Personnel Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 801 (13 March 2026)

Key holding

A person is not dismissed under s.386(1)(a) where only a binding contract of employment is terminated before the employment relationship has commenced.

I conclude that the applicant's employment with her employer was not terminated on the employer's initiative because the applicant's employment had not commenced by 29 September 2025. The applicant was therefore not dismissed for the purposes of s.365.

Joanna Yi v Epec Group Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 749 (5 March 2026)

The Fair Work Commission rejected that objection, found Yi was dismissed under s.386, and ordered the matter to proceed to conference.
Key holding

Where an employer is on notice that an employee is resigning because of a mistaken belief about their role or need to relocate, accepting the resignation without clarifying the actual position may constitute termination on the employer’s initiative under s.386(1)(a).

I consider EPEC's acceptance of Ms Yi's resignation without further clarifying what it considered to be her actual position, and the need for her to relocate to Brisbane, to be a termination at the initiative of EPEC.

Brent Houston v AWX Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 702 (3 March 2026)

3 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 702Employment & workplace
Brent Houston brought a s 365 general protections application against labour-hire employer AWX Pty Ltd after his placement ended and AWX issued a separation certificate citing “shortage of work”. AWX objected that the Commission lacked jurisdiction because Mr Houston had not been dismissed. Deputy President Colman found the employment ended on the employer’s initiative, dismissed the objection, and issued a s 368 certificate while indicating no reasonable prospect of success under s 369 or in court.
Key holding

Where an employer issues a separation certificate citing shortage of work and does not confirm or record that a casual employee is resigning, the employment is treated as terminated on the employer’s initiative and the employee is dismissed under s 386(1)(a).

But if AWX believes the casual is resigning, it should confirm this and also state in the certificate that the employee is ceasing work voluntarily. In my view Mr Houston's employment was terminated on the employer's initiative, and he was therefore dismissed within the meaning of s 386(1)(a) of the Act.
