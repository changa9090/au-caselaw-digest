Ms Vikki Turner v Ithaca Crystals Pty Ltd

21 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4080Employment & workplace
Ms Turner lodged an unfair dismissal application under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Her former employer asserted she was not protected because it was a small business and she had not completed the minimum employment period. Ms Turner sought to amend to a general protections claim under s 586 (with an alternative extension of time under s 366(2)); the Commission held the application was in substance a general protections claim filed on the wrong form and allowed the amendment.
Key holding

Although s 586 cannot be used merely to substitute a general protections cause of action for an unfair dismissal claim so as to avoid ss 725/729, the Commission may waive the irregularity and permit amendment where the initiating application was in substance a general protections claim made on the wrong approved form.

Though there is no power to amend an application to substitute a general protections cause of action for an unfair dismissal one, that does not mean that the form in which the application is made is determinative. If a party simply uses the wrong form to make one type of claim or the other, that is an irregularity that can be corrected or waived pursuant to s 586 of the Fair Work Act: Hambridge v Spotless Facilities Services Pty Ltd

Mr Matthew Vickers v ASC Pty Ltd

16 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4039Employment & workplace
Mr Matthew Vickers made a general protections application under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after ASC Pty Ltd dismissed him on 23 April 2025. He filed on 22 October 2025, 161 days after the 21-day statutory limit expired. The Fair Work Commission refused an extension of time under s.366(2), finding no exceptional circumstances.
Key holding

An applicant seeking an extension under s.366(2) must provide credible reasons for the delay; absent credible reasons, exceptional circumstances will not be established.

The absence of credible reasons for the delay advanced by Mr Vickers for filing his claim weigh heavily against a finding that exceptional circumstances exist in this case.

Mr Taryq Mousley v Coles Supermarkets Australia Pty Ltd

15 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3854Employment & workplace
Mr Mousley applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) alleging Coles took adverse action by reducing his shifts and terminating him because of a workers’ compensation claim and injury. Coles said the dismissal took effect on 13 May 2026, making the 10 June 2026 application seven days late. The Fair Work Commission granted an extension of time, finding exceptional circumstances because Mr Mousley did not receive the termination letter and Mr Angelo failed to clarify his employment status after reasonable inquiries.
Key holding

Exceptional circumstances under s.366(2) may be established by a combination of the employee not receiving the termination letter, reasonably inquiring about dismissal status, and the employer failing to clarify the dismissal.

Having regard to all of the matters listed at s.366(2) of the FW Act, I am satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances. The combination of Mr Mousley not receiving a termination letter from Coles, seeking to clarify his employment status with Mr Angelo on 15 and 17 May 2026 and not receiving a proper response from Mr Angelo are matters which in my view are out of the ordinary course, unusual, special or uncommon.
