October 2026 (3)

Application by Abdul Samee Paracha

23 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4142Employment & workplace
Mr Paracha applied under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair deactivation remedy after his Uber driver account was deactivated on 25 September 2024. The Fair Work Commission amended the respondent name to Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd and dismissed the application because Mr Paracha was not a person protected from unfair deactivation. His pre-commencement platform work could not be counted, leaving only about one month of relevant service.
Key holding

Under s 536LD(c), a platform worker must have performed work on a regular basis for at least six months at the time of deactivation to be protected from unfair deactivation.

Because Mr Paracha has not performed work on a regular basis for a period of at least 6 months, he is not a person protected from unfair deactivation.

Application by Raham Shahzad

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3914Employment & workplaceContracts
Mr Shahzad applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after Amazon deactivated him from the Amazon Flex digital labour platform. Amazon objected that he was not protected from unfair deactivation because he had not performed work on the platform on a regular basis for at least six months. The Fair Work Commission dismissed the application.
Key holding

For s 536LD(c), the Commission’s inquiry is limited to whether the person actually performed work and whether that work was performed on a regular basis for at least six months before deactivation.

The relevant inquiry under s 536LD(c) of the Act is limited to, first, whether a person has been performing work and secondly, whether such work was performed on a regular basis for a period of at least 6 months.

Application by Md Arman Hossain Himel

7 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3846Employment & workplace
In [2026] FWC 3846, the Fair Work Commission dismissed Mr Himel’s unfair deactivation remedy application against Uber Eats. The Commission held that Mr Himel was not a person protected from unfair deactivation because he had not performed platform work on a regular basis for at least six months under s 536LD(c).
Key holding

Under s 536LD(c), “regular basis” requires a sufficiently frequent and predictable pattern of platform work over at least six months; infrequent and unpredictable work with many weeks of no work is not regular.

The infrequent and unpredictable manner that Mr Himel worked for Uber during that period, coupled with his pattern of not working at all on any days in many weeks, demonstrates that he was not performing work on a regular basis.

Application by Sanjula Ariyapperuma

22 Jun 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1761Employment & workplace
Ms Ariyapperuma, an Uber Eats driver, applied for an unfair deactivation remedy after her account was deactivated. The respondent objected that she did not perform work on a regular basis for at least six months as required by s 536LD(c). The Commission found that working each week over a nine-month unbroken period—except when study commitments or illness prevented it—constituted a regular pattern consistent with gig work, dismissed the first jurisdictional objection, and programmed the matter for the second objection and merits.

Application by Satbir Singh

9 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 409Employment & workplaceContracts
Satbir Singh applied for an unfair deactivation remedy after his access to the Uber delivery platform was deactivated on 9 May 2024. The Commission found he was not a person protected from unfair deactivation because the 6-month regular work period under s.536LD(c) could not include work performed before the Part 3A-3 commencement on 26 August 2024, and he performed no work after 9 May 2024. The application was therefore dismissed without considering the merits.
