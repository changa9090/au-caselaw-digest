Australian Securities and Investments Commission v Dawson

20 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1548Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
SXHR, a Sri Lankan unauthorised maritime arrival in immigration detention, sought judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Bridging E visa under s 501(1) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Tribunal had applied Direction No 110 and refused the visa notwithstanding the applicant’s outstanding Ministerial intervention request. The Federal Court dismissed the review.
Key holding

Direction 110 requires comparison of the risk attached to refusal and grant of the visa, but not separate assessment of every possible outcome after grant where only the grant creates a risk of the applicant remaining in the community.

There is no error in that approach; nothing could have been gained by comparing the nature or extent of that risk depending on which of the two identified scenarios following the grant of a visa eventuated. DVRL does not say anything against that.

Smith v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

15 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1520Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
Mr Smith, a New Zealand citizen, had his Class TY Subclass 444 visa cancelled under s 501(3A) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) after criminal convictions and imprisonment. A delegate refused to revoke the cancellation, and the Administrative Review Tribunal affirmed that refusal. On judicial review, the Federal Court found the Tribunal misconstrued Direction 110’s definition of “family violence” and set aside its decision.
Key holding

Conduct falling within an example of family violence in Direction 110 cl 4 is not automatically family violence; it must also have the effect of coercing or controlling a family member or causing that person to be fearful.

For example, behaviour which involves "intentionally damaging or destroying property" will not necessarily constitute family violence. It will only do so if that behaviour had the effect of coercing or controlling a member of the perpetrator's family, or causing the family member or members to be fearful.

Morton v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

The High Court dismissed Gary Gerard Morton's application for constitutional writs challenging a delegate's refusal of a Return (Residence) (Class BB) visa under s 501(1) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). YVXP v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

13 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1544Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration

Where two or more affected children have differing interests, Direction No. 110 cl 8.4(3) requires the Tribunal to give individual consideration to each child’s best interests; collective consideration does not discharge that obligation.

Daniel v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

8 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1480Migration: court challengeVisa cancellationMigration
Mr Daniel sought judicial review of a Tribunal decision refusing to revoke the mandatory cancellation of his Class TY Subclass 444 visa after he failed the character test due to a substantial criminal record. He alleged the Tribunal misconstrued the tolerance principle in Ministerial Direction 110 and reasoned illogically about reoffending, rehabilitation and weight. The Federal Court dismissed the application with costs.
Key holding

Paragraph 5.2(6) of Ministerial Direction 110 does not deem a higher tolerance and is not a mandatory consideration; it is a framework principle the Tribunal may consider.

First, principle (6) does not deem the Australian community to have a higher tolerance of criminal conduct on the part of those who have lived in Australia for most of their lives, nor indeed those who have lived in Australia from a very young age. As Kumar makes plain, the Tribunal is not required to have regard to it at all; it rises no higher than a consideration within the decision-making framework to which a Tribunal may have regard.

Cristobal Fida v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

8 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1471Migration: court challengeVisa cancellationMigration
The applicant sought judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Working Holiday visa under s 501(1) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) due to character concerns. The Federal Court allowed the application, finding the Tribunal misapplied Direction 110 by assessing substantive visa eligibility rather than whether the duration and purpose of the intended stay affected risk of harm to the Australian community. The Tribunal decision was quashed and the matter remitted for determination according to law.
Key holding

Under Direction 110 [8.1.2(2)(c)], the Tribunal must consider whether and how the duration and purpose of an intended stay affect risk of harm to the Australian community, not whether the applicant satisfies substantive visa eligibility criteria.

The Tribunal erred by misapprehending the nature of the inquiry. It was required to consider, under [8.1.2(2)(c)] of Direction 110, how the duration and purpose of the intended stay affected the risk of harm to the Australian community, and misconstrued its task by considering the issue through the wrong lens (possible visa ineligibility).

Iiga v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 234 (10 March 2026)

10 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 234Migration: court challenge
Mr Iiga sought judicial review of an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming the non-revocation of his mandatory visa cancellation under s 501(3A) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). He alleged the Tribunal failed to comply with Direction 110 by not properly considering family violence seriousness and the risk of reoffending. The Federal Court dismissed the application with costs.
Key holding

A breach of a domestic violence order is only an act of “family violence” for Direction 110 if it involved threatened or actual violence, coercion/control, or fear; benign non-violent breaches are not enough.

As the definition is written, a breach of a domestic violence order will only be capable of constituting an act of "family violence" if it involved threatened or actual violence, had a coercive or controlling effect, or caused fear. If a contravention of a domestic violence order does not have that characteristic, it cannot be regarded as an act of "family violence" for the purposes of applying Direction 110.
