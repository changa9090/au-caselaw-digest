February 2026 (1)

Victorian Hospitals' Industrial Association [2026] FWC 556 (22 February 2026)

22 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 556Employment & workplace
The VHIA applied to the Fair Work Commission under s 425 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to suspend protected industrial action being taken by HSU/HWU members across Victorian public sector health services. The Commission dismissed the application after the HWU gave undertakings to withdraw three clinical bans affecting category 2 surgery and to report on its member ballot by 25 February 2026.
Key holding

A s 425 suspension order is not made unless the Commission is satisfied, taking into account the s 425(1)(a)–(d) matters and any relevant undertakings, that suspension is appropriate.

In light of the HWU undertakings and the other matters I have referred to above in my consideration of s 425(1)(a) - (d), I am not satisfied that it is appropriate for me to make an order suspending protected industrial action under s 425.

September 2025 (1)

Application by Alcoa Portland Aluminium Pty Ltd Trading AS Portland Aluminium

15 Sep 2025Fair Work Commission[2025] FWC 2756Employment & workplace
Alcoa applied to suspend or terminate protected industrial action by AWU members at its Portland aluminium smelter under ss.423, 424 and 425 of the Fair Work Act. The Commission dismissed the applications, finding the two-day-old action was not 'protracted' under s.423, that the smelter was not an important part of the Australian economy under s.424, and that a three-month suspension was not appropriate under s.425 given the advanced state of bargaining.

March 2024 (1)

AKG18 v Minister for Immigration, Citizenship and Multicultural Affairs

8 Mar 2024Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2024] FedCFamC2G 215Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicant sought judicial review of an Administrative Appeals Tribunal decision affirming the refusal of his protection visa. He argued the Tribunal breached s 425 by failing to put the determinative issue to him and failed to consider claims of imputed political opinion and future inability to earn a livelihood. The Federal Circuit and Family Court dismissed the application, finding the Tribunal's questioning sufficiently raised the issues and that no freestanding claim clearly emerged requiring determination.

April 2021 (1)

AAN17 v Minister for Immigration and Border Protection

1 Apr 2021FCCA[2021] FCCA 647Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicants sought judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming the refusal of a Protection (Class XA) visa, where the first applicant claimed to fear harm due to Christian practice in China. The applicants argued the Tribunal was unreasonable, ignored relevant evidence (a Release Certificate), and failed to afford procedural fairness under s 425. The Court found no jurisdictional error and dismissed the application with costs.

September 2009 (1)

Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v SZIAI

23 Sep 2009High Court of Australia[2009] HCA 39Migration: court challengeMigration
The High Court allowed the Minister's appeal from the Federal Court, holding that the Refugee Review Tribunal's failure to make certain inquiries was not a jurisdictional error and that any failure to inquire would be unreasonable only in exceptional circumstances. The Court also found the Tribunal was not obliged under s 425 to give the respondent a fourth oral hearing because the forgery and Jamaat records issues were already live at the third hearing, and the Tribunal had complied with s 424A.
