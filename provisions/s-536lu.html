Bilal Rasheed [2026] FWC 1191 (8 April 2026)

8 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1191Employment & workplace
Bilal Rasheed applied under s 536LU for an unfair deactivation remedy after Uber deactivated his platform access for failing to maintain an average customer rating of 85%. Deputy President Colman found Uber complied with every relevant requirement of the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code and dismissed the application. The case confirms that Code compliance precludes an unfair deactivation finding under s 536LF(c).
Key holding

Under s 536LF(c) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), compliance with the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code means the deactivation is not unfair and the Commission does not then consider whether it was unfair.

If the Code has been complied with, the Commission does not then consider whether the deactivation was unfair.

March 2026 (2)

Tanzeel Ur Rehman v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 953 (20 March 2026)

20 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 953Employment & workplace
Tanzeel Ur Rehman was deactivated from Portier Pacific Pty Ltd’s Uber Driver Platform on 15 December 2025 and applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Portier Pacific voluntarily reactivated him on 20 February 2026 but contested any further order, including lost pay. The FWC made a formal reactivation order under s 536LQ(1) and an order for lost remuneration of $7,096.96 gross under s 536LQ(3).
Key holding

A digital labour platform operator cannot rely on voluntary reactivation, coupled with an argument that a s 536LQ(1) order has no utility, to prevent the FWC from making a formal reactivation order and an appropriate lost-pay order under s 536LQ(3).

However, that a respondent to an application under s 536LU may avert the issuance of an order to restore lost pay under s 536LQ(3) of the Act by voluntarily taking measures as referred to in s 536LQ(1) and contending that an order under s 536LQ(1) is of no utility and therefore the Commission has no basis upon which to make such order, appears contrary to the legislative intent of the provision.

Key holding

The Commission can extend time for an unfair termination remedy only if it is satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist, assessed against the s.536LU(4) factors.

The Commission can extend the time for making an unfair termination application only if it is satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances. Taking into account all of the matters in s.536LU(4), I am not satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances in this case.

February 2026 (2)

Muhammad Shakeel v UBER [2026] FWC 674 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 674Employment & workplace
Muhammad Shakeel brought an unfair dismissal application under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) against Uber. The Fair Work Commission formed a preliminary view that the application had no reasonable prospects, noting consistent decisions that Uber “Driver Partners” were not employees and pointing to the alternative unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU. The application was dismissed under s 587(1)(c) after the Applicant failed to respond to Chambers or prosecute his case.
Key holding

The Commission may dismiss an unfair dismissal application on its own initiative and is not required to persevere with it where the applicant’s conduct clearly demonstrates an unwillingness to participate in proceedings they commenced.

The Commission has power to dismiss an unfair dismissal application on its own initiative. It is accepted that there is no legislative or common law requirement pursuant to which the Commission must persevere with an application in circumstances where an applicant's conduct clearly demonstrates an unwillingness to participate in proceedings commenced at her or his initiative.
