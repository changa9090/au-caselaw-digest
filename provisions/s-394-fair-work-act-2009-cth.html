13 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3930Employment & workplace
Mrs Christine Hayes filed an unfair dismissal application under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 after being dismissed by Southern Cross Care (Qld) Ltd on 24 March 2026. The application was lodged 147 days out of time, so the Commission had to decide whether to extend time based on exceptional circumstances. Key holding

The Commission may extend time for an unfair dismissal application only if satisfied there are exceptional circumstances under s 394(3).

The Commission can extend the time for making an unfair dismissal application only if it is satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances. Taking into account all of the matters in s 394(3), I am not satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances in this case.

Manpreetsingh Nanda v NTI Limited

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3659Employment & workplace
Mr Nanda lodged an unfair dismissal application under s.394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) against NTI Limited. He repeatedly failed to attend Commission conciliations and a case management conference and did not respond to directions on the employer’s s.399A dismissal application. The power to dismiss a substantive application should only be exercised cautiously and sparingly because dismissal extinguishes the applicant’s right to have the application heard and determined according to law.

The power to dismiss a substantive application should only be exercised cautiously and sparingly because it results in the complete extinguishment of an applicant's right to have their application heard and determined according to law before they have had their "day in court.

Mr Bradley Johnson v Mt Arthur Coal Pty Limited

11 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3890Employment & workplace
Mr Bradley Johnson, a Maintenance Technician at Mt Arthur Coal, was dismissed on 17 December 2025 for failing to follow a lawful and reasonable direction and for incapacity to perform the inherent requirements of his role. He brought an unfair dismissal application under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The Fair Work Commission dismissed the application, finding a valid reason and that the dismissal was not harsh, unjust or unreasonable.
Key holding

An employee’s failure to comply with a lawful and reasonable direction can constitute a valid reason for dismissal on the conduct ground.

A failure to follow a lawful and reasonable direction may give an employer a valid reason to dismiss an employee on the basis of their conduct.

Mr Joshua Barbagallo v Bentrol Pty Ltd

8 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3853Employment & workplace
Mr Barbagallo applied for an unfair dismissal remedy under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) on 10 July 2026. The Commission had to determine when his dismissal took effect and, if the application was late, whether exceptional circumstances warranted an extension. The Commission found the dismissal took effect on 10 June 2026, the application was 9 days late, and no exceptional circumstances were established, so the application was dismissed.
Key holding

Under s 394(2) and s 394(3), if an unfair dismissal application is made more than 21 days after dismissal takes effect and the Commission is not satisfied there are exceptional circumstances, it cannot allow further time and must dismiss the application.

As the application was made more than 21 days after the dismissal took effect and I am not satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances, I cannot allow Mr Barbagallo further time. I dismiss his application for an unfair dismissal remedy.
