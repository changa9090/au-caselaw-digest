October 2026 (1)

Morgan v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

9 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1489Migration: court challengeMigration
Brenton Morgan sought judicial review of the Assistant Minister’s decision under s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to set aside the Administrative Review Tribunal’s revocation of his visa cancellation and cancel his Class TY Subclass 444 visa. He alleged legal unreasonableness because the Assistant Minister relied on his new partner relationship being “not yet tested in the community” without seeking updated information after his parole.
Key holding

A failure to seek updated information will not make a s 501BA decision legally unreasonable where the inference drawn from the available dated material—such as giving limited weight to a new relationship not yet tested by community life—is reasonably open and logically supported.

It was not legally unreasonable to regard a relationship that had existed outside of the prison environment for no more than eight weeks as a relationship that "had yet to be tested in the community". In the context of the reasons, it was a finding supported by logical grounds.

Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v XMBQ (Costs) [2026] FCAFC 55 (30 April 2026)

30 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia (Full Court)[2026] FCAFC 55Migration: court challenge
This costs judgment follows the Minister’s successful appeal in Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v XMBQ [2026] FCAFC 19, in which the Full Court held that s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) is not subject to an implied reasonable-time limit. The primary judge had found otherwise in XMBQ v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs [2025] FCA 553. The Full Court ordered the respondent to pay the Minister’s appeal costs and 50% of the Minister’s first-instance costs.
Key holding

Costs ordinarily follow the event, but the Court may split costs where a party has unsuccessfully pursued a distinct fact-intensive issue.

Although the Court is generally unwilling to split costs orders by reference to particular issues, we consider that in this case the respondent should not have to bear the cost of the Minister having unsuccessfully pursued a fact-intensive issue.

March 2026 (3)

Wihongi-Lim v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 317 (24 March 2026)

24 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 317Migration: court challenge
The Federal Court dismissed an application challenging the Minister’s personal decision under s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to set aside an AAT decision and cancel the applicant’s visa on character and national interest grounds. The applicant alleged the Minister’s relapse-risk finding was irrational and that the Minister failed personally to consider all material before deciding. Collier J found neither ground established.
Key holding

A Minister exercising the personal visa cancellation power is not required personally to read and examine every submission or item of material; reliance on a departmental brief or submission that accurately summarises and orders the material is permitted.

For the foregoing reasons, it is not a condition of the valid exercise of the power conferred by s 501CA(4) for the Minister, when personally exercising that power, personally to read and examine the submissions, representations and other material received in every case. The Minister may rely instead upon departmental briefs and submissions which accurately summarise and order that material.

Archer v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCAFC 20 (9 March 2026)

9 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia (Full Court)[2026] FCAFC 20Migration: court challenge
Margaret Archer appealed against judicial review of the Minister’s decision under s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to set aside an AAT decision and cancel her visa after she was convicted of assisting her son conceal a murder. The Full Court dismissed the appeal, holding there is no implied reasonable-time limitation on s 501BA(2) and that the Minister’s national-interest risk assessment was not legally unreasonable.
Key holding

The power in s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) is not subject to an implied limitation that it be exercised within a reasonable period of time.

Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v XMBQ [2026] FCAFC 19 (9 March 2026)

9 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia (Full Court)[2026] FCAFC 19Migration: court challenge
The Full Court allowed the Minister’s appeal against orders setting aside a visa cancellation made under s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The respondent’s visa had been revoked by the Tribunal under s 501CA, but the Minister later personally cancelled it almost 38 months after that Tribunal decision. The Court held that s 501BA(2) is not subject to an implied reasonable-time limitation, and dismissed the apprehended-bias notice of contention.
Key holding

Section 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) is not subject to an implied temporal limitation that extinguishes the Minister’s power if it is not exercised within a reasonable time after the original s 501CA decision; delay goes to lawful exercise, not jurisdiction.

The central question in this appeal is whether s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) confers a power that is silently extinguished if not exercised within a "reasonable time" after the occurrence of the event which enlivens the power. The answer is that it does not.
