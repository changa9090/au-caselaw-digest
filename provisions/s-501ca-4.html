Australian Securities and Investments Commission v Dawson

20 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1547Migration: court challengeVisa cancellationMigration
This was a judicial review of the Administrative Review Tribunal’s decision affirming non-revocation of Mr Dabea’s visa cancellation under s 501CA(4) after serious child sexual offences. Mr Dabea alleged the Tribunal failed to properly consider impediments if removed to Fiji and denied procedural fairness by sending post-hearing DFAT country information. Key holding

On judicial review, a tribunal’s reasons must be read fairly and as a whole, so a failure to evaluate a mandatory consideration is not readily inferred merely because the reasons are brief.

The task on judicial review is to read the reasons fairly and as a whole: see EGH19 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] HCA 33 at [68] (Gordon, Steward and Gleeson JJ).

Hoang and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

19 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2396Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration

Although Mr Hoang had a substantial criminal record for repeat drug and weapons offending and failed the character test, the Tribunal found another reason to revoke under s 501CA(4) and set aside the refusal.
Key holding

A residual risk of reoffending may be acceptable, and another reason to revoke may exist, where serious addiction-related offending is offset by sustained rehabilitation, family support and long residence.

The Applicant has, nonetheless, made significant progress in rehabilitation and has retained the support and trust of his immediate family who are an important element to his future success. I consider that the 'residual' risk of reoffending is acceptable despite the potentially serious consequences for the community, and Mr Hoang's length of residence from a young age must take into account.

Gautama v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

15 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1519Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicant sought judicial review of an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming the non-revocation of his cancelled Five-Year Resident Return (Class BB) (Subclass 155) visa under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). He argued the Tribunal erred by failing to apply the s 201 “10-year rule”, failing to consider mandatory considerations and misconstruing s 501CA(4)(b)(ii) as discretionary. The Federal Court dismissed the application.
Key holding

Section 201 of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth), the “10-year rule”, has no application to mandatory visa cancellation under s 501(3A), because s 201 belongs to the separate deportation framework.

I accept the Minister's submissions. The Tribunal was correct in its conclusion that s 201 had no application to the matter before it.

Upson and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

1 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2275Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
In Upson and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration) [2026] ARTA 2275, a 31-year-old New Zealand citizen who has lived in Australia since infancy had his special category visa mandatorily cancelled under s 501(3A) after being sentenced to 7 years 6 months for serious drug offences. A delegate refused to revoke the cancellation, but the Administrative Review Tribunal set that decision aside and substituted revocation after finding “another reason” under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii).
Key holding

Under Direction no. 110, whether a non-citizen poses an “unacceptable risk” is a distinct evaluative conclusion reached by balancing the likelihood and potential harm of future offending, not merely the likelihood of re-offending.

The concept of 'risk', and whether it is 'unacceptable', under cl 8.1.2(1) of the Direction is not the same thing as the 'likelihood of the non-citizen engaging in further criminal or other serious conduct'.[25]

Although she failed the character test, the Tribunal found another reason to revoke the cancellation. The decision under review was set aside and the visa cancellation revoked under s 501CA(4).
Key holding

Where a non-citizen fails the character test after mandatory visa cancellation, the Tribunal may revoke cancellation under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii) if satisfied there is another reason, exercising the discretion in accordance with the applicable ministerial direction.

Section 501CA(4)(b)(ii) of the Act requires the Tribunal to consider if there is another reason why the discretion should be exercised to revoke the original decision. In exercising this discretion, the Tribunal must have regard to the Direction (s 499(2A)).

Vu v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural Affairs [2026] FCA 413 (10 April 2026)

10 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 413Migration: court challenge
Vu, a Vietnamese citizen, had his visa cancelled after convictions for serious drug and firearm offences. On remittal after an earlier Tribunal decision was quashed, the same Tribunal member again affirmed the delegate’s non-revocation decision. The Federal Court dismissed Vu’s judicial review application challenging Direction 110, the Tribunal’s approach, legal reasonableness, and apprehended bias, with costs.
Key holding

A direction under s 499 cannot require a decision-maker exercising the s 501CA(4) discretion to reach a particular outcome or compel specific weight for a particular matter.

In relation to the former, a direction given under s 499(1) of the Act cannot require a decision-maker exercising power under, relevantly, s 501CA(4) either "to reach a particular outcome, or compel specific weight to be given to a particular matter": Singh at [23] and [81] (Mortimer J; Raper J agreeing); Jagroop at [57] and [79] (Kenny and Mortimer JJ; Dowsett J agreeing).

So v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 345 (26 March 2026)

26 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 345Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter test
The Federal Court reviewed an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming non-revocation of the applicant’s mandatorily cancelled Resident Return visa under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act. The Tribunal found the applicant failed the character test and that there was no another reason to revoke, but the Court held it failed to consider his non-refoulement claims as required by Ministerial Direction 110. The decision was set aside and remitted for redetermination.
Key holding

A decision-maker considering revocation under s 501CA(4) must consider claims capable of engaging international non-refoulement obligations raised by a non-citizen not subject to a protection finding, as required by para 9.1.2(1) of Direction 110.

Paragraph 9.1.2(1) of Direction 110 expressly requires that claims raised by a non-citizen who is not the subject of a protection finding that may give rise to international non-refoulement obligations "must be considered". The provision recognises that a person may raise international non-refoulement obligations distinct from the statutory "protection obligations" assessed in the protection visa process.

Wihongi-Lim v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 317 (24 March 2026)

24 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 317Migration: court challenge
The Federal Court dismissed an application challenging the Minister’s personal decision under s 501BA(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to set aside an AAT decision and cancel the applicant’s visa on character and national interest grounds. The applicant alleged the Minister’s relapse-risk finding was irrational and that the Minister failed personally to consider all material before deciding. Collier J found neither ground established.
Key holding

A Minister exercising the personal visa cancellation power is not required personally to read and examine every submission or item of material; reliance on a departmental brief or submission that accurately summarises and orders the material is permitted.

For the foregoing reasons, it is not a condition of the valid exercise of the power conferred by s 501CA(4) for the Minister, when personally exercising that power, personally to read and examine the submissions, representations and other material received in every case. The Minister may rely instead upon departmental briefs and submissions which accurately summarise and order that material.

TSXN v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 205 (6 March 2026)

6 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 205Migration: court challenge
The Federal Court judicially reviewed an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Protection (Class XA) (subclass 866) visa under s 501(1) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Court found the Tribunal misunderstood its review task by repeatedly reasoning as if it were deciding whether to revoke a mandatory visa cancellation under s 501CA(4), rather than whether to refuse to grant a visa under s 501(1). The application was allowed, the Tribunal decision quashed, and the matter remitted for reconsideration.
Key holding

On review of a visa refusal under s 501(1), the Tribunal must address the distinct statutory task of deciding whether to refuse to grant the visa, not whether to revoke a mandatory cancellation under s 501CA(4).

A decision made under s 501(1) is distinct to one under s 501CA(4). Whilst s 501(1) is concerned with a refusal by the Minister to grant a visa, s 501CA(4) is concerned with the revocation of the mandatory cancellation of a visa.
