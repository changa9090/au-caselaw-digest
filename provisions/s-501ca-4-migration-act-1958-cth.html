GWN25 by his Litigation Representative the Public Advocate v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

16 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1535Migration: court challengeMigration
GWN25 sought judicial review of an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming refusal to revoke the mandatory cancellation of his visa under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Minister conceded jurisdictional error in light of Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v DVRL, and the parties jointly proposed certiorari and mandamus. The Federal Court set aside the Tribunal decision and remitted the matter for redetermination, with costs.
Key holding

When assessing community risk under the relevant ministerial direction, the Tribunal must consider the different levels of risk depending on the visa status the applicant would hold, where that issue is advanced and supported by evidence.

The effect of the full court's ruling in DVRL is that the Tribunal was obliged, when considering "... the risk to the Australian community ..." for the purposes of cl 8.1(2) of the applicable ministerial direction, to consider not merely the risk that the applicant might pose if his permanent visa were restored but also the risk that he might pose as the holder of an ongoing bridging visa.

Gautama v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

15 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1519Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicant sought judicial review of an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming the non-revocation of his cancelled Five-Year Resident Return (Class BB) (Subclass 155) visa under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). He argued the Tribunal erred by failing to apply the s 201 “10-year rule”, failing to consider mandatory considerations and misconstruing s 501CA(4)(b)(ii) as discretionary. The Federal Court dismissed the application.
Key holding

Section 201 of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth), the “10-year rule”, has no application to mandatory visa cancellation under s 501(3A), because s 201 belongs to the separate deportation framework.

I accept the Minister's submissions. The Tribunal was correct in its conclusion that s 201 had no application to the matter before it.

Niven and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

8 Oct 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2274Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
This was a merits review of a delegate’s decision not to revoke the mandatory cancellation of Mr Niven’s Class TY Subclass 444 Special Category (Temporary) visa under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Tribunal found the applicant did not pass the character test but accepted there was another reason to revoke the cancellation, set aside the delegate’s decision, and substituted a decision revoking the cancellation.
Key holding

Under s 501CA(4)(b)(ii), where an applicant fails the character test, the Tribunal may revoke mandatory cancellation if satisfied there is another reason, applying Direction no. 110.

Section 501CA(4) states that the Minister may revoke the original decision if: (a) the person makes representations in accordance with the invitation; and (b) the Minister is satisfied: (i) that the person passes the character test (as defined by s 501); or (ii) that there is another reason why the original decision should be revoked.

Ututaonga and Minister for Immigration and Citizenship (Migration)

15 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2285Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
This is a migration review of a delegate’s decision not to revoke the mandatory cancellation of a New Zealand citizen’s Class TY Subclass 444 Special Category (Temporary) visa under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The applicant had a substantial criminal record, including serious drug supply offences, and the Tribunal had to weigh the Direction no. 110 considerations for and against revocation.
Key holding

In exercising the s 501CA(4) discretion, the Tribunal must weigh all primary and other considerations under Direction no. 110 in a single evaluation, with greater weight generally given to protection of the Australian community.

In determining the weight to be applied to each consideration, the Tribunal considered the primary and other considerations and weighed them in light of the evidence and findings using the guidance provided by the Direction. Greater weight must generally be given to the protection of the Australian community than other primary considerations.

ZTBL v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCAFC 48 (16 April 2026)

16 Apr 2026Federal Court of Australia (Full Court)[2026] FCAFC 48Migration: court challenge
This Full Court appeal concerned a Tribunal decision affirming the non-revocation of the appellant’s mandatory visa cancellation under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The appellant argued the Tribunal erred by assuming bridging visa curfew and monitoring conditions were validly imposed, later held invalid in YBFZ v Minister for Immigration [2024] HCA 40, and by misdescribing the statutory satisfaction test in cl 070.612A. The Full Court dismissed the appeal.
Key holding

Generally, an error of law will amount to jurisdictional error only if it was material to the decision actually made, in the sense that there is a realistic possibility the decision could have been different absent the error.

in most cases an error will only be jurisdictional if the error was material to the decision that was made in fact, in the sense that there is a realistic possibility that the decision that was made in fact could have been different if the error had not occurred.

Malielegaoi v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 171 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 171Migration: court challenge
Malielegaoi sought an extension of time under s 477A(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth) to seek judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming non-revocation under s 501CA(4). The applicant did not identify the decision or explain why an extension was in the interests of justice, failed to comply with court orders, and did not appear at hearings. The Federal Court dismissed the extension application and the proceeding, with fixed costs.
Key holding

Under s 477A(2) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth), an extension of time cannot be granted unless the applicant specifies why an extension is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice and the Court is satisfied it is necessary.

As the applicant has not specified why he considers that it is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice to make the order sought, he fails on both fronts. Ultimately, I cannot be satisfied that it is necessary in the interests of the administration of justice to make the order.

Hughes and Minister for Immigration, Citizenship, and Multicultural Affairs (Migration)

4 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3158Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewCharacter testMigration
Michael Hughes, a 58-year-old New Zealand/United Kingdom citizen, had his subclass 189 visa mandatorily cancelled after being convicted of indecent dealing with a child under 13 and sentenced to 20 months’ imprisonment. He sought review of a delegate’s decision not to revoke that cancellation under s 501CA(4) of the Migration Act 1958 (Cth). The Tribunal affirmed the non-revocation decision, applying Direction no. 110 and refusing to go behind the conviction and sentence.
Key holding

In a s 501CA review, the Tribunal cannot go behind the essential facts of the conviction and sentence that founded the mandatory cancellation, and an applicant bears a heavy onus to challenge those facts.

In those circumstances, it is not open to the Tribunal to go behind the essential facts of that conviction and sentence. Moreover, the Applicant has not discharged what would otherwise be his "heavy onus" to challenge the facts upon which the conviction and sentence are necessarily based as set out above.
