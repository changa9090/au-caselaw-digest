Kaur v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

30 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1644Migration: court challengeMigration
The appellant challenged the Federal Circuit and Family Court’s dismissal of her judicial review application concerning a Subclass 500 student visa refusal. The Tribunal had affirmed the refusal because it was not satisfied she was enrolled in a course of study as required by cl 500.211(a). The Federal Court dismissed her appeal.
Key holding

The Tribunal does not contravene ss 359A or 359AA by relying on an absence of evidence in an applicant’s own documents, as that absence is not, without more, separate adverse information that must be put to the applicant.

The absence of evidence of payment was not itself separate adverse information which the Tribunal was required to put to her.

Ismath v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

1 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1919Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
Judicial review of an Administrative Appeals Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Skilled (Provisional) (Class VC) visa under cl 485.231(3) of the Migration Regulations 1994 (Cth). The Tribunal sent a s 359A natural justice letter allowing a response by 27 February 2024 but made its decision on 26 February 2024. The Court found a failure to afford procedural fairness and quashed the Tribunal decision.
Key holding

A Tribunal that decides before the expiry of the prescribed period it gave an applicant to respond to adverse information under s 359A breaches s 359B and denies procedural fairness, amounting to jurisdictional error.

In failing to grant the applicant the prescribed period in which to comment on adverse information the Tribunal did not comply with s 359B of the Migration Act and this amounted to a failure to afford procedural fairness.

March 2026 (1)

Shah v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship [2026] FCA 226 (9 March 2026)

9 Mar 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 226Migration: court challenge
This is a Federal Court appeal by a family whose Employer Nomination (subclass 186) visa applications were refused because the first appellant lacked an approved employer nomination. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal after the appellants failed to respond to a s 359A invitation, and the primary judge dismissed judicial review. Cheeseman J dismissed the appeal, holding there was no jurisdictional error and the appellants were not entitled to appear before the Tribunal.
Key holding

Where a review applicant fails to respond to a s 359A invitation or seek an extension within the prescribed period, the combined operation of ss 359C(2), 360(3) and 363A means they are not entitled to appear before the Tribunal.

The combined effect of the cascading provisions in ss 359C(2), 360(3) and 363A was that the appellants were not entitled to appear before the Tribunal.

September 2024 (2)

Varas Elgueta (Migration)

13 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3465Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Jade Murphy
Mr Varas Elgueta sought review of a delegate’s refusal to grant a Temporary Skill Shortage (Class GK) Subclass 482 visa in the Medium-term stream for the occupation of Internal Auditor. The related nomination by Black and White Waiters Pty Ltd was refused and later affirmed by the Tribunal, meaning the visa criterion requiring an approved nomination was not met. The Tribunal also proceeded without a hearing after the applicant failed to respond to a s 359A invitation.
Key holding

For a Subclass 482 visa, the visa criterion requires the identified nomination to be an approved, current nomination made by an approved work sponsor.

Clause 482.212(1) requires that the nomination identified in the visa application is approved, was made by a person who was an approved work sponsor at the time of approval, and has not ceased.

March 2009 (1)

Minister for Immigration and Citizenship v Kumar

11 Mar 2009High Court of Australia[2009] HCA 10Migration: court challengeMigration
The High Court allowed the Minister's appeal, holding that the Migration Review Tribunal was not obliged under s 359A of the Migration Act to disclose to a visa applicant information that it had received in confidence from an informant, including the informant's identity, because such information constituted 'non-disclosable information' under s 359A(4). The Tribunal had complied with s 359A(1) by putting the substance of the allegations to the applicant and inviting a response, without revealing the confidential source.
