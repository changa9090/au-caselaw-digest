Application by Parminder Singh

16 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3947Employment & workplace
Mr Parminder Singh applied under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair deactivation remedy after Uber permanently deactivated his driver account on 25 February 2026 following two touting complaints. The Commission found Uber failed to comply with the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code and that there was no valid reason for the deactivation. It ordered reactivation and restoration of lost pay.
Key holding

For s 536LF(c), consistency with the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code requires complete compliance with the Code, not merely substantial compliance.

We do not accept, for the purpose of the consideration required under s 536LF(c), that consistency with the Code tolerates less than complete compliance with it. Section 536LJ(3) makes it clear that consistency with the Code requires compliance with it.

Application by Zelai Zhao

13 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3921Employment & workplace
Mr Zelai Zhao applied to the Fair Work Commission under s.536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair deactivation remedy after Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd deactivated his access to the Uber Delivery Platform. The application was filed 124 days out of time. The Commission determined the extension-of-time issue on the papers and dismissed the application because it was not satisfied there were exceptional circumstances.
Key holding

The Commission may extend time under s.536LU(4) only if satisfied exceptional circumstances exist after weighing the statutory factors; absent exceptional circumstances, a late unfair deactivation remedy application must be dismissed.

I have weighed each of the considerations I am required to take into account and have determined that there are not exceptional circumstances in this case warranting an extension of time of 124 days. As I have refused the application to extend time, the application is dismissed.

Application by Raham Shahzad

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3914Employment & workplaceContracts
Mr Shahzad applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after Amazon deactivated him from the Amazon Flex digital labour platform. Amazon objected that he was not protected from unfair deactivation because he had not performed work on the platform on a regular basis for at least six months. The Fair Work Commission dismissed the application.
Key holding

For s 536LD(c), the Commission’s inquiry is limited to whether the person actually performed work and whether that work was performed on a regular basis for at least six months before deactivation.

The relevant inquiry under s 536LD(c) of the Act is limited to, first, whether a person has been performing work and secondly, whether such work was performed on a regular basis for a period of at least 6 months.

Shaharyar Ali v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd T/A Uber Eats [2026] FWC 1182 (8 April 2026)

8 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1182Employment & workplace
Mr Ali, an Uber Eats delivery person, was deactivated after failing to maintain Melbourne’s 85% minimum customer satisfaction rating. He applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The Commission found Uber Eats breached the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code’s timing requirement but held the deactivation was not unfair and dismissed the application.
Key holding

Under s 536LH, a deactivation may be not unfair despite a s 15(a) Code timing breach where there is a valid reason and the other relevant Code processes are met.

While the respondent failed to comply with s 15(a) of the Code, it otherwise met its Code obligations with respect to the process it adopted in relation to Mr Ali's ratings. Having regard to all the circumstances, I do not consider that deactivation was a disproportionate response.

Tanzeel Ur Rehman v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 953 (20 March 2026)

20 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 953Employment & workplace
Tanzeel Ur Rehman was deactivated from Portier Pacific Pty Ltd’s Uber Driver Platform on 15 December 2025 and applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Portier Pacific voluntarily reactivated him on 20 February 2026 but contested any further order, including lost pay. The FWC made a formal reactivation order under s 536LQ(1) and an order for lost remuneration of $7,096.96 gross under s 536LQ(3).
Key holding

A digital labour platform operator cannot rely on voluntary reactivation, coupled with an argument that a s 536LQ(1) order has no utility, to prevent the FWC from making a formal reactivation order and an appropriate lost-pay order under s 536LQ(3).

However, that a respondent to an application under s 536LU may avert the issuance of an order to restore lost pay under s 536LQ(3) of the Act by voluntarily taking measures as referred to in s 536LQ(1) and contending that an order under s 536LQ(1) is of no utility and therefore the Commission has no basis upon which to make such order, appears contrary to the legislative intent of the provision.

Chris Karras v Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd trading as Uber [2026] FWC 613 (13 March 2026)

13 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 613Employment & workplace
Mr Karras applied for an unfair deactivation remedy under s 536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) after Uber suspended his platform access from 4 to 12 December 2025. Uber objected that the suspension was for not more than 7 business days and therefore could not be an unfair deactivation under s 536LH(3). The Commission dismissed the application.
Key holding

Under s 536LH(3)(a), “business day” has its ordinary Australian meaning of Monday to Friday, excluding Saturday, Sunday and public holidays, regardless of the platform’s operating hours.

Accordingly, I find that business day for the purpose of s 536LH(3)(a) means Monday to Friday, excluding Saturday, Sunday and public holidays. Consequently, Mr Karras was only suspended for 6 business days, and therefore his suspension does not meet a threshold requirement to be considered an unfair deactivation.

Syed Bukhari [2026] FWC 625 (25 February 2026)

25 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 625Employment & workplace
Syed Bukhari applied to the Fair Work Commission under s.536LU of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair deactivation remedy, alleging Uber Australia Pty Ltd deactivated him in October 2022. The respondent did not dispute the October 2022 deactivation date. The Commission dismissed the application for lack of jurisdiction because Part 3A-3 only applies to deactivations occurring after 26 August 2024.
Key holding

Part 3A-3 of the FW Act only applies to deactivations occurring after commencement on 26 August 2024, so the Commission lacks jurisdiction over an unfair deactivation application for an earlier deactivation.

Having regard to the statutory provisions of the FW Act and the Amending Act as set out above, it is apparent that Part 3-3A of the Act has no application to a deactivation which occurred prior to 26 August 2024.
