Estefania Asensio Tato v Lycée Condorcet Trading AS The International French School Of Sydney Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4154Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dealt with a s 739 dispute brought by a former secondary Spanish teacher against her former school over alleged underpayments/classification for February and March 2025 work. The Commission upheld the school’s jurisdictional objection because the dispute was not raised until after employment ended, dismissed the application, and noted any underpayment claim could be pursued in court. The deed of release was not determinative.
Key holding

Under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), the Commission lacks jurisdiction to determine an enterprise agreement dispute where the dispute is raised after the employment relationship has ended.

The entitlements conferred in the Agreement remain enforceable for a limited period on application to the relevant Court, but the Commission is unable to deal with the dispute under the dispute resolution clause where the dispute was raised after the employment relationship had ended.

Jason Perry v Australian Port Service (Qld) Pty Ltd Trading AS Ausport Marine

23 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4146Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission resolved a s.739 dispute concerning the Ausport Marine enterprise agreement, principally the date for the first CPI wage increase and travel-time/reimbursement entitlements under cl 12(a). The Deputy President issued a recommendation answering five questions and confirmed that s.739 cannot be used to terminate the Agreement or revisit the BOOT.
Key holding

Clause 12(a) entitles an employee to reimbursement for kilometres and tolls only where the employer requires the employee to use their own vehicle, not where the employee chooses to do so.

Therefore, in my view, the entitlement to be reimbursed for distance travelled and tolls is limited to circumstances where the use of the employee's own vehicle is a requirement by the employer, not a choice by the employee.

Communications, Electrical, Electronic, Energy, Information, Postal, Plumbing and Allied Services Union of Australia (128V) v Ausgrid

21 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4082Employment & workplace
The ETU brought a s 739 dispute against Ausgrid over whether Leading Hands performing Emergency Response Coordinator (ERC) on-call duties in Newcastle/Lower Hunter should be paid higher duties at Level 1 of the D2 Field Supervisor band. The Commission found ERC work overlapped with the Leading Hand role and was not the work of a higher-band Field Supervisor, so clause 13 did not apply. It declined to dismiss for want of a live dispute but answered the formulated question “no”.
Key holding

A Leading Hand performing ERC duties is not entitled to higher duties pay under clause 13 because ERC work overlaps with and is contemplated by the Leading Hand role rather than being the responsibilities and duties of a higher-band Field Supervisor Role.

For the reasons set out above, I am not persuaded that the work performed by an ERC is to be regarded as that of a Field Supervisor and not that of a Leading Hand. Rather, I accept Mr Richardson's evidence that there is an overlap between the Roles when it comes to emergency response, and that such overlap is a feature of the R&P Framework.

Peter Hugh Crossley v Protech GPS Pty Ltd

14 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3876Employment & workplace
The applicant, Mr Crossley, applied under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for the Fair Work Commission to deal with a dispute about redundancy severance and a pro-rata attendance bonus under the Global Product Search Enterprise Agreement 2018. Protech GPS Pty Ltd objected that the Commission lacked jurisdiction because the applicant was no longer employed and the dispute procedure had not been invoked before termination. The Commission upheld the objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

The Commission may deal with a s.739 enterprise agreement dispute after employment ends only if it was seized of jurisdiction before termination; a dispute first invoked or filed after termination is outside jurisdiction.

Whilst I accept that the authorities in decisions such as Jajoo, Telstra , Deakin University, Kentz, Broadspectrum, and Goonyella make clear the Commission does not necessarily lose its capacity to deal with a dispute because an employment relationship comes to an end, the Commission must at some point before the termination of employment become seized of the jurisdiction in order to continue to deal with it after termination occurs.

Police Federation of Australia (200V) v The Chief Commissioner of Police - Victoria Police

14 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3946Employment & workplace
The FWC determined a s 739 dispute between TPAV and Victoria Police about whether two injured employees on AMUP were entitled to continued COT and DA allowances after their secondments to the DST ended. Commissioner Fox held that non-payment was consistent with cl 77 of the enterprise agreement because those allowances were not ordinarily payable in the employees’ substantive positions once the secondments ceased.
Key holding

Clause 77 AMUP does not require continued payment of position-specific allowances after the temporary secondment attracting them ends; “full pay” reflects what the employee would ordinarily have received absent AMUP.

Having considered the words, context and purpose, I do not accept that the relevant allowances continue to be payable upon the cessation of the secondment to DST.

Construction, Forestry and Maritime Employees Union v Qube Ports Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1331 (17 April 2026)

17 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1331Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dealt with a s.739 dispute between the CFMEU and Qube Ports about whether the Stevedoring Industry Award 2020 prevented Qube from rostering employees who had worked a night shift to an evening shift the next calendar day. Deputy President Dean found the Agreement and Award were inconsistent on shift definitions and minimum breaks, so the Qube Ports Port of Port Kembla Enterprise Agreement 2024 prevailed. The answer to the disputed question was no.
Key holding

Where an enterprise agreement comprehensively regulates shift classifications and minimum breaks and specifically permits rostering that the Award prohibits, the inconsistency is resolved in favour of the Agreement.

The inconsistency is clear, that is the Agreement specifically allows for a rostering arrangement that the Award does not. In those circumstances, the Agreement prevails.

The Australian Maritime Officers' Union, The Australian Institute of Marine and Power Engineers v Solstad Australia Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1304 (15 April 2026)

15 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1304Employment & workplace
The AMOU and AIMPE brought a s.739 dispute against Solstad over whether the Anchor Handling Allowance in two 2023 enterprise agreements is payable only on days with anchor handling operations or for every day between lifting the first anchor and setting the last anchor. The dispute concerned tow and offshore demobilisation work from 16 November to 28 December 2025. Commissioner Lim issued a recommendation favouring the Unions’ construction.
Key holding

The Anchor Handling Allowance is payable for each day or part thereof in the continuous period from lifting the first anchor at the current location to setting the last anchor at the new location, not only on days when anchor handling operations occur.

I recommend that Solstad apply the Anchor Handling Allowance so that it is paid to employees when on board a Support Vessel, for each day (or part thereof) in the period of time that commences with the lifting of the first anchor at a current location and concludes with the setting of the last anchor at a new location.
