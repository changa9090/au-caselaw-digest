12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3867Employment & workplace

Ms Mary Howard v P & S St Quintin Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1323 (16 April 2026)

16 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1323Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission determined an extension of time application under s 394(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Ms Howard filed her unfair dismissal application 59 days after her dismissal took effect, outside the 21-day statutory period. Commissioner Walkaden granted the extension, finding exceptional circumstances based principally on delays attributable to her solicitors’ representative error.
Key holding

Where a delay is fairly characterised as representative error by the applicant’s solicitors, rather than the applicant’s own fault, that reason may, when weighed with the other s 394(3) matters, amount to exceptional circumstances supporting an extension of time.

Taking into account the reason for the delay, I am satisfied that reasons at [17](a) - (d) in all the circumstances amounts to exceptional circumstances. This weighs in favour of a finding that there are exceptional circumstances to warrant the grant of an extension of time.

Daniel Hunter v Microlise Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1265 (13 April 2026)

13 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1265Employment & workplace
The applicant, a New Zealand-based employee of an Australian company, was made redundant and filed an unfair dismissal application one day after the 21-day statutory time limit. The Fair Work Commission considered whether to extend time under s 394(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). It found no exceptional circumstances and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A time extension under s 394(3) requires exceptional circumstances, and ignorance of the 21-day limit or New Zealand residence will not by itself provide an acceptable explanation for delay.

Ignorance of the 21-day time period is not an acceptable explanation for delay. I do not consider that the applicant's residence in New Zealand is sufficient to give his ignorance of the 21-day period, the character of an exceptional circumstance.

March 2026 (2)

Gary Henry Mountney v H-E Parts International Mining Solutions Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 796 (11 March 2026)

11 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 796Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dismissed Mr Mountney’s s.394 unfair dismissal application because it was filed six days late and the Commission was not satisfied there were exceptional circumstances to extend time under s.394(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). Mr Mountney relied principally on a pre-booked family holiday, but the Commission found this did not explain the whole delay.
Key holding

A pre-booked family holiday does not amount to exceptional circumstances under s.394(3) where it fails to explain the whole delay and the applicant had time before and after the holiday to lodge.

In those circumstances, the pre-booked holiday does not satisfactorily explain the entirety of the delay, nor does it demonstrate circumstances that were out of the ordinary course, or unusual, or special, or uncommon so as to be characterised as exceptional.

Andrew Sutherland v [2026] FWC 736 (5 March 2026)

5 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 736Employment & workplace
Mr Sutherland applied for an unfair dismissal remedy against Aon Services Pty Ltd, but his application was filed five days outside the 21-day statutory period. The Fair Work Commission considered whether to extend time under s 394(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The Commission found no exceptional circumstances and dismissed the application.
Key holding

Under s 394(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), the Commission may extend time for an unfair dismissal application only if it is satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist, regardless of how short the delay is.

Regardless of the length of the delay, where an application is lodged outside the statutory time limit, the Commission must be satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances supporting an extension of time.

Himanshu Patel v Telstra Corporation Limited [2026] FWC 618 (25 February 2026)

25 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 618Employment & workplace
Mr Patel lodged an unfair dismissal application against Telstra 14 days outside the 21-day statutory time limit after his dismissal took effect on 16 December 2025. The Fair Work Commission considered whether to extend time under s 394(3) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) but found no exceptional circumstances. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

On an extension of time application under s 394(3), ordinary reactions to dismissal, financial uncertainty and ignorance of the 21-day limit, without evidence of incapacity, do not establish exceptional circumstances.

I am not satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances in this case, either when the various circumstances are considered individually or together.
