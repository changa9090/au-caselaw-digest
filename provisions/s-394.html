Mitchell Hooper v The trustee for Barefoot Grass Unit Trust

15 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3950Employment & workplace
Mr Hooper applied under s.394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) alleging unfair dismissal after his Project Coordinator role with Barefoot Projects was made redundant. The Fair Work Commission found the dismissal was a genuine redundancy under s.389 because the role no longer existed due to financial/operational restructuring, consultation obligations were met, and reasonable redeployment was not available. The s.394 application was dismissed.
Key holding

A dismissal is a genuine redundancy under s.389(1)(a) where operational requirements cause the employer to make a role redundant, even if the role’s duties are redistributed among existing employees.

The decision to make the position of Project Coordinator redundant was to meet operational requirements. Allocation of various duties and responsibilities of the role among existing functional roles met the requirement.

Ms Duyen Ho v Persol Staffing Australia Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1121 (1 April 2026)

1 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1121Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dismissed an unfair dismissal application by a labour-hire chemist after her host client, PPG, barred her from its site. The Commission found she was not dismissed by her employer, Persol Staffing Australia Pty Ltd, because her employment was neither terminated at the employer's initiative nor forced by its conduct. The s.394 application therefore failed at the jurisdictional threshold.
Key holding

Under s.386, a labour-hire employee is not dismissed where the employer has not terminated employment at its initiative and has not engaged in conduct forcing the employee to resign.

More specifically, if the Applicant's employment was not terminated at the initiative of the Respondent, or if my finding is that she was not forced to resign because of conduct or a course of conduct engaged in by the Respondent, there is no jurisdictional basis for her to pursue her unfair dismissal application.

James Fisher v Dolphin Line Marking Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 949 (20 March 2026)

Deputy President Beaumont dismissed the application on the Commission’s own initiative under s 587(1)(c) for having no reasonable prospects of success.
Key holding

The Commission may dismiss an unfair dismissal application on its own initiative under s 587(1)(c) where, after procedural fairness, the application has no reasonable prospects of success.

Subject to affording procedural fairness, s 587 of the Act allows the Commission to dismiss an application on the Commission's own initiative in the early stages of proceedings. Protracted proceedings can be avoided when there is no reasonable prospect of an outcome other than the dismissal of the application.

Narinder Handa v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 655 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 655Employment & workplace
Mr Handa lodged a s 394 unfair dismissal application after his Uber delivery driver account was deactivated by Portier Pacific Pty Ltd. The Commission found he was not an employee but an employee-like worker and had filed the wrong remedy—an unfair dismissal rather than an unfair deactivation application. Key holding

The FWC may dismiss an unfair dismissal application on its own initiative under s 587(1)(c) where the applicant was not an employee and the application therefore has no reasonable prospects of success.

For the reasons outlined above, I am satisfied that the application has no reasonable prospects of success as the Respondent was not the Applicant's employer. In the circumstances, I have decided to dismiss the application pursuant to s.587(1)(c) of the Act.
