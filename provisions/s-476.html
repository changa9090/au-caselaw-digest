ANP22 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

2 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1943Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationMigration
This was an application for judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming the refusal of Protection (subclass 866) visas to a married Jordanian couple. The applicants claimed protection based on alleged honour-based harm arising from their marriage. The Federal Circuit and Family Court dismissed the application, finding no jurisdictional error, and ordered costs against the applicants.
Key holding

On judicial review under s 476, the Court is confined to jurisdictional error and cannot review the merits of the Tribunal’s decision.

The Court is not permitted to conduct a merits review of the Tribunal decision when reviewing it: see Djokovic v Minister for Immigration, Citizenship, Migrant Services and Multicultural Affairs (2022) 289 FCR 21.

BCD25 v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

1 Oct 2026Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia[2026] FedCFamC2G 1932Migration: court challengeTribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
BCD25, a Malaysian citizen, sought judicial review of an Administrative Review Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Protection (Class XA) (Subclass 866) visa. The Tribunal accepted some past harm but found no ongoing real chance or real risk from alleged gang-linked threats. The Federal Circuit and Family Court dismissed the judicial review application, finding no jurisdictional error.
Key holding

Judicial review under s 476 is confined to jurisdictional error, and the Court cannot substitute its own assessment of the merits of the Tribunal’s protection visa decision.

As will be explained, for the applicant to succeed in this Court, he must establish that the Tribunal decision contains a jurisdictional error. This Court cannot undertake a review of the merits of the decision under review.

FJE20 v Minister for Home Affairs

31 Aug 2021FCCA[2021] FCCA 1900Migration: court challengeMigration
The applicant, who had been released from immigration detention, brought a claim in negligence/tort for unlawful detention against the Minister and the Commonwealth. The Federal Circuit Court held that it lacked jurisdiction to hear the claim, because its migration jurisdiction under s 476 was confined to judicial review remedies and there was no federal matter capable of supporting accrued jurisdiction over a tort claim. The application (and numerous related matters) was dismissed on the basis of lack of jurisdiction.

MZXOT v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

18 Jun 2008High Court of Australia[2008] HCA 28Migration: court challengeMigration
The plaintiff, a migration applicant, brought a case stated in the High Court challenging the validity of provisions (ss 476, 476A, 476B and 484) of the Migration Act that, on one reading, would make the High Court the only court able to hear certain challenges to 'primary decisions'.
