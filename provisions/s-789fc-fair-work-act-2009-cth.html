Applications by Ms Rose Banfield and Ms Katrina Stolz [2026] FWC 717 (2 April 2026)

2 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 717Employment & workplace
Two TARHF employees, Ms Banfield and Ms Stolz, applied under s.789FC of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for stop-bullying orders against Ms Best, the Body Corporate secretary. Both applications were dismissed: Ms Banfield accepted there was no future risk of bullying, and Ms Stolz did not establish that Ms Best’s confronting emails created the requisite risk to health and safety.
Key holding

To establish bullying under s.789FD(1), the Commission must be satisfied that the relevant behaviour created a risk to the worker’s health and safety.

However, I am not satisfied that Ms Stolz's health and safety has been put at risk by Ms Best's behaviour, which is a requirement of s.789FD(1)(b).

Mr Mark Raymond Charteris [2026] FWC 885 (18 March 2026)

18 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 885Employment & workplace
Mr Charteris applied for a Fair Work Commission order to stop bullying under s.789FC of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), naming Mr Warton and Ms Schofield. He sought Commissioner Riordan’s recusal on apprehended bias grounds because of the Commissioner’s disclosed past university and union-movement connection with Ms Schofield. The Commission dismissed the recusal application, finding no reasonable apprehension of bias.
Key holding

A Commission Member is not required to recuse for apprehended bias where a fair-minded lay observer would not reasonably apprehend that the Member might not bring an impartial mind to the matter.

I am satisfied and find, for the reasons identified above, that a fair-minded lay observer would not be of the view that I will not bring an impartial mind to this matter.

Carter [2026] FWC 666 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 666Employment & workplace
Mr Carter applied under s.789FC of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an order to stop bullying in connection with his employment at Canning Vale College, a Western Australian public school. The Commission dismissed the application because the College, as a WA public school, is not a constitutionally-covered business and Mr Carter could not be “bullied at work” under s.789FD(1).
Key holding

The FWC lacks jurisdiction under s.789FC to hear a stop-bullying application where the alleged workplace is a WA public school, because the school is not a constitutionally-covered business and the applicant cannot satisfy the “bullied at work” requirement in s.789FD(1).
