Shah v Minister for Immigration and Citizenship

21 Oct 2026Federal Court of Australia[2026] FCA 1560Migration: court challengeMigration
This was an appeal to the Federal Court from the dismissal of judicial review of a Tribunal decision affirming refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) visa. Refusing to accept an applicant’s bare assertion that a subjective mandatory criterion is satisfied does not, of itself, establish jurisdictional error.

A decision not to accept a bare assertion by an applicant that a mandatory subjective criterion has been satisfied, does not establish jurisdictional error.

September 2026 (2)

Zhou (Migration)

28 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2365Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Ms Zhou sought review of the refusal of a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) Subclass 500 visa. The delegate refused because it was not satisfied she was a genuine temporary entrant under cl 500.212. By the Tribunal’s decision time she had completed her course and returned home, but because she had no current enrolment, the Tribunal affirmed under cl 500.211.
Key holding

For a Subclass 500 visa, cl 500.211(a) requires current enrolment in a course of study at the time of the Tribunal’s decision, and absence of current enrolment requires affirmation even if the applicant previously studied and completed courses.

However, as she is now not enrolled and cl 500.211 is a time of decision criterion, the Tribunal must affirm the review.

Manpreet Kaur (Migration)

25 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2386Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewVisa integrityMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa for Ms Kaur, an Indian citizen who has lived in Australia as a student since 2018. Although the Tribunal accepted she was enrolled in a registered course, it was not satisfied she genuinely intended to stay temporarily because her latest lower-level course appeared designed to extend her residence rather than advance her studies. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Under cl 500.212, all of paras (a), (b) and (c) must be satisfied, so failure to establish cl 500.212(a) alone is sufficient to fail the genuine applicant criterion.

To satisfy cl 500.212, all of subcls (a), (b) and (c) must be met and an unfavourable finding in relation to either (a) or (b) forecloses the possibility of a favourable outcome.

August 2026 (1)

NAT (Migration)

26 Aug 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2391Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Subclass 500 Student visa because the delegate was not satisfied Mr Patwari was a genuine applicant under cl 500.212. It set aside the refusal and remitted the application, finding his strong recent master’s results, family ties in India and the course’s value outweighed earlier concerns about course changes and slow completion.
Key holding

Under cl 500.212(a), the genuine temporary entrant assessment requires a holistic weighing of Direction No 108 factors rather than checklist compliance, allowing later sustained academic progress and home-country ties to outweigh earlier study concerns.

The Direction indicates that the factors specified should not be used as a checklist but rather, are intended only to guide decision makers when considering the applicant's circumstances as a whole, in reaching a finding about whether the applicant satisfies the genuine temporary entrant criterion.

June 2026 (1)

Sukhdeep Kaur (Migration)

30 Jun 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2352Tribunal reviewVisa nominationVisa refusal reviewMigration
The Administrative Review Tribunal reviewed a refusal of a Subclass 500 (Student) visa based on the delegate’s finding that Ms Kaur was not a genuine temporary entrant. The Tribunal accepted that her nursing study pathway, course changes, family ties and Indian job offer supported a genuine intention to stay temporarily and remitted the visa application for reconsideration. The decision turns on the application of cl 500.212 and Direction No 108.
Key holding

Changes to course enrolment do not necessarily show that a student visa is being used to maintain residence where the changes are credibly explained and consistent with a genuine study and career intention.

The Tribunal is satisfied that the applicant's intention to study nursing has been consistent, and the changes to course enrolments largely demonstrate the options she undertook in order to achieve her ambition, and were not undertaken for the primary purpose of extending or maintaining residence in Australia.

Gurung (Migration)

19 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3487Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Frank Russo
The Tribunal considered a review application by Mr Ugen Gurung, a secondary applicant for a Subclass 500 Student visa refused because he did not meet cl 500.311. The Tribunal found he was outside the migration zone when the review application was made, so it lacked jurisdiction to review his refusal. His wife’s separate review application had earlier been remitted with a direction that she met cl 500.212.
Key holding

For a decision described in s 338(2) of the Migration Act, the Tribunal has jurisdiction only if the person applying for review was physically present in the migration zone when the review application was made.

In the case of a decision described in s.338(2), an application for review may only be made by a non-citizen who is the subject of the decision and is physically present in the migration zone when the application for review is made: s.347(2)(a) and (3).

Sharma (Migration)

4 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3406Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Penelope Hunter
This is a review of a refusal to grant a Student (Temporary) (Class TU) Subclass 500 visa. The delegate refused because the applicant, an Indian citizen, was not satisfied to be a genuine temporary entrant under cl 500.212. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Refusal is upheld where the Tribunal is not satisfied, after considering Direction No 108 factors as a whole, that the applicant genuinely intends to stay in Australia temporarily under cl 500.212(a).

Overall having considered the criteria in Direction 108 and the relevant circumstances of the applicant, the Tribunal it is not satisfied that the applicant intends to stay in Australia temporarily. Accordingly, the applicant does not meet cl 500.212(a).
