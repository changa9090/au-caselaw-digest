March 2026 (1)

Olga Illich [2026] FWC 776 (9 March 2026)

9 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 776Employment & workplace
Mrs Illich applied under s.789FC for stop-bullying orders against Mr Geoffrey Illich. After her employment was terminated, the Commission considered dismissing the application because no stop-bullying order could have practical effect. She did not oppose dismissal, and the application was dismissed under s.587(1)(c).
Key holding

Where an applicant’s employment has ended and there is no present or reasonably foreseeable risk of continuing bullying at work, the FWC cannot make a stop-bullying order and may dismiss the application under s.587(1)(c) for no reasonable prospects of success.

I am satisfied that, as the Applicant's employment was terminated effective from 26 February 2026, there is no risk at present or in the reasonably foreseeable future that the Applicant will be bullied at work by the Person Named. Accordingly, I cannot make an order to stop bullying.

February 2026 (5)

Carter [2026] FWC 666 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 666Employment & workplace
Mr Carter applied under s.789FC of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an order to stop bullying in connection with his employment at Canning Vale College, a Western Australian public school. The Commission dismissed the application because the College, as a WA public school, is not a constitutionally-covered business and Mr Carter could not be “bullied at work” under s.789FD(1).
Key holding

The FWC lacks jurisdiction under s.789FC to hear a stop-bullying application where the alleged workplace is a WA public school, because the school is not a constitutionally-covered business and the applicant cannot satisfy the “bullied at work” requirement in s.789FD(1).

the College is not a constitutionally-covered business. Mr Carter could not have been 'bullied at work' within the meaning of that phrase in s.789FD(1) of the Act.
