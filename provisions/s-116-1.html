Qiu (Migration)

11 Aug 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2272Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration
Haifeng Qiu sought review of a delegate’s cancellation of his Subclass 050 (Bridging (General)) visa under s 116(1)(b) for allegedly working contrary to condition 8101. Although he delegated day-to-day massage business operations to Mr Wang, the Tribunal found he still performed work as sole director/secretary/shareholder by funding, monitoring, instructing accountants, signing financials and deciding to cease trading. The Tribunal affirmed cancellation.
Key holding

Condition 8101 is not confined to operational or customer-facing work; it requires identifying the activity the visa holder personally performed and asking whether that activity, in Australia and in the same situation, normally attracts remuneration.

Condition 8101 is not confined to operational or customer-facing activity. It asks what activity the applicant himself performed and whether that activity normally attracts remuneration.

May 2026 (1)

Mdallal (Migration)

18 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2382Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa nominationMigration
This was a review of a delegate’s decision to cancel Mr Mdallal’s Subclass 820 (Spouse) visa after he was convicted of contravening an AVO and common assault. Although the Tribunal found the conviction ground for cancellation was made out, it exercised the discretion not to cancel because of his remarriage, his support for two minor children (including an autistic stepdaughter), and the hardship removal would cause. The Tribunal set aside the cancellation and substituted a decision not to cancel.
Key holding

Even where a conviction ground for cancellation under s 116(1)(g) is established, the Tribunal may decline to cancel a temporary spouse visa where the best interests of affected children and associated family hardship outweigh the seriousness of the offending in the particular circumstances.

In the particular circumstances of this case, the Tribunal has decided to give greatest weight to the best interests of the children which weigh against the cancellation. Considering the circumstances as a whole, the Tribunal concludes that the cancellation should be set aside.

Khan (Migration)

9 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4018Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationMigration
Before: Senior Member D Connolly
The Tribunal reviewed a delegate’s cancellation of Mr Khan’s Class TU (Subclass 500) student visa. The delegate cancelled because PRISMS showed he was not enrolled in a full-time registered course from 11 November 2022 to 29 September 2023. The Tribunal found the breach established and affirmed cancellation after weighing discretionary factors.
Key holding

A breach of condition 8202(2)(a) may ground cancellation under s 116(1)(b), and once breach is found cancellation is discretionary with no mandatory statutory considerations.

Having found that the applicant has not complied with a condition of the visa, the Tribunal must consider whether the visa should be cancelled. There are no matters specified in the Act or Regulations that must be considered in the exercise of this discretion.

2410235 (Migration)

7 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4272Tribunal reviewVisa cancellationVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Michael Ison
The Tribunal affirmed cancellation of an Albanian national’s Subclass 010 (Bridging A) visa under s 116(1)(g) and reg 2.43(1)(oa) because he had convictions for cannabis cultivation in 2018 and 2021. Although cancellation would cause family and psychological hardship, the Tribunal gave determinative weight to adverse credibility findings about his explanations for the offending and the absence of extenuating circumstances. He continued to hold a Bridging E visa permitting work, so cancellation did not require departure.
Key holding

Where a prescribed cancellation ground is established but cancellation is not mandatory, the Tribunal must still determine whether the visa should be cancelled in the exercise of its discretion.

For these reasons, the Tribunal is satisfied that the ground for cancellation in s 116(1)(g) and reg 2.43(1)(oa) exists. As that ground does not require mandatory cancellation under s 116(3), the Tribunal must proceed to consider whether the visa should be cancelled.
