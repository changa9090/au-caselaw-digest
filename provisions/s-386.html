Ms Karen McClear v A Team Tuition Australia Pty Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4174Employment & workplace
Ms McClear resigned after a lengthy suspension pending an unresolved misconduct investigation and brought a s 365 general protections claim alleging she was forced to resign. A Team Tuition objected that she was not dismissed within s 386. The Commission dismissed the application, finding she was not forced to resign because of the employer’s conduct and was therefore not dismissed.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s 386(1)(b) only where an objective assessment of the employer’s conduct shows it made resignation the probable result or left the employee with no effective or real choice but to resign.

In making that assessment, it is appropriate to make an objective analysis of the Respondent's conduct to determine whether it was of such a nature that resignation was the probable result or that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

Under s.386, a labour-hire employee is not dismissed where the employer has not terminated employment at its initiative and has not engaged in conduct forcing the employee to resign.

Mr Robin Charles Houstein v Binc Premix Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 652 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 652Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission found that Mr Houstein, a truck driver, was dismissed rather than having genuinely resigned after a heated exchange with a leading hand on 17 June 2025. It also granted an extension of time for his unfair dismissal application, which was lodged three days late after postal and Commission registry delays. The matter will now proceed to a merits hearing.
Key holding

An ostensible resignation made in the heat of the moment and not genuinely intended, where the employer ought to appreciate that, is a dismissal at the employer’s initiative under s 386.

In all the circumstances, I consider that Mr Houstein's resignation was made in the heat of the moment, was not genuine, and that the employer ought to have appreciated this. The termination of the employment must therefore be taken to be at the employer's initiative, and is a dismissal for the purposes of section 386 of the Act.
