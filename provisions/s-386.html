Ms Alex Culhane v Psychsense Therapeutic Services Pty Ltd & Chantelle Chapman

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3893Employment & workplace
Ms Culhane applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), alleging dismissal by Psychsense Therapeutic Service Pty Ltd and Ms Chapman. The Commission had to determine a jurisdictional objection that she had not been dismissed because she resigned. Deputy President Clancy found there was no dismissal and dismissed the application.
Key holding

For a s.365 application at the material time, the Commission lacks jurisdiction unless the applicant was actually dismissed under s.386(1), meaning termination on the employer’s initiative or a resignation forced by employer conduct.

I have concluded that in this case there was no dismissal within the meaning of s.386 of the Act because I am satisfied there was no termination of the Applicant's employment on the Respondent's initiative and nor have I been persuaded that the Applicant was forced to resign because of conduct or a course of conduct engaged in by the Respondent .

Nicole Silipa v AllStaff Australia QLD Pty. Ltd. [2026] FWC 1263 (13 April 2026)

13 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1263Employment & workplace
Ms Silipa made a s.365 general protections dismissal dispute application against AllStaff Australia QLD Pty. Ltd. The Fair Work Commission found she was still a casual employee at the hearing date and had not been dismissed within s.386 of the Fair Work Act 2009. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

A s.365 general protections dismissal application requires that the applicant has been dismissed within the meaning of s.386; where the applicant remains a casual employee and was not dismissed, the application must be dismissed.

For the reasons given on transcript during the hearing conducted on 9 April 2026, I find that Nicole Silipa was still a casual employee of AllStaff Australia QLD Pty. Ltd. ( AllStaff ) as at the date of the hearing and had not been dismissed by AllStaff within the meaning of s.386 of the Fair Work Act 2009 .

Ms Duyen Ho v Persol Staffing Australia Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1121 (1 April 2026)

1 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1121Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dismissed an unfair dismissal application by a labour-hire chemist after her host client, PPG, barred her from its site. The Commission found she was not dismissed by her employer, Persol Staffing Australia Pty Ltd, because her employment was neither terminated at the employer's initiative nor forced by its conduct. The s.394 application therefore failed at the jurisdictional threshold.
Key holding

Under s.386, a labour-hire employee is not dismissed where the employer has not terminated employment at its initiative and has not engaged in conduct forcing the employee to resign.

More specifically, if the Applicant's employment was not terminated at the initiative of the Respondent, or if my finding is that she was not forced to resign because of conduct or a course of conduct engaged in by the Respondent, there is no jurisdictional basis for her to pursue her unfair dismissal application.

Adam Tate v Department Of Education, David Howes [2026] FWC 823 (13 March 2026)

13 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 823Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dealt with a s 365 general protections application by a teacher whose employment ended after he was issued a Working with Children Exclusion. The Commission upheld the Department’s jurisdictional objection, finding the Applicant was not dismissed within the meaning of s 386 of the Fair Work Act 2009 because his employment ceased by operation of law. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

Where an employee’s employment ends by operation of law or legislative mandate rather than by a relevant act of the employer, there is no dismissal within the meaning of s 386(1) of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth).

The Applicant's employment was brought to an end by legislative mandate and not by any action taken by the Respondents. The Applicant cannot therefore be said to have been "dismissed" within the meaning of section 386 of the FW Act.

Daniel De Silva v 75 688 174 585 Pty Ltd, Mr Glen McGarry [2026] FWC 765 (6 March 2026)

6 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 765Employment & workplace
Mr De Silva applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), alleging his dismissal by the first respondent contravened the general protections provisions. The Commission upheld the respondents' jurisdictional objection, finding Mr De Silva was not an employee and therefore was not dismissed within the meaning of s.386. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

An application under s.365 cannot proceed unless the applicant was dismissed within s.386, which requires an employment relationship; employee status is determined by the real substance, practical reality and true nature of the relationship.

In the absence of a relationship of employer and employee, a person cannot be dismissed within the meaning of s.386 and an application under s.365 cannot proceed.

Joanna Yi v Epec Group Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 749 (5 March 2026)

5 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 749Employment & workplace
Joanna Yi brought a s.365 general protections application alleging dismissal by EPEC Group Pty Ltd. EPEC raised a jurisdictional objection that Yi was not dismissed, arguing she voluntarily resigned. The Fair Work Commission rejected that objection, found Yi was dismissed under s.386, and ordered the matter to proceed to conference.
Key holding

Where an employer is on notice that an employee is resigning because of a mistaken belief about their role or need to relocate, accepting the resignation without clarifying the actual position may constitute termination on the employer’s initiative under s.386(1)(a).

I consider EPEC's acceptance of Ms Yi's resignation without further clarifying what it considered to be her actual position, and the need for her to relocate to Brisbane, to be a termination at the initiative of EPEC.

Shaine Mutton v Borderline Plumbing Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 730 (5 March 2026)

5 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 730Employment & workplace
Mr Mutton applied under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair dismissal remedy against Borderline Plumbing Pty Ltd. Borderline Plumbing objected that Mr Mutton was not its employee and therefore could not have been dismissed under s 386. The Commission found Mr Mutton worked through his own company, East Coast Safety Consultants Pty Ltd, under a contract for services and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A claim that a person is an employee or contractor of another can only be assessed where there is an established contractual relationship between them; absent a direct contract, there can be no dismissal by the respondent.

Consideration of whether a person is an employee of, or contractor to, another person or entity arises in the context of an established contractual relationship between them. Here, there was no such relationship between Mr Mutton and Borderline Plumbing for the reasons stated above.

Todd Green v Commonwealth of Australia as represented by Department of Veterans' Affairs [2026] FWC 728 (5 March 2026)

5 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 728Employment & workplace
Dr Todd Green applied under s.394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair dismissal remedy after resigning from the Department of Veterans’ Affairs. The Respondent objected that he was not dismissed within the meaning of s.386. The Fair Work Commission found the resignation was voluntary and dismissed the application.
Key holding

For a forced-resignation claim under s.386(1)(b), the applicant bears the onus of proving that the employer’s conduct left no real or effective choice but to resign.

As noted earlier, the onus is on the Applicant to prove that he had no real or effective choice in the circumstances but to resign because of the Respondent's conduct.

Amy Doran v The Jewellery Group Pty Limited [2026] FWC 673 (5 March 2026)

5 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 673Employment & workplace
Amy Doran alleged she was forced to resign from The Jewellery Group Pty Limited and applied to the Fair Work Commission under s 365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The Respondent objected on jurisdiction, arguing Ms Doran was not dismissed because she resigned voluntarily. The Commission dismissed the application, finding Ms Doran had not been “dismissed” for the purposes of s 386.
Key holding

Under s 386(1)(b), a resignation is a dismissal only if the employer’s conduct left the employee no real choice but to resign, and the line between forced and voluntary resignation is narrow.

The line distinguishing conduct that leaves an employee no real choice but to resign from an employee resigning at their own initiative is a narrow one.

Dr A Applicant v The Respondent [2026] FWC 724 (4 March 2026)

4 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 724Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission considered a general protections application under s 365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) where the Respondent objected that the Applicant had not been dismissed. The Applicant alleged her fixed-term contract was really terminated at the employer’s initiative through a misconduct process, suspension and alleged pretextual reliance on the end date. The Commission found the contract expired by effluxion of time and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A fixed-term employee is not dismissed within s 386 if the employment terminates on the specified end date, and informal or incomplete variation steps do not displace the written contract unless the contract is validly varied or otherwise vitiated.

The Applicant's employment ended in accordance with the fixed term specified in the Contract. There was no dismissal of the Applicant at the initiative of the employer.

Mr Robin Charles Houstein v Binc Premix Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 652 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 652Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission found that Mr Houstein, a truck driver, was dismissed rather than having genuinely resigned after a heated exchange with a leading hand on 17 June 2025. It also granted an extension of time for his unfair dismissal application, which was lodged three days late after postal and Commission registry delays. The matter will now proceed to a merits hearing.
Key holding

An ostensible resignation made in the heat of the moment and not genuinely intended, where the employer ought to appreciate that, is a dismissal at the employer’s initiative under s 386.

In all the circumstances, I consider that Mr Houstein's resignation was made in the heat of the moment, was not genuine, and that the employer ought to have appreciated this. The termination of the employment must therefore be taken to be at the employer's initiative, and is a dismissal for the purposes of section 386 of the Act.

Kibreab Teklu v Liferaft System Australia [2026] FWC 668 (27 February 2026)

27 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 668Employment & workplace
Mr Teklu claimed he was unfairly dismissed after leaving Liferaft System Australia on 8 July 2025 and not returning to work. LSA disputed the dismissal, variously saying he was dismissed for misconduct, resigned, or was not dismissed. The Fair Work Commission found he was not dismissed at LSA’s initiative, upheld the jurisdictional objection, and dismissed the application.
Key holding

Before an unfair dismissal claim can proceed, the employee must establish a dismissal under s.386(1); where the evidence shows voluntary departure rather than employer-initiated termination or forced resignation, there is no dismissal.

I consider the evidence does not support a finding that Mr Teklu was dismissed on the initiative of the employer within the meaning of s.386 of the Act.

Alana Woolston v Department Of Education Qld [2026] FWC 611 (25 February 2026)

25 Feb 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 611Employment & workplace
Ms Woolston brought a general protections application under s 365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), alleging dismissal by Hudson Global Resources and knowing involvement by the State of Queensland. The Commission had to decide whether she had been dismissed within s 386 before exercising s 368 powers. It held that the end of her labour-hire assignment with the State did not end her casual employment with Hudson, so the application was dismissed.
Key holding

The termination by a labour-hire employer of a single client assignment does not, without more, terminate the employee’s employment with the labour-hire employer for the purposes of s 386.

In circumstances where a labour hire company, such as Hudson, terminates one assignment on which an employee is working, the termination of the assignment does not, without more, terminate the employee's employment with the labour hire company.
