Ms Erica Bosloper v Linkpoint Lawyers Pty Ltd & Mr Jun Pan

29 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4208Employment & workplace
In [2026] FWC 4208, the Fair Work Commission considered a jurisdictional objection by Linkpoint Lawyers Pty Ltd and Jun Pan to Erica Bosloper’s s 365 application, contending she was not an employee and therefore could not have been dismissed. The Commission examined the written “Partnership Agreement” and how it was performed in practice, and found she was an employee dismissed on the employer’s initiative. The objection was dismissed.
Key holding

Under s 15AA, whether a person is an employee is determined by the real substance, practical reality and true nature of the relationship, considering the totality including actual performance, and is not controlled by the parties’ expressed intention or contractual label.

Ms Karen McClear v A Team Tuition Australia Pty Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4174Employment & workplace
Ms McClear resigned after a lengthy suspension pending an unresolved misconduct investigation and brought a s 365 general protections claim alleging she was forced to resign. A Team Tuition objected that she was not dismissed within s 386. The Commission dismissed the application, finding she was not forced to resign because of the employer’s conduct and was therefore not dismissed.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s 386(1)(b) only where an objective assessment of the employer’s conduct shows it made resignation the probable result or left the employee with no effective or real choice but to resign.

In making that assessment, it is appropriate to make an objective analysis of the Respondent's conduct to determine whether it was of such a nature that resignation was the probable result or that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

Under the Fair Work Act, if an unfair dismissal application has been made, it must have been withdrawn before a subsequent s 365 general protections application is made; simultaneous withdrawal in the later application does not disengage s 725.

The Fair Work Commission considered a jurisdictional objection to a s.365 general protections application brought by a former HR manager who alleged she was constructively dismissed. The Commission upheld the objection, finding the Applicant had not been dismissed within s.386 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The decision turned on whether the Employer’s conduct forced her resignation.
Key holding

For a s.386(1)(b) forced-resignation dismissal, the employer’s conduct must be intended to bring employment to an end, or be of such a nature that termination was the probable result and the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.

A resignation that is "forced" by conduct or a course of conduct on the part of the employer will be a dismissal within the second limb of the definition in s.386(1)(b). The test to be applied here is whether the employer engaged in the conduct with the intention of bringing the employment to an end or whether termination of the employment was the probably result of the employer's conduct such that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign.
