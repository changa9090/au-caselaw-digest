Ms Mary Muzorewa v Eldercare Pty Ltd

13 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3623Employment & workplace
Ms Muzorewa lodged a s.365 general protections dismissal application against Eldercare 297 days after the 21-day limit. The Commission considered whether to extend time under s.366(2) but found no exceptional circumstances, largely because her reliance on ANMF/administrative processes did not reasonably explain the delay. The application was dismissed; the s.725 statute-bar and settlement-release objections were noted but not finally determined.
Key holding

The Commission may extend time for a s.365 general protections dismissal application under s.366(2) only if satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist, taking into account the statutory factors.

Section 366(2) of the Act provides the Commission with discretion to extend the time for lodgement beyond the 21 -day period where it is satisfied that exceptional circumstances exist to warrant that action. In considering whether exceptional circumstances exist for this purpose, I am required to take into account the considerations outlined in paragraphs (a) to (e) of s.366(2) of the Act.

Mr Mark Shorrock v Cinc Switchboards Pty Ltd

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3911Employment & workplace
Mark Shorrock made a s.365 general protections application against Cinc Switchboards Pty Ltd, alleging constructive dismissal. The Commission determined a jurisdictional objection that he had resigned voluntarily and was not dismissed. It upheld the objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

A resignation is a dismissal under s.386(1)(b) only where the employer’s conduct or course of conduct was intended to bring about the resignation or had the probable result that the employee had no effective or real choice but to resign, with employer conduct being essential.

To satisfy the test for constructive dismissal requires a substantive and demonstratable course of conduct aimed at bringing about the end of the employment. This action is evident in the actions of the Employer, and the intended result of the conduct to bring about the Applicant's resignation; or that that would be the probable result.

Ms Alex Culhane v Psychsense Therapeutic Services Pty Ltd & Chantelle Chapman

12 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3893Employment & workplace
Ms Culhane applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), alleging dismissal by Psychsense Therapeutic Service Pty Ltd and Ms Chapman. The Commission had to determine a jurisdictional objection that she had not been dismissed because she resigned. Deputy President Clancy found there was no dismissal and dismissed the application.
Key holding

For a s.365 application at the material time, the Commission lacks jurisdiction unless the applicant was actually dismissed under s.386(1), meaning termination on the employer’s initiative or a resignation forced by employer conduct.

I have concluded that in this case there was no dismissal within the meaning of s.386 of the Act because I am satisfied there was no termination of the Applicant's employment on the Respondent's initiative and nor have I been persuaded that the Applicant was forced to resign because of conduct or a course of conduct engaged in by the Respondent .

Ms Kathleen Emery v Sharon Binding

8 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3866Employment & workplace
Ms Emery lodged a s.365 general protections dismissal application against Ms Binding, who objected that Ms Emery was not an employee and therefore could not have been dismissed. The Fair Work Commission decided the matter on the papers and found Ms Emery was not an employee, so it dismissed the application for want of jurisdiction.
Key holding

Under s 15AA(2), employee status is determined by the totality of the relationship, including the contract terms and how the contract is performed in practice.

Section 15AA(2) requires a consideration of the totality of the relationship which involves in turn a consideration of, amongst other things, the terms of the contract between the parties and an assessment as to how the contract is performed in practice.
