Kulwant KAUR (Migration)

23 Sep 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2351Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This was a review of a refusal of a Visitor (Class FA) Subclass 600 visa on genuine temporary entrant grounds under cl 600.211. The applicant was a 59-year-old widow from India whose children all live in Australia, and the family sought to keep her onshore so she could later apply for a permanent visa. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal because it was not satisfied she genuinely intended only a temporary stay.
Key holding

The Tribunal’s task under cl 600.211 is to assess whether the applicant genuinely intends a temporary stay for the visa’s purpose, not to facilitate permanent residence.

The Tribunal does not see it as its role to facilitate the applicant's intention to remain in Australia permanently regardless of the requirements of the Visitor visa. The Tribunal's role is to simply assess whether the applicant genuinely intends to stay in Australia temporarily for the purpose for which the visa is granted.

October 2024 (1)

KHALED (Migration)

1 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3643Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Anne Grant
Mr Ahmad Khaled sought a Visitor (Class FA) Subclass 600 visa in the Sponsored Family stream to visit his uncle and family in Australia. The delegate refused the visa because it was not satisfied he was a genuine temporary entrant, and the Tribunal affirmed that refusal. The Tribunal found Lebanon’s economic and security crisis created a strong incentive to remain beyond a permitted stay, outweighing his employment, property and upcoming marriage.
Key holding

Under cl 600.211, the Tribunal must be satisfied that an applicant genuinely intends to stay temporarily for the visa purpose; if not so satisfied, the visa criteria are not met.

I am not satisfied that the visa applicant genuinely intends to stay temporarily in Australia for the purpose for which the visa is granted, and find that the requirements of cl 600.211 are not met.

September 2024 (2)

Cai (Migration)

23 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3835Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Paul Windsor
Mr Cai applied for a Visitor (Class FA) subclass 600 visa in the Tourist stream. The delegate refused the visa because it was not satisfied he genuinely intended to stay temporarily in Australia under cl 600.211. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding his long continuous stay and unsupported medical claims indicated he was using the visitor visa to establish de facto residence.
Key holding

To satisfy cl 600.211, the applicant must positively satisfy the Tribunal of a genuine intention to stay temporarily for the visa purpose; a long continuous stay without a credible departure timeframe or supporting medical evidence can show the visitor visa is being used for de facto residence.

Given the applicant has been in Australia continuously since January 2020 and could not give any time-frame within which he might feel well enough to depart Australia, the Tribunal considers the applicant is seeking to use the subclass 600 Visitor visa to establish de facto residence in Australia. Consequently, the Tribunal is not satisfied that the visa applicant genuinely intends to stay temporarily in Australia for the purpose for which the visa would be granted.

2308191 (Migration)

4 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 3820Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Member Nathan Goetz
The Tribunal reviewed a refusal of Visitor (class FA) Sponsored Family (subclass 600) visas for the review applicant’s parents. The delegate refused because the applicants did not satisfy the genuine temporary stay criterion in cl 600.211. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal, finding the applicants’ oral evidence inconsistent with the review applicant’s protection visa claims and that their claimed intention to stay temporarily should be given no weight.
Key holding

Under cl 600.211, the Tribunal may give no weight to an applicant’s claimed genuine intention to stay temporarily where their oral evidence is inconsistent with the review applicant’s protection claims and shows a flexible approach to the truth.

The fact that the visa applicants provided oral evidence inconsistent with the protection claims advanced by the review applicant satisfies the Tribunal that they have a flexible approach to the truth, and that the Tribunal should not give any weight to their claimed intention to travel to Australia temporarily for the purpose for which the visa is issued.
