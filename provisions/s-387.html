Roberto Biguzzi v Eco Alliance Pty. Ltd.

19 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3872Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dealt with an unfair dismissal application by a former Senior Sales Representative whose role was made redundant after the employer closed its internal sales function. The employer asserted genuine redundancy under s 389, but the Commission found the job was no longer required due operational requirements while the written consultation did not satisfy cl 27.2 of the Miscellaneous Award 2020. The provided extract does not include final orders on the remaining s 387 merits.
Key holding

For a genuine redundancy defence under s 389(1)(b), written consultation must explain the nature of the major change itself, not merely its effects or causes.

It would in my view be insufficient to meet the written notice requirements in clause 27.2 to deal only with the effects of the changes without explaining what the changes actually are. This is consistent with "nature" and "effect" being separately itemised in paragraphs (a) and (b) of clause 27.2.

Mr Hossein Nasiri v Hendrickson Asia Pacific Pty Ltd

Mr Nasiri applied under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) alleging unfair dismissal after Hendrickson Asia Pacific Pty Ltd terminated his employment on 29 May 2026 for alleged serious misconduct. The Commission found the Applicant lacked credibility, preferred the Respondent’s witnesses, and held there was a valid conduct-based reason with no s 387 factor favouring unfairness. Application dismissed: Nasiri v Hendrickson Asia Pacific Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 3929.
Key holding

A dismissal is not harsh, unjust or unreasonable under s 387 where the Commission finds a valid conduct-based reason and none of the statutory matters weigh in favour of unfairness.

None of the factors set out in s 387 of the Act weigh in favour of the conclusion that the termination of The Applicant's employment was unfair.

Vlasios Rotziokos v Futuresleep Pty/ Ltd

16 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4040Employment & workplace
In Vlasios Rotziokos v Futuresleep Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 4040, the Fair Work Commission considered an unfair dismissal application by a casual production worker dismissed after removing his safety vest and singlet on the factory floor during a meal break. The Commission found there was a valid reason for dismissal and a fair process, and that inconsistent enforcement of a related PPE colour requirement did not outweigh those matters. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

A dismissal for a clear safety-policy breach is not unfair where there is a valid reason, procedural fairness and prior specific warnings, even if the employer inconsistently enforced a related aspect of the policy, provided that inconsistency is outweighed by the other s 387 matters.

It follows from the above that having considered each of the matters specified in s.387 of the Act, I am satisfied that the dismissal of the Applicant was not harsh, unjust, or unreasonable. That is because there was a valid reason for the dismissal , the process followed by the Respondent was procedurally fair, the Applicant had been previously warned in relation to the specific conduct, all of which outweighs the inconsistent application of the Procedure I have identified.

Mashood Qureshi v The Trustee For Aabs Melbourne Unit Trust Trading AS Archery Attack

8 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3856Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission considered Mr Qureshi’s unfair dismissal application against Archery Attack, which objected that his dismissal was a genuine redundancy under s 389 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth). The Commission found the dismissal was not a genuine redundancy because the employer failed to comply with Award consultation obligations, but ultimately dismissed the application because the dismissal was not harsh, unjust or unreasonable under s 387.
Key holding

A redundancy dismissal is not necessarily unfair merely because the employer failed to comply with award consultation obligations, if the failure did not materially alter the outcome or timing of the redundancy.

While the Respondent failed to comply with the award consultation obligations, I do not regard the deficiencies in its approach to have materially altered the outcome or timing of the Applicant's redundancy.

Emma Turner v Endeavour Group Limited [2026] FWC 1318 (16 April 2026)

16 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1318Employment & workplace
Emma Turner, a casual Dan Murphy’s employee, was dismissed after ongoing incapacity following an armed robbery at work. The Fair Work Commission held the dismissal was not unfair because there was a valid reason relating to capacity and procedural fairness was afforded. Her unfair dismissal application was dismissed.
Key holding

A dismissal for ongoing incapacity arising from a traumatic workplace incident is not unfair if the employer has a valid capacity-related reason and affords procedural fairness under s.387.

I have found there was a valid reason for the dismissal and that Endeavour afforded procedural fairness. I do not consider the fact that Ms Turner was dismissed due to incapacity arising from an armed robbery she endured at work to be sufficient to justify a finding that the dismissal was harsh, unjust, or unreasonable.

Ms Abbie Small v Sharp N Stylish Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 1084 (30 March 2026)

30 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1084Employment & workplace
Ms Small resigned with four weeks’ notice after raising unpaid superannuation and underpayment concerns; Sharp summarily dismissed her the next day. The Fair Work Commission found the dismissal unfair and ordered modest compensation, with reinstatement refused.
Key holding

A dismissal is harsh, unjust and unreasonable where the employer had no valid reason for summary dismissal and afforded no procedural fairness, even after the employee had given notice of resignation.

After considering and taking into account each of the matters specified in section 387 of the Act, my value judgment is that Sharp's dismissal of Ms Small was harsh, unjust and unreasonable. Ms Small was summarily dismissed after she had given four weeks' notice of her resignation.

Kee Onn Yong v UGC Holdings Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 976 (23 March 2026)

23 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 976Employment & workplace
Kee Onn Yong was summarily dismissed by UGC Holdings Pty Ltd for alleged serious misconduct, including allowing staff to use a blocked side door. The Fair Work Commission found there was no valid reason and no procedural fairness, and awarded compensation. UGC did not attend the hearing or otherwise engage with the Commission after December 2025.
Key holding

A dismissal will be unfair under s 387 where the Commission finds there was no valid reason for dismissal and the employee was denied procedural fairness.

I find that there was no valid reason for Mr Yong's dismissal. I further find that Mr Yong was denied procedural fairness in his dismissal.
