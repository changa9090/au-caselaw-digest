United Firefighters' Union of Australia (259V) v Triple Zero Victoria

28 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4199Employment & workplace
The UFU applied under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for the Commission to determine whether a TZV employee was entitled to an independent investigator for serious misconduct allegations. The Commission held that neither the Triple Zero Victoria Operations Enterprise Agreement 2024 nor the TZV Workplace Investigations Procedure created that entitlement. It nonetheless recommended that Mr Walker proceed as TZV’s appointed investigator.
Key holding

Clause 67.4 requires any summary dismissal to be carried out in accordance with TZV policies and procedures consistent with the FW Act and Regulations, but it does not incorporate those policies into the Agreement or create a pre-dismissal entitlement to an independent investigation.

Properly construed, in my view, what the clause commits the Respondent to do is to enact the "summary dismissal of any employee" in accordance with its policies and procedures which must be consistent with the provisions of the Act and FW Regulations.

Estefania Asensio Tato v Lycée Condorcet Trading AS The International French School Of Sydney Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4154Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission dealt with a s 739 dispute brought by a former secondary Spanish teacher against her former school over alleged underpayments/classification for February and March 2025 work. The Commission upheld the school’s jurisdictional objection because the dispute was not raised until after employment ended, dismissed the application, and noted any underpayment claim could be pursued in court. The deed of release was not determinative.
Key holding

Under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), the Commission lacks jurisdiction to determine an enterprise agreement dispute where the dispute is raised after the employment relationship has ended.

The entitlements conferred in the Agreement remain enforceable for a limited period on application to the relevant Court, but the Commission is unable to deal with the dispute under the dispute resolution clause where the dispute was raised after the employment relationship had ended.

"Automotive, Food, Metals, Engineering, Printing and Kindred Industries Union" known as the Australian Manufacturing Workers' Union (AMWU) (188V) v Applus Pty Ltd

27 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4190Employment & workplace
The AMWU applied under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for the Fair Work Commission to resolve a dispute over clause 17.2 of the Applus Pty Ltd Onshore NDT & Inspection Greenfields Agreement 2025. The issue was whether Easter Saturday and Easter Sunday, both falling on a weekend, entitled employees to public holidays on the next two working days or only the next working day. The Commission held each public holiday had to be taken on successive next working days.
Key holding

Under clause 17.2, where two gazetted public holidays fall on a weekend, each holiday must be taken on successive next working days so employees receive both entitlements.

Having considered the text, context and purpose, I find that the answer to the question is that employees were entitled to take the Easter Saturday public holiday on Tuesday, 7 April 2026, and the Easter Sunday public holiday on Wednesday, 8 April 2026.

"Automotive, Food, Metals, Engineering, Printing and Kindred Industries Union" known as the Australian Manufacturing Workers' Union (AMWU) (188V) v Schindler Lifts Australia Pty Ltd

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4166Employment & workplace
The AMWU referred a dispute under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) and clause 10 of the Schindler Lifts Australia Pty Ltd Victorian Enterprise Agreement 2024–2028, asking how the "make up pay for workers comp" entitlement in clause 42 is calculated. The Commission held that "average weekly earnings" in clause 42 is to be worked out by reference to the Victorian workers' compensation concept of pre-injury average weekly earnings under the WIRC Act, and left the arithmetic to the parties.
Key holding

Where an enterprise agreement requires a top-up to 100% of "average weekly earnings" but does not define the term, that expression is to be calculated by reference to the pre-injury average weekly earnings concept in the Victorian workers' compensation legislation, with either s.153(1) or s.153(2) of the WIRC Act available.

As such, I consider that Mr Savari's 'average weekly earnings' for the purposes of clause 42 of the 2024 Agreement in relation to his workers' compensation claim for injury for the injury on 20 March 2025 are able to be calculated by reference to either s.153(1) or s.153(2) of the WIRC Act.

The Commission found substantial procedural non-compliance with the employer’s misconduct policy and the Agreement’s fair, transparent and measured requirement. The extract provided does not include the final order disposing of the warning.
Key holding

Where an enterprise agreement obliges an employer to apply disciplinary procedures fairly, transparently and in a measured way, a substantial failure to put the specific misconduct allegation and afford a genuine opportunity to respond means misconduct is not confirmed under the policy and disciplinary action is inconsistent with the agreement.

The Civil Air Operations Officers’ Association Of Australia Trading AS Civil Air v Airservices Australia

20 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3820Employment & workplace
Civil Air applied under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for the Fair Work Commission to arbitrate a dispute about redundancy-type benefits under the Airservices Australia (Air Traffic Control and Supporting Air Traffic Services) Enterprise Agreement 2024-2027. The dispute concerned whether air traffic controllers who cannot obtain, or refuse to obtain, a National Security Clearance or Citizenship Eligibility Waiver are entitled to cl 62.13(a)(i)-(ii) benefits on termination.
Key holding

Clause 62.13 termination benefits require the employee to be a “potentially surplus employee” under cl 62.2, and inability or refusal to obtain an NSC or CEW does not make an ATC surplus where the class of ATCs remains required.

For these reasons I do not consider that employees who are unable or refuse to obtain an NSC (or CEW) can qualify for the entitlements in clause 62.13(a)(i) and (ii) because they are in a class of employees formally identified by the Respondent as a class which has a greater number of employees in it that is necessary for its efficient and economical working, within the meaning of clause 62.2(a) of the Agreement.

Peter Hugh Crossley v Protech GPS Pty Ltd

14 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3876Employment & workplace
The applicant, Mr Crossley, applied under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for the Fair Work Commission to deal with a dispute about redundancy severance and a pro-rata attendance bonus under the Global Product Search Enterprise Agreement 2018. Protech GPS Pty Ltd objected that the Commission lacked jurisdiction because the applicant was no longer employed and the dispute procedure had not been invoked before termination. The Commission upheld the objection and dismissed the application.
Key holding

The Commission may deal with a s.739 enterprise agreement dispute after employment ends only if it was seized of jurisdiction before termination; a dispute first invoked or filed after termination is outside jurisdiction.

Whilst I accept that the authorities in decisions such as Jajoo, Telstra , Deakin University, Kentz, Broadspectrum, and Goonyella make clear the Commission does not necessarily lose its capacity to deal with a dispute because an employment relationship comes to an end, the Commission must at some point before the termination of employment become seized of the jurisdiction in order to continue to deal with it after termination occurs.

Community Accommodation and Respite Agency Incorporated v United Workers Union & Australian Services Union (ASU)

13 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3878Employment & workplace
CARA referred a dispute under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 concerning cl 6.11 of the CARA Support Employees Enterprise Agreement 2022. The Commission determined whether employees who agree to supervise clients on overnight excursions, camps or holidays are limited to 10 hours’ pay at the applicable rate plus a sleepover allowance, or can also claim other minimum-rate, penalty, overtime and sleepover entitlements without limitation.
Key holding

A specific enterprise agreement clause governing agreed overnight excursion, camp or client holiday work overrides more general agreement provisions, so the cl 6.11 entitlement of 10 hours at the applicable rate plus a sleepover allowance limits other payments, while the “applicable rate” may itself incorporate penalty or overtime rates.

Applying the well established principle of more specific provisions overriding more broad provisions when they deal with the same subject matter, the entitlements in cl. 6.11 apply when an employee undertakes the work set out in that clause. Employees are not entitled to the payment in cl . 6.11 and a further payment arising from each of the broader agreement provisions.

Thomas Quayle v Alpine Pty Ltd Trading AS Alpine MDF Industries [2026] FWC 1139 (1 April 2026)

1 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1139Employment & workplace
Thomas Quayle applied to the Fair Work Commission under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to resolve a dispute with Alpine Pty Ltd trading as Alpine MDF Industries under the Alpine MDF Industries Pty Ltd Enterprise Agreement 2022. The dispute concerned when Mr Quayle was entitled to Level 5 and Level 6 rates and whether Alpine complied with clause 41 (Skills Criteria) when assessing his QA Laboratory and Refiner Operation competencies.
Key holding

Under clause 41, an employee becomes entitled to move to a higher pay level only once they have been assessed as competent under clause 41.2 and have accumulated sufficient points, not merely when they believe they satisfy clause 41.1.

It follows from the above that an employee is entitled to progress through the Pay Levels, only when they are assessed as competent pursuant to clause 41.2 and their accumulated points are sufficient to enable them to move to a higher Pay Level.

"Automotive, Food, Metals, Engineering, Printing and Kindred Industries Union" known as the Australian Manufacturing Workers' Union (AMWU) v EDI Rail PPP Maintenance Pty Ltd [2026] FWC 876 (17 March 2026)

17 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 876Employment & workplace
The AMWU and EDI Rail disputed how to calculate the percentage of night shifts under cl 30.5(g) of the Downer Rail Auburn Maintenance Agreement 2023. The Fair Work Commission arbitrated the dispute under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 and held the divisor was the total possible shifts Mr Peach could actually work in the 12-week roster cycle (38), not EDI Rail’s notional 60. This produced a 39.5% night-shift result, exceeding the 25% table limit and requiring an Annual Rate review.
Key holding

Clause 30.5(g) requires the divisor for calculating the percentage of night shifts to be the total possible shifts the employee can actually work in the relevant roster cycle, determined by the employee’s roster pattern rather than a notional figure.

The clause requires the total possible shifts an employee can work in the relevant roster cycle to be determined based on the shifts the employee works, not a notional figure. That figure is the divisor for the calculation.

Mining and Energy Union v Ensham Resources Pty Ltd T/A Ensham [2026] FWC 814 (12 March 2026)

12 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 814Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission arbitrated a dispute under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) about whether employees absent due to personal illness and receiving salary continuance insurance under cl 13.1 are also entitled to Medical Benefit Payments under cl 9.7 of the Ensham Mine Employees Enterprise Agreement 2021. The Union says yes; Ensham says no, relying on the express preservation of only Production Reward Scheme payments in cl 13.1 and historical practice.
Key holding

Enterprise agreement interpretation starts with the ordinary meaning of the relevant words and resolves disputed construction by reference to the language, context and purpose of the agreement.

The construction of an enterprise agreement, like that of a statute or contract, begins with a consideration of the ordinary meaning of the relevant words. The resolution of a disputed construction of an agreement will turn on the language of the agreement having regard to its context and purpose.

Mr Aditya Singhal v Reserve Bank of Australia [2026] FWC 754 (9 March 2026)

9 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 754Employment & workplace
Mr Singhal applied to the Fair Work Commission under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to resolve a dispute under the Reserve Bank of Australia Enterprise Agreement 2023 concerning non-payment of salary while he was medically restricted but certified fit for suitable duties. The Commission held that clause 10 salary is payable according to ordinary hours actually worked, so no entitlement arose where no work was performed. Participation in disciplinary processes was accepted as work, but quantum remains unresolved.
Key holding

Under the Reserve Bank of Australia Enterprise Agreement 2023, clause 10 salary is conditional on the employee actually performing work in the relevant pay period; no entitlement arises merely from continuing employment or readiness and willingness where no work is performed.

The entitlement to wages under clause 10 is in my view conditional upon the employee performing work in the relevant pay period. As no work was performed, the entitlement to the payment of wages does not arise under the Agreement.

National Tertiary Education Industry Union v The University Of Newcastle [2026] FWC 727 (4 March 2026)

4 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 727Employment & workplace
The NTEU applied under s 739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for the Fair Work Commission to arbitrate disputes with the University of Newcastle over alleged failures to consult and comply with WHS obligations regarding 2025 major workplace change. The University belatedly argued the Commission should decline to arbitrate because the disputes concerned past, implemented change and lacked utility. Deputy President Saunders held the Commission had a discretion to arbitrate and decided to exercise it.
Key holding

Under an enterprise agreement dispute resolution clause, the words “may arbitrate” confer a discretion on the Fair Work Commission to arbitrate an unresolved dispute after unsuccessful mediation or conciliation, rather than a mandatory obligation.

Accordingly, on the proper construction of clause 11.2 of the Agreements, the Commission has a discretion under the dispute resolution procedure to arbitrate a dispute which remains unresolved following an unsuccessful mediation and/or conciliation.

Xanthe Thomson v Public Service Association and Professional Officers' Association Amalgamated Union of New South Wales T/A Public Service Association of New South Wales [2026] FWC 632 (4 March 2026)

4 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 632Employment & workplace
Ms Xanthe Thomson, a PSA Industrial Officer, applied under s.739 of the Fair Work Act 2009 disputing the PSA’s misconduct process over alleged timesheet falsification. The Commission held the PSA breached cl 51 of the Public Service Association of NSW Staff Enterprise Agreement 2024 because the Assistant Secretary both investigated/formulated the allegations and assessed her response/recommended dismissal. The show cause process was invalid, but the Commission allowed the investigation to continue only via a third-party investigator.
Key holding

Where an enterprise agreement incorporates a misconduct policy requiring an independent investigation, procedural fairness is breached if the same officer investigates/formulates allegations and also assesses the employee’s response or recommends discipline, rendering the show cause process invalid.

In relation to clause 6 of the Policy as extracted at paragraph [23] of this decision, put simply, the Respondent has conflated two of the steps in the process by having the Assistant Secretary act as the Applicant's manager and also act as the independent investigator. As a result, the process lacks the required procedural fairness, so the application must succeed.
