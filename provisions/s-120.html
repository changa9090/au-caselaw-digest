Application by Queensland Police Citizens Youth Welfare Association

26 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4157Employment & workplace
The Fair Work Commission determined an employer’s application under s.120 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to reduce Ms Ashleigh Silver’s redundancy pay to nil after it offered her redeployment to another OSHC Educator role. Ms Silver declined the role because of care arrangements for her puppy and did not file any submissions opposing the application. The Commission found the alternative employment acceptable and reduced the redundancy entitlement to nil.
Key holding

Under s.120, the Commission may reduce redundancy pay to nil where the employer has obtained objectively acceptable alternative employment, notwithstanding the employee’s personal reasons for refusing it.

I am satisfied that that the redeployment role is acceptable alternative employment. The role has the same rate of pay, the same classification level, and the same hours.

Application by Sydney Night Patrol & Inquiry Co Pty Trading AS Certis Security Australia

15 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 3953Employment & workplace
Certis made Ms Culbert’s position redundant and offered her an alternative role, which she declined; her employment ended on 28 May 2026. Certis applied under s.120 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to reduce her seven weeks’ redundancy pay to nil on the basis it had obtained acceptable alternative employment. The Fair Work Commission granted the application and reduced the redundancy pay to nil.
Key holding

Where an employer obtains an objectively acceptable alternative position that would avoid or reduce redundancy hardship, the FWC may reduce the employee’s severance pay under s.120.

If the alternative position offered is objectively acceptable to avoid or reduce the hardship to the employee, s.120 allows for the severance payment to be reduced accordingly.

ASN Events Pty Ltd v Courtney Harris-Spencer & Michelle Harris-Spencer [2026] FWC 672 (30 March 2026)

30 Mar 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 672Employment & workplace
ASN Events Pty Ltd applied under s.120 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) to reduce the redundancy pay of two former finance employees by 50%, after closing its Hastings office and offering redeployment to the Melbourne CBD. The Fair Work Commission dismissed the application, finding the employer had not obtained “other acceptable employment” for either employee because of significant additional travel time, travel costs and, for one employee, childcare responsibilities.
Key holding

Under s.120, alternative employment is acceptable only if it is objectively suitable considering matters including location and travelling time; significant travel and cost disadvantages can make otherwise like-for-like employment unacceptable.

Importantly, 'acceptable' requires that the alternative employment meets a relevant objective test. The test is not whether the alternative employment is identical, but requires consideration of whether the work is of a like nature, the effect of location, pay, the hours of work, workload, job security, fringe benefits, compliance with statutory conditions, seniority and the like, and not whether the employee finds the alternative employment to be acceptable.
