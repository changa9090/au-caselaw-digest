Key holding

Exceptional circumstances under s.366(2) may be established by a combination of the employee not receiving the termination letter, reasonably inquiring about dismissal status, and the employer failing to clarify the dismissal.

The combination of Mr Mousley not receiving a termination letter from Coles, seeking to clarify his employment status with Mr Angelo on 15 and 17 May 2026 and not receiving a proper response from Mr Angelo are matters which in my view are out of the ordinary course, unusual, special or uncommon.

Jessie Perrin v Yarra City Council [2026] FWC 1133 (1 April 2026)

1 Apr 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 1133Employment & workplace
Jessie Perrin, a casual aqua instructor, applied under s.365 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) alleging Yarra City Council dismissed her by removing her from her regular Friday shift after she sought payment for set-up time. The Council objected that there was no dismissal and she remained a casual employee. The Commission found she had been dismissed at the Council’s initiative and dismissed the jurisdictional objection.
Key holding

Removing a casual employee from their regular continuing roster and replacing them can constitute termination at the employer’s initiative under s.386(1)(a), even without an express dismissal.

Objectively viewed, in circumstances where Ms Perrin had worked a regular Friday shift since early 2022, in being removed from these shifts and replaced on the roster with another member of staff, it is reasonably apparent the Respondent did not intend to offer her any future work and that her employment was being brought to an end.
