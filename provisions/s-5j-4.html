2111508 (Refugee)

14 May 2026Administrative Review Tribunal[2026] ARTA 2336Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
This is an ART review of a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa to a Filipino applicant relying on her mental and physical health conditions and claimed inability to access or afford treatment in the Philippines. The Tribunal accepted much of her medical history but found her conditions controlled with medication and that country information indicated available healthcare. It affirmed the refusal because it was not satisfied of the relevant persecution or significant harm risk.
Key holding

A claimed inability to access or afford mental health care does not establish refugee persecution absent the statutory requirement of systematic and discriminatory conduct, assessed against the evidence and country information.

I also raised with the applicant my concerns that she may not meet the criteria for a protection visa because there is no persecution involving systematic and discriminatory conduct as required by s 5J(4)(c) of the Act.

October 2024 (1)

1816066 (Refugee)

2 Oct 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4440Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Paul White
The applicant, a Vietnamese citizen, sought review of a delegate’s refusal to grant her a protection visa. She relied primarily on her husband’s Catholic/political claims, her own Catholic faith, low-level protest activity in Australia, and alleged registration/economic difficulties on return to Vietnam. The Tribunal affirmed the refusal.
Key holding

Generalised adjustment and economic circumstances do not amount to persecution or discrimination under s 5J(4) absent systematic and discriminatory conduct.

Adjustment to life in Vietnam and generalised economic circumstances do not meet the requirements of systematic and discriminatory conduct in the absence of other considerations and do not constitute persecution or discrimination within the meaning of s 5J(4).

September 2024 (1)

1727642 (Refugee)

20 Sep 2024Administrative Review Tribunal[2024] AATA 4409Tribunal reviewVisa refusal reviewMigration
Before: Mary-Ann Cooper
This is an AATA review of a delegate’s refusal of a protection visa to an Indonesian citizen who claimed fear of a moneylender and debt collectors over an unpaid business loan, plus general economic hardship in Indonesia. The Tribunal accepted his debt account as credible but found the feared harm was not for a Convention reason and did not meet complementary protection thresholds. It affirmed the visa refusal.
Key holding

Harm feared from private debt collectors is not persecution for a Convention reason where the essential and significant reason is coercion to repay an unpaid debt.

This claimed harm arises because the applicant owes money that he has not repaid and/or is unable to repay. This is the essential and significant reason for the harm that the applicant claims he will suffer, not for one of the reasons listed in s 5J(1)(a) of the Act (s 5J(4)(a)).
