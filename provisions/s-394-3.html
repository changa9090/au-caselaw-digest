Madana Harrop v Maryborough District Health Service

22 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4112Employment & workplace
Ms Harrop lodged an unfair dismissal application 16 days late after her union, the ANMF, failed to transmit a legal referral due to an automated email system error. The Fair Work Commission accepted representative error as a reasonable explanation, found exceptional circumstances and granted an extension of time. The merits of the unfair dismissal claim have not yet been determined.
Key holding

Where an applicant is blameless and reasonably relies on their representative, a delay caused by the representative’s administrative or system error can be an acceptable explanation for the whole delay and support a finding of exceptional circumstances under s 394(3).

In this case, the reason for the delay was the error of the ANMF, and it is in my view reasonable for the Applicant to have relied on the ANMF's advice that referral would have been made to her legal representatives to pursue an unfair dismissal application. The Applicant should not be disadvantaged because the ANMF experienced a system error in processing of the legal referral.

Mr Gabriel Roccisano v Chop Shop Post Pty Ltd

21 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4091Employment & workplace
Mr Roccisano applied under s 394 of the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) for an unfair dismissal remedy after being notified of his dismissal by Chop Shop Post Pty Ltd on 4 November 2025. His application, filed on 12 December 2025, was out of time on either possible calculation of the end date. Deputy President Butler refused to extend time, finding that the medical evidence did not establish incapacity to lodge and that distress, financial pressure and ignorance of the 21-day limit were not exceptional circumstances.
Key holding

For the purposes of s 394(3), the distress inherent in losing a job is not of itself an exceptional circumstance, and neither is an asserted psychological condition exceptional unless it is shown to have incapacitated the applicant from lodging within time.

As the cause of action is founded on something as inherently distressing as job loss, distress per se cannot be considered exceptional.

Mr Muhammad Ahsan Malik v On The Run Pty Ltd

19 Oct 2026Fair Work Commission[2026] FWC 4054Employment & workplace
Mr Malik filed an unfair dismissal application 23 days after the 21-day statutory period expired. The Commission refused an extension because he had no satisfactory explanation for the delay, knew of the dismissal immediately, and the remaining factors were neutral or only marginally supportive. The application was dismissed.
Key holding

In weighing the s 394(3) factors, a marginal favour from the applicant’s efforts to dispute dismissal is outweighed by the lack of a satisfactory reason for delay and immediate awareness of dismissal, so no exceptional circumstances are established.

Balancing the factor that weighs marginally in favour of a finding of exceptional circumstances, being the Applicant's efforts to dispute his dismissal, against the factors weighing more heavily against such a finding, being the lack of a satisfactory reason for delay and the Applicant's awareness of the dismissal on the day it was conveyed by the Respondent, I find that there are no exceptional circumstances in this matter.
